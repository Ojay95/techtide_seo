## Why your Google Business Profile is your most important local SEO asset

When someone searches for your business or a service you offer on Google, your Google Business Profile (GBP) is often the first thing they see, before your website, before your social media, before anything else. It powers:

- The information card that appears to the right of Google's search results (the Knowledge Panel)
- Your listing on Google Maps
- Your appearance in the local map pack, the three businesses Google highlights for local searches

A complete, optimised Google Business Profile can generate calls, direction requests and website clicks directly from the search results page, without the user even visiting your website. For many local businesses, GBP is the highest converting touchpoint in their entire digital presence.

This guide walks through every step of setting up, verifying and optimising your profile for maximum visibility and conversions.

## Step 1: Create or claim your Google Business Profile

![Google Business Profile setup screen showing a business name search with a Claim this business button](/images/guides/google-business-profile-optimization/step01_claim.png)

### Access the dashboard

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and sign in with the Google account you want to use to manage your business. Use a business Google account where possible, rather than a personal Gmail.

### Search for your business

Before creating a new listing, search for your business name. Google automatically creates listings for some businesses based on third party data. If a listing already exists, claim it rather than creating a duplicate.

### Claiming an existing listing

If you find your business, click "Claim this business" or "Own this business?" and follow the verification steps. If you cannot claim it because another account owns it, Google has a process for requesting ownership transfer.

### Creating a new listing

If no listing exists, create one. Enter your:
- **Business name:** Your exact trading name, precisely as it appears on your signage and official documents. Do not add keywords. Do not add your location. Use only your actual business name.
- **Business type:** Online only, local store, or service area business.
- **Primary business category:** The most important decision at this stage (see Step 2).
- **Location or service area:** Your physical address, or the areas you serve if you travel to customers.
- **Contact details:** Phone number and website.

## Step 2: Choose your categories with care

![Google Business Profile category editor with Plumber as the primary category and three additional categories](/images/guides/google-business-profile-optimization/step02_categories.png)

Your primary category is one of the most powerful ranking signals in local SEO. Google uses it to determine what searches to show your business for. If your primary category is wrong or too broad, you miss the searches that matter.

### How to choose the right primary category

Ask: what is the single most specific, accurate description of my main business? If you are a family dentist, "Dentist" is better than "Dental clinic." If you are a criminal lawyer, "Criminal justice attorney" is better than "Lawyer." Specificity beats breadth.

Search your main service terms on Google and look at what primary categories the top ranking map pack businesses use. This competitive research often reveals the best choices.

### Adding secondary categories

After selecting your primary category, add secondary categories for other services you offer. If you are a dentist who also offers orthodontics and teeth whitening, add those as secondary categories. Secondary categories expand the searches you can appear for.

### Updating categories

Google regularly adds new categories and refines existing ones. Review your categories every six months to ensure you are using the best available options.

## Step 3: Verify your business

Verification confirms to Google that your business genuinely exists at the address you have claimed. Unverified listings have limited functionality and reduced visibility.

### Verification methods

Google offers several verification methods depending on your business type and location:

- **Video verification:** The most common current method. You record a video showing the outside of your business, your business signage, your equipment or stock, and yourself in the space. Follow Google's instructions precisely, failed video verifications are common when businesses rush the process.
- **Phone or email:** Available for some businesses; Google calls or emails a code you enter in the dashboard.
- **Postcard:** A physical postcard is sent to your address with a code. This takes 5 to 14 days. Do not edit your address before the postcard arrives, as it will be cancelled.

### If verification is rejected

Common reasons for rejection include: your name on the video does not match your Google account, the signage is not visible, or the premises looks residential. Review Google's verification guidelines, address the issue and re apply. Suspension is possible for businesses that repeatedly attempt verification incorrectly.

## Step 4: Write a compelling business description

Your description appears in your Google Business Profile and helps both Google and potential customers understand who you are, what you do, and who you serve.

### What to include

- What your business does (core services or products)
- Where you serve (city, region or nationwide)
- What makes you different from competitors
- Any credentials, years of experience or notable achievements

### What to avoid

Google's guidelines prohibit:
- Promotional language (e.g. "best prices," "number one")
- Phone numbers or website links in the description
- Keyword stuffing
- Content unrelated to your business

### Length

Aim for 250 to 500 words in natural, conversational language. You have 750 characters available, but quality matters more than length.

## Step 5: Complete your services and products

Many businesses leave this section empty, which means Google cannot match them to specific service searches. Completing your services is a free, quick way to improve relevance.

### Services

Add each distinct service you offer. For each one:
- Use the exact service name customers search for
- Write a short, accurate description (one to two sentences)
- Add a price or price range if it helps customers qualify themselves

For a plumber, this might include: "Emergency call out," "Boiler repair," "Bathroom installation," "Leak detection." Each appears in your profile and helps Google understand your full offering.

### Products

If you sell physical products, the Products section lets you showcase them with photos, descriptions and prices. Products with good imagery and clear descriptions can appear in Google Shopping results.

## Step 6: Set your opening hours accurately

Inaccurate opening hours are one of the most damaging things for a local business profile. A customer who arrives when you are supposed to be open and finds you closed will leave a negative review, and future customers who see outdated hours will lose trust.

### Regular hours

Set your opening and closing times for each day of the week. If you are closed on a specific day, mark it as closed rather than leaving it blank.

### Holiday hours

Google prompts you to add special hours for public holidays. In Nigeria, key public holidays include New Year's Day, Democracy Day, Independence Day, and Christmas Day, among others. Keeping these updated prevents Google from showing "Permanently closed" on your profile when you are actually closed for a holiday.

### "More hours"

If your business has different hours for different services, for example, a restaurant with separate hours for breakfast, lunch and dinner, use the "More hours" feature to add these.

## Step 7: Add high quality photos and video

Profiles with photos consistently outperform those without in clicks, calls and direction requests. Google's own research shows that businesses with photos receive 42% more direction requests than those without.

### What photos to upload

| Photo type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Exterior (multiple angles) | Helps customers find you and establishes the physical location |
| Interior | Builds trust and sets expectations |
| Team | Humanises the business and shows real people |
| Work in progress | Demonstrates expertise and process |
| Completed work | Showcases quality and builds confidence |
| Products | Highlights stock and variety |
| Logo | Appears on your profile as your identity marker |
| Cover photo | The large featured image customers see first |

### Quality standards

Upload high resolution photos (at least 720 x 720 pixels, ideally 1080 x 1080 or larger). Google compresses uploaded images, starting with higher quality ensures the result remains sharp. Avoid using stock photos; real photos of your actual business convert significantly better.

### Video

Short videos (up to 30 seconds) can be uploaded directly to your profile. Walkthroughs of your premises, team introductions and before/after project videos all perform well.

### Frequency

Upload at least a few new photos every month. A profile with recent photo uploads signals an active, legitimate business. Stale profiles with no new photos from years ago often rank lower than regularly updated ones.

## Step 8: Collect and respond to reviews

Reviews are the most visible trust signal on your Google Business Profile, and they are a significant local ranking factor. A systematic approach to collecting and responding to reviews can be the single biggest differentiator between your business and a competitor.

### Building your review collection system

1. **Get your direct review link.** In your GBP dashboard, find "Get more reviews" and copy the link. Shorten it with a URL shortener for easier sharing.

2. **Automate review requests.** Include your review link in:
   - Your WhatsApp follow up messages after a job
   - Automated post purchase or post appointment emails
   - Your email signature
   - A card or flyer left at the end of a job
   - A QR code displayed at your reception or on your vehicle

3. **Time it right.** Ask when satisfaction is at its peak, immediately after a completed project, appointment or delivery.

4. **Ask personally.** "Would you mind leaving us a Google review? It really helps our business." Personal requests convert at much higher rates than automated ones alone.

### Responding to reviews

**Positive reviews:** Thank the reviewer by name, mention a specific detail from their review, and reinforce your key message. A response like "Thank you, Chidi! We're glad the kitchen installation went smoothly, it's a pleasure working with clients who know exactly what they want. Hope to see you again" is far more effective than a generic "Thanks for the review!"

**Negative reviews:** Respond within 24 hours. Acknowledge the experience, apologise genuinely, and offer a specific resolution, a call, a visit or a refund. Never be defensive, sarcastic or dismissive. Other readers will judge your professionalism by how you handle criticism.

### What not to do

- **Never buy reviews.** Google actively detects paid or incentivised reviews and can suspend your profile.
- **Never ask only happy customers.** This is considered selective solicitation and violates Google's guidelines.
- **Never write reviews for your own business or ask employees to.** These are detected and removed.

## Step 9: Post regular updates

Google Business Profile posts are short, public updates that appear on your profile in search results. They keep your profile active and give you a free, direct way to reach people who are already searching for you.

### Types of posts

- **What's new:** General announcements, news or updates. Expire after 7 days.
- **Offers:** Promotions with start and end dates. Appear with a yellow label.
- **Events:** For specific upcoming events with date and time.
- **Products:** Highlight a specific product with a photo and price.

### Best practices

- Post at minimum once a week
- Use a real, high quality photo, posts with photos see more engagement
- Keep copy short: under 150 words
- Include a clear call to action button (Call, Book, Learn more)
- Mention your location or service area naturally in some posts

## Step 10: Use Q&A to answer common customer questions

The Q&A section of your Google Business Profile allows anyone, including you, to post and answer questions publicly. Customers can see these questions and answers before they contact you.

### How to use Q&A proactively

Seed your own Q&A with the questions you hear most often:
- "Do you offer free quotes?"
- "How long does a typical installation take?"
- "Do you serve [specific area]?"
- "Are you open on weekends?"

Log in to your Google account, find your business profile in Google Search, click on Q&A and post the question yourself, then answer it. This fills your profile with useful information before a real customer has to ask.

## Step 11: Track your performance

Google Business Profile provides a Performance dashboard with key metrics. Review these monthly and track them against your baseline.

### Key metrics to monitor

| Metric | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Profile views | How many times your profile was shown |
| Website clicks | How often people clicked through to your website |
| Calls | How many people called directly from your profile |
| Direction requests | How many people asked for directions to your location |
| Messages | How many people messaged you via the profile |
| Booking clicks | How many people clicked your booking link |

Track these monthly in a simple spreadsheet alongside your review count and rating. This creates a baseline so you can measure what is working.

## Common Google Business Profile mistakes

### Adding keywords to your business name

This is a policy violation and can result in profile suspension. The business name field should contain only your actual business name.

### Using a virtual office or PO box as your address

Google requires a real, verifiable business address for service location businesses. Businesses that try to use virtual offices or mail forwarding services are frequently suspended. If you work from home, enable the service area option and hide your address instead.

### Duplicate profiles

If your business appears twice in Google Maps, those duplicate listings split your reviews, confuse customers and weaken your ranking signal. Report duplicates to Google and request their removal through the Business Profile support.

### Outdated opening hours

Incorrect hours frustrate customers and harm your reputation. Review your hours regularly and update them for holidays and seasonal changes.

### No response to reviews

An unanswered review, especially a negative one, signals disengagement. Customers read responses as carefully as they read reviews.

### Abandoning the profile after setup

A Google Business Profile is not a set and forget asset. Regular updates, fresh photos, new posts and actively collecting reviews all contribute to your ranking and conversion rate over time.

## Frequently asked questions about Google Business Profile

### How long does it take to rank in the map pack after setting up my profile?

It varies, but a fully optimised profile in a low to moderate competition market can appear in the map pack within four to eight weeks. Higher competition markets take longer. The combination of profile completeness, positive reviews and local citations typically produces the fastest results.

### Can I rank in a city where I do not have a physical address?

This is one of the most common questions. For businesses without a physical presence in a target city, ranking in the map pack is very difficult. Google favours businesses with a verified address close to the searcher. If you serve a broader area, service area settings help, but they do not replicate the ranking power of a physical location.

### What happens if my profile gets suspended?

Suspension is usually triggered by a policy violation: a keyword stuffed business name, an ineligible address, suspected fake reviews or a sudden change of category. Google provides a reinstatement request process. Be prepared to provide proof of business legitimacy: a business registration document, utility bill or photo of your signage.

### Does my Google Business Profile affect my website's organic rankings?

Directly, no. Your GBP and your website rankings are separate. But indirectly, a strong GBP profile, with many reviews, active posts and consistent NAP information, contributes to your overall local prominence, which does influence organic rankings for local searches.
