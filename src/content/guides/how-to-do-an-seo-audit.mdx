## What is an SEO audit?

An SEO audit is a structured review of everything that affects how your website performs in organic search. It examines whether search engines can find, crawl, understand and index your pages, whether those pages deserve to rank for the searches your customers make, and whether the visitors who arrive actually turn into enquiries, calls or sales.

A good audit is not a 200 page PDF full of red warnings exported from a tool. A good audit ends with a **short, prioritised action plan**: what to fix, in what order, who owns it, and what result you expect. Tools find issues; judgement decides which issues matter.

If you are new to search, start with our guide to [what SEO is and how it works](/guides/what-is-seo/), it gives you the vocabulary you'll need for the rest of this guide.

---

## Why you should audit your website

Most business owners only think about an SEO audit when something goes wrong, traffic drops, a competitor overtakes them, or a new website launches and enquiries dry up. Those are all good reasons, but regular audits prevent problems before they cost you money.

You should run an SEO audit when:

- **Organic traffic or leads have dropped** and you don't know why
- **You are about to redesign or migrate** your website (audit before *and* after)
- **You are hiring an SEO agency** and want to understand the starting point
- **A Google core update** has rolled out and your rankings have moved
- **It has been more than 12 months** since anyone looked closely at the site
- **You are expanding** into new services or locations

### How often should you do an SEO audit?

For most small and medium sized businesses, a **full audit once a year** plus a **light monthly health check** (Search Console errors, rankings for key terms, broken pages) is enough. Large ecommerce or publishing sites with thousands of URLs benefit from quarterly technical audits because problems multiply quickly at scale.

## What a complete SEO audit covers

A thorough audit looks at five areas. Skipping any of them leaves blind spots.

| Area | Key question | Typical tools |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | Can Google crawl, render and index the site? | Search Console, Screaming Frog |
| Performance | Is the site fast and stable on mobile? | PageSpeed Insights, CrUX |
| On page & content | Does each page answer its search intent better than competitors? | Manual review, keyword tool |
| Local | Is the business visible in the map pack and local results? | Google Business Profile, citation tools |
| Off page | Does the site have the authority and links to compete? | Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz |

The ten steps below walk through each area in the order we use for client audits.

## Step 1: Set goals and record a baseline

![Google Search Console Performance report showing organic clicks trending upward over 12 months, with key metrics: 4,821 clicks, 52,300 impressions, 9.2% CTR and average position 14.3](/images/guides/how-to-do-an-seo-audit/audit_step01_baseline.png)
*Your Google Search Console Performance report is the first place to capture your baseline. Record clicks, impressions, CTR and average position before making any changes so you can prove the impact of your work later.*

Before opening a single tool, decide what success means for the business. "More traffic" is not a goal. More qualified phone calls from people in your service area is a goal. More online orders of your highest margin products is a goal.

Then record your starting numbers, because without a baseline you cannot prove the audit led to improvement:

- **Organic clicks and impressions** (last 3, 6 and 12 months) from Google Search Console
- **Top 20 queries and top 20 landing pages** by clicks
- **Organic conversions**, form fills, calls, purchases, from Google Analytics 4
- **Average position** for 10 to 30 priority keywords
- **Google Business Profile actions**, calls, direction requests, website clicks
- **Number of indexed pages** vs. the number of pages you actually want indexed

Export these to a spreadsheet and date it. You'll compare against it in three and six months. If you want to know how quickly to expect movement, read [how long SEO takes](/guides/how-long-does-seo-take/).

> **Tip:** Compare year on year rather than month on month wherever possible. Many businesses are seasonal, and a "drop" in December may be perfectly normal.

## Step 2: Crawl the whole website

![Screaming Frog SEO Spider crawl results table showing 847 URLs found, with several rows highlighted red for 404 errors and missing title tags](/images/guides/how-to-do-an-seo-audit/audit_step02_crawl.png)
*Screaming Frog displays every URL on your site alongside its status code, title tag and meta description. Red cells immediately highlight broken pages and missing metadata that need attention.*

A crawler visits every page on your site the way a search engine bot would and records what it finds. [Screaming Frog SEO Spider](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/) is the industry standard and is free for up to 500 URLs. Sitebulb, Semrush Site Audit and Ahrefs Site Audit are good alternatives.

Run a full crawl and look for:

### Response codes
- **4xx errors (broken pages):** Fix the link pointing to them or redirect the URL to the most relevant live page.
- **5xx errors (server errors):** Escalate to your developer or host immediately, these stop Google crawling.
- **Redirect chains and loops:** A → B → C should become A → C. Each hop wastes crawl budget and dilutes link value.

### Titles and meta descriptions
- Missing, duplicated or truncated title tags
- Missing or duplicated meta descriptions
- Titles that don't contain the main keyword for the page

### Content signals
- **Thin pages** with very little unique content
- **Duplicate content**, including near identical location or product pages
- **Missing H1s or multiple H1s**

### Site architecture
- **Orphan pages**, pages with no internal links pointing to them
- **Crawl depth**, important pages should be reachable within three clicks of the homepage
- **Canonical tags** pointing to the wrong URL or to non indexable pages

Connect Screaming Frog to Search Console and Google Analytics via its API settings and you'll see traffic data next to each URL, invaluable for deciding which issues matter.

For a deeper explanation of each technical item, work through our full [technical SEO checklist](/guides/technical-seo-checklist/).

## Step 3: Check indexing in Google Search Console

![Google Search Console Pages indexing report showing a doughnut chart with 30 indexed and 38 not indexed pages, and a table breaking down not indexed reasons including 23 crawled but not indexed, 11 discovered but not indexed, and 4 excluded by noindex tag](/images/guides/how-to-do-an-seo-audit/audit_step03_indexing.png)
*The Search Console Pages report reveals exactly which pages Google has indexed and, crucially, why it left others out. Each reason in the table requires a different fix.*

Crawling tells you what *could* be indexed. [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) tells you what Google *actually* indexed, and why it ignored the rest.

Open **Indexing → Pages** and review:

- **Indexed pages:** Does the count roughly match the number of pages you want in search? A site with 40 real pages and 1,200 indexed URLs has a parameter, tag or pagination problem.
- **"Crawled, currently not indexed":** Google saw the page and decided it wasn't worth indexing. Usually a quality or duplication signal.
- **"Discovered, currently not indexed":** Google knows the URL exists but hasn't crawled it. Often an internal linking or crawl budget issue.
- **"Excluded by noindex tag":** Check that nothing important is accidentally noindexed, a common mistake after a site launch.
- **"Duplicate, Google chose different canonical":** Google disagrees with your canonical tag; investigate why.

Also confirm:

1. Your **XML sitemap** is submitted under Indexing → Sitemaps and returns no errors
2. Your **robots.txt** isn't blocking CSS, JavaScript or important sections
3. Your key money pages pass the **URL Inspection** test and render correctly

A quick sanity check: search `site:yourdomain.com` in Google. It's not precise, but wildly unexpected results are a red flag.

## Step 4: Test page speed and Core Web Vitals

![Google PageSpeed Insights results for a plumbing website showing a performance score of 71 in orange, with LCP at 3.2 seconds needing improvement, INP at 180ms in green and CLS at 0.05 in green, plus two opportunities listed below](/images/guides/how-to-do-an-seo-audit/audit_step04_pagespeed.png)
*PageSpeed Insights shows both a lab score and real user Core Web Vitals data. An LCP of 3.2 seconds tells you the main content loads too slowly, and the Opportunities panel pinpoints the exact files causing the delay.*

Page experience is a confirmed ranking signal and, more importantly, slow pages lose customers. Run your homepage, top service pages and top landing pages through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/).

Focus on the three [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) using **field data** (real users) where available:

| Metric | What it measures | Good threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) | Loading speed of the main content | ≤ 2.5 seconds |
| Interaction to Next Paint (INP) | Responsiveness to taps and clicks | ≤ 200 milliseconds |
| Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) | Visual stability while loading | ≤ 0.1 |

The most common fixes we find in audits:

- **Oversized images**, serve modern formats (WebP/AVIF) at the correct dimensions
- **Render blocking scripts**, defer non critical JavaScript, especially chat widgets and tracking tags
- **Slow hosting**, cheap shared hosting with a high time to first byte
- **Layout shift from ads, embeds or web fonts** without reserved space
- **Bloated page builders and plugins** on WordPress sites

Then **test on a real phone** over mobile data. Tap every button, open the menu, fill in the contact form. Lab scores don't catch a sticky banner that covers your phone number.

## Step 5: Review on page SEO for every key page

![Google Sheets on-page SEO review spreadsheet with columns for Page URL, Target Keyword, Search Intent, Title Tag, Meta Description, H1 Tag, Word Count, Internal Links and CTA Present. Cells are colour-coded green for good, yellow for needs improvement and red for missing](/images/guides/how-to-do-an-seo-audit/audit_step05_onpage.png)
*A colour-coded spreadsheet makes it easy to scan dozens of pages at once. Green cells pass, yellow need minor work, and red cells are the priority fixes your team should tackle first.*

Now move from the site as a whole to individual pages. List your 10 to 30 most important pages, usually the homepage, core service or category pages, location pages and top performing articles, and assign each a primary keyword and search intent.

For each page, check:

- **Search intent match:** Does the page format match what Google ranks? If the top results are lists of providers and yours is a blog post, you're fighting the wrong battle.
- **Title tag:** Includes the primary keyword near the front, is under roughly 60 characters, and gives a reason to click.
- **Meta description:** Summarises the benefit and includes a call to action.
- **H1 and heading structure:** One clear H1, logical H2s and H3s that cover the sub topics searchers care about.
- **Content depth and quality:** Does it answer the questions people actually ask? Does it show first hand experience, pricing guidance, process detail, photos and proof?
- **Internal links:** Does the page link to related services and guides, and do other pages link to it with descriptive anchor text?
- **Calls to action:** Is the next step obvious above the fold and repeated further down?
- **Images:** Compressed, with descriptive file names and alt text.
- **Schema markup:** LocalBusiness, Service, Product, FAQ or Article schema where relevant, validated in Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results).

Our [on page SEO checklist](/guides/on-page-seo-checklist/) covers each of these items in detail, with examples.

### Watch for keyword cannibalisation

Cannibalisation happens when two or more of your pages target the same keyword and compete with each other. In Search Console, filter by a query and click the **Pages** tab, if several URLs swap positions for the same term, consolidate them into one stronger page and redirect the others.

## Step 6: Run a content gap analysis

![SEO content gap report table showing keywords like emergency plumber london, boiler repair cost and drain unblocking near me. Competitor columns show green ranking positions while the Your Site column shows red dashes indicating the site does not rank for those terms](/images/guides/how-to-do-an-seo-audit/audit_step06_contentgap.png)
*A content gap report reveals the high volume searches your competitors rank for while you are invisible. Each row with a red dash in your column represents a page you need to create or improve.*

On page review tells you how good your existing pages are. A content gap analysis tells you **what's missing entirely**.

1. Pick three to five competitors who genuinely rank for your target searches (not necessarily your biggest offline rivals).
2. Use a keyword tool's "content gap" or "keyword gap" feature to find terms they rank for and you don't.
3. Filter for terms with commercial or local intent that match services you actually offer.
4. Group related keywords into topics, each topic becomes a page or a section of an existing page.

Also look at:

- **"People also ask" boxes** for your main keywords, each question is a potential FAQ or section
- **Customer questions** your sales and support teams hear every week
- **Outdated content**, articles with old prices, dates or advice that need refreshing

Don't chase every gap. Prioritise topics where you have real expertise and where a ranking would plausibly lead to revenue.

## Step 7: Audit your local SEO presence

If you serve customers in a specific area, a trade, clinic, law firm, restaurant or agency, local SEO is often where the fastest wins hide. Review:

### Google Business Profile
- Is the profile **verified** and owned by the business (not a former agency or employee)?
- Is the **primary category** the most specific one available?
- Are services, hours, holiday hours, service areas, attributes and description complete?
- Are there recent **photos** and regular **posts**?
- How many **reviews**, what's the average rating, and does the business respond to them?

Our [Google Business Profile optimisation guide](/guides/google-business-profile-optimization/) walks through every setting.

### NAP consistency and citations
Your business **name, address and phone number** should be identical on your website, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Facebook and major directories. Inconsistencies confuse search engines and customers.

### Local landing pages
If you serve multiple towns, do you have genuinely useful pages for each, with local proof, projects, reviews and directions, or thin copies with the town name swapped?

For the full local strategy, see our [complete local SEO guide](/guides/local-seo-guide/).

## Step 8: Review backlinks and authority

Links from other reputable websites remain one of the strongest signals Google uses to judge trust and authority. Use a backlink tool such as [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/backlink-checker), [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/) or [Moz Link Explorer](https://moz.com/link-explorer), plus the **Links** report in Search Console.

Assess:

- **Referring domains:** How many unique websites link to you, and how does that compare with competitors?
- **Link quality and relevance:** Links from local news, industry associations, suppliers and trade bodies are worth far more than random directories.
- **Anchor text:** A natural profile is mostly brand name and URLs. Lots of exact match keyword anchors can look manipulative.
- **Toxic patterns:** Sudden spikes of links from spammy, foreign language or unrelated sites. Google ignores most of these automatically; disavowing is only needed in rare cases, such as following a manual action.
- **Broken backlinks:** Links pointing to pages that now 404, redirect those URLs to recover the lost value.
- **Unlinked mentions:** Places that mention your brand without linking, an easy outreach win.

Then compare your profile with the three to five competitors from Step 6. The links they have and you don't form your link building shortlist.

## Step 9: Check analytics, tracking and conversions

An audit that ignores conversions can "improve SEO" while revenue stays flat. Verify that:

- **Google Analytics 4** is installed on every page, exactly once
- **Key events** (form submissions, phone clicks, purchases, bookings) are tracked and marked as conversions
- **Call tracking** is in place if phone calls are a major lead source
- **Search Console is linked** to Google Analytics
- **Internal traffic** from your office is filtered out
- **Consent mode** is configured correctly if you serve visitors in the UK or EU

Then look at the landing pages that get organic traffic but convert poorly. Sometimes the biggest audit win isn't a ranking at all, it's fixing a broken contact form or a hidden phone number on a page that already gets hundreds of visits.

## Step 10: Prioritise findings into an action plan

This is the step that separates a useful audit from an expensive spreadsheet. Score every issue on two dimensions:

- **Impact:** How much will fixing this improve rankings, traffic or conversions on pages that matter?
- **Effort:** How much time, money or developer involvement does it need?

Then sort into four groups:

| | Low effort | High effort |
|---|---|---|
| **High impact** | **Do now**, quick wins | **Plan**, schedule major projects |
| **Low impact** | **Batch**, fix when convenient | **Ignore** (for now) |

Typical "do now" items include fixing noindex tags on money pages, repairing broken contact forms, rewriting poor title tags on high impression pages, redirecting broken pages with backlinks and completing the Google Business Profile.

Typical "plan" items include site speed rebuilds, migrating to better hosting, consolidating cannibalised content and creating new service or location pages.

Present the result as a **one page action plan** with an owner and a target date for each item, followed by supporting detail for anyone who wants it. Revisit it monthly.

## The best SEO audit tools (free and paid)

You can complete a surprisingly thorough audit with free tools alone.

| Tool | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search Console | Free | Indexing, queries, Core Web Vitals, manual actions |
| Google Analytics 4 | Free | Traffic, engagement and conversions |
| PageSpeed Insights | Free | Speed and Core Web Vitals |
| Rich Results Test | Free | Validating schema markup |
| Screaming Frog | Free up to 500 URLs; paid licence beyond | Full site crawls |
| Bing Webmaster Tools | Free | A second opinion on crawl issues and backlinks |
| Ahrefs / Semrush | Paid | Keyword research, content gaps, backlinks |

Google's own [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) is also worth reading alongside your audit, it explains what Google itself considers good practice.

## Common SEO audit mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating every tool warning as urgent.** A "missing meta description" on a privacy policy page doesn't matter. A noindex tag on your main service page does.
2. **Chasing a perfect tool score.** A 100/100 health score is not the goal; revenue from organic search is.
3. **Auditing without a baseline.** You'll never know whether your fixes worked.
4. **Ignoring search intent.** Technically perfect pages that answer the wrong question won't rank.
5. **Forgetting mobile.** Most local searches happen on phones, audit on one.
6. **Producing a report nobody acts on.** If there's no owner and no deadline, nothing changes.
7. **Auditing once and never again.** Websites change, competitors improve and Google updates its algorithms.

## Frequently asked questions about SEO audits

### How long does an SEO audit take?
A light audit of a small business site (under 50 pages) can take a few hours. A thorough audit including competitor, content and backlink analysis typically takes two to five working days. Large ecommerce sites can take several weeks.

### How much does an SEO audit cost?
Costs vary widely depending on site size and depth, from free automated reports to several thousand for enterprise technical audits. Our [SEO pricing guide](/guides/how-much-does-seo-cost/) explains what drives the cost and what you should expect at each level.

### Can I do an SEO audit myself?
Yes. If you're comfortable with Search Console and a crawler, following the ten steps in this guide will uncover most problems. Where business owners usually need help is interpreting findings and prioritising, knowing which of the 300 warnings actually matter.

### What's the difference between a free and a paid SEO audit?
Free audits are usually automated scans that highlight surface level issues. A paid audit adds manual review, competitor and content analysis, local and backlink review, and, most importantly, a prioritised plan tailored to your business goals.

### Will an SEO audit improve my rankings?
The audit itself doesn't change anything; implementing its recommendations does. Quick technical fixes can show results within weeks, while content and authority improvements usually take three to six months or longer.

### What should be in an SEO audit report?
An executive summary, your baseline metrics, the top issues ranked by impact, a prioritised action plan with owners and dates, and supporting detail for each area (technical, performance, on page, local and off page).
