## What is local SEO and why does it matter?

Local SEO is the practice of optimising your online presence so your business appears when people nearby search for what you offer. When someone types "dentist near me," "plumber in Ikeja" or "best restaurant in Victoria Island" into Google, local SEO determines which businesses appear — and which don't.

The most valuable position in local search is the **map pack**: the three businesses that appear with a map at the top of Google's local results. A business in the map pack receives the majority of clicks on local searches. Position four — just outside the pack — receives dramatically less traffic than position three, even though they appear on the same page.

For any business that serves customers in a specific location, local SEO is one of the highest-return investments available. It brings calls, direction requests, website visits and walk-in customers from people who are actively looking for what you offer right now.

## How Google decides which businesses rank locally

Google describes three main factors that determine local rankings:

### 1. Relevance

How well does your business profile match what the searcher is looking for? If someone searches "accountant in Lekki" and your profile correctly identifies you as an accountant serving Lekki, you are relevant. If your categories are vague or wrong, Google may not show you.

### 2. Distance

How close is your business to the searcher or the location they specified? You cannot change where you are physically located, but you can expand your service area in your Google Business Profile if you serve a wider region.

### 3. Prominence

How well known and trusted is your business, both online and offline? Google measures this through:
- The quantity and quality of your reviews
- The number of websites that mention or link to your business
- Your position in organic search results
- How complete and active your Google Business Profile is

Of these three factors, prominence is the one you have the most control over, and it is where sustained local SEO effort delivers the biggest returns.

## Step 1: Set up and verify your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single most important element of local SEO. It powers the information shown in Google Maps, in the map pack and in the knowledge panel when someone searches your business name directly.

### Create or claim your listing

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and search for your business. If a listing already exists — perhaps created automatically by Google or by a previous owner — claim it. If not, create a new one.

Use your **exact legal business name**. Adding keywords to your business name (e.g., "Lagos Plumber — Best Prices") violates Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.

### Complete verification

Google offers several verification methods: video verification, phone, email or postcard. Video verification is the most common and requires you to show your premises, signage and equipment. Follow the process carefully — incomplete verification attempts can delay approval.

### Choose the right categories

Your **primary category** is one of the strongest ranking signals in local search. Choose the most specific, accurate description of your main business. If you are a dentist, choose "Dentist" not "Healthcare provider." If you are a general contractor, choose your most common speciality.

Add secondary categories for other services you offer. Look at the categories used by top-ranking competitors in your area for ideas.

### Complete every section

Leave nothing blank. Fill in:
- Business description (see Step 2)
- Opening hours (including holiday hours)
- Phone number (primary and secondary)
- Website URL
- Service areas (if you travel to customers)
- Appointment booking link
- WhatsApp link (increasingly used in Nigerian markets)
- Attributes (wheelchair accessible, women-led, etc.)

A complete profile ranks better than an incomplete one, and it converts better because customers get everything they need without visiting your website.

## Step 2: Build a systematic review collection process

Reviews are one of the most powerful local ranking signals, and they directly influence whether a searcher calls you or your competitor.

### Why reviews matter so much

Google's algorithm uses review quantity, recency, rating and the presence of keywords in reviews as ranking signals. A business with 100 recent reviews rated 4.7 stars will consistently outrank a similar business with 20 reviews rated 4.2 stars, all else being equal.

Beyond rankings, reviews are the first thing potential customers look at after finding you. Research by [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) consistently shows that the majority of consumers read online reviews before choosing a local business.

### How to collect reviews ethically

1. **Create your direct review link.** In your Google Business Profile dashboard, find your review link and shorten it. Share this link with customers.

2. **Ask at the moment of satisfaction.** The best time to ask is immediately after a positive customer experience — when the project is complete, the appointment ends or the delivery is received.

3. **Use multiple channels.** Ask in person, via WhatsApp message, SMS or email. Not everyone responds to the same channel.

4. **Make it effortless.** The fewer steps, the more reviews you collect. A direct link that opens the review box immediately is far more effective than asking customers to find you on Google.

5. **Never buy reviews or ask only happy customers.** Google actively detects fake or incentivised reviews and can suspend your profile. Ask all customers, not just the ones you know are happy.

### Responding to reviews

Reply to **every** review — positive and negative. Here is why:

- Responses show potential customers that you are engaged and professional
- For negative reviews, a calm, helpful response can recover the situation and shows others you take feedback seriously
- Responses that mention your services and location can provide a mild local SEO benefit

For negative reviews: acknowledge the concern, apologise for the experience, and offer to resolve it offline. Never argue or dismiss a negative reviewer publicly.

## Step 3: Build consistent local citations

A citation is any mention of your business's **Name, Address and Phone number (NAP)** on another website. Citations on authoritative directories are a key local ranking signal — they tell Google that your business genuinely exists at the address you claim.

### Why consistency matters

If your business is listed as "TechTide SEO Ltd" on Google, "Tech Tide SEO" on a directory and "TechTide" on another, Google treats these as potentially different businesses. Inconsistent NAP information weakens the trust signals that citations are supposed to provide.

### Priority citation sources

Start with these:
- **Google Business Profile** (primary)
- **Bing Places for Business** ([bingplaces.com](https://www.bingplaces.com))
- **Apple Maps** ([mapsconnect.apple.com](https://mapsconnect.apple.com))
- **Facebook Business Page**
- **Nigeria-specific directories:** VConnect Nigeria, ConnectNigeria, Naijacontacts, BusinessList Nigeria
- **Industry-specific directories** relevant to your trade
- **Your local Chamber of Commerce** listing

### How to audit existing citations

Search for your business name in quotes along with your phone number or address. Look for any listings with incorrect information and either claim and correct them or request their removal.

Tools like [Moz Local](https://moz.com/local), [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca) or [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com) can automate citation audits and submissions, though manual checking is important for accuracy.

## Step 4: Optimise your website for local search

Your website and your Google Business Profile work together. Google cross-references both and uses your website to validate and enrich your profile.

### Add LocalBusiness schema markup

[LocalBusiness structured data](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness) is code you add to your website that explicitly tells Google your business name, address, phone number, opening hours and the type of business you are. This is one of the clearest signals you can send.

Most web platforms support schema through plugins or built-in features. If you are using Astro (as this site does), LocalBusiness schema can be added in your Base layout alongside your Organisation schema.

### Create a clear contact page

Your contact page should include your full NAP — exactly as it appears on your Google Business Profile. Embed a Google Map showing your location. List the areas you serve. Make your phone number clickable on mobile.

### Create service pages for each major service

Instead of one generic "Services" page, create individual pages for each core service. A plumber might have separate pages for "bathroom fitting," "boiler repair" and "drain unblocking." This allows you to target different keywords with each page and link to the relevant category on your Google Business Profile.

### Create location pages for each area you serve

If you serve multiple areas, create a distinct, useful page for each one. Each page should include:
- Real local content about the area, not just a swapped city name
- Local testimonials or case studies if available
- An embedded map with the service area
- Clear contact details for that area

Avoid creating dozens of thin location pages. Google can detect "doorway pages" — multiple pages with nearly identical content — and may penalise your site for them.

## Step 5: Earn local links and mentions

Links from other local websites are a stronger signal than citations from directories. A link from your local newspaper, a business association, a supplier's website or a community organisation tells Google you are genuinely embedded in the local business ecosystem.

### How to earn local links

- **Local press:** Announce newsworthy things — opening, expansion, community initiatives, local hires — to local journalists and bloggers.
- **Supplier and partner links:** Ask suppliers to list you as an authorised dealer or partner. These links are often easy to get and highly relevant.
- **Business associations:** Join your local Chamber of Commerce, trade association or industry body. Most include a member directory with a link.
- **Sponsorships:** Sponsor a local sports team, school event or community project. These often include a link on the organisation's website.
- **Community involvement:** Host a local event, contribute a guest post to a local blog or offer expertise to a local news story.

## Step 6: Add and maintain Google Business Profile posts

GBP posts are short updates that appear directly on your profile in search results. They keep your profile active and give you an additional way to share offers, news, events and announcements.

### Best practices for GBP posts

- Post at least twice a month to signal an active business
- Use a high-quality photo with every post
- Keep the copy short and action-oriented
- Include a clear call-to-action button (Call, Learn more, Book)
- Posts expire after seven days for "What's new" posts; events and offers can have longer durations

## Step 7: Add photos and video consistently

Profiles with photos receive significantly more clicks than those without. Google's own data shows that businesses with more than 100 photos receive 2,717% more direction requests than the average business with fewer photos.

**What to upload:**
- Your exterior (at different times of day)
- Your interior
- Your team (with their permission)
- Your work in progress and completed projects
- Products you sell
- Your vehicles or equipment

Upload new photos regularly — at least a few per month. Fresh photo activity signals an active, legitimate business.

## Step 8: Track your local SEO results

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Track these metrics consistently:

| Metric | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Profile views | Google Business Profile Performance tab |
| Calls from Google | GBP Performance tab |
| Direction requests | GBP Performance tab |
| Website clicks from GBP | GBP Performance tab |
| Map pack positions | A local rank tracker or geo-grid tool |
| Review count and rating | Your GBP listing |
| Organic clicks for local terms | Google Search Console |

A **geo-grid tracking tool** shows you where you appear in the map pack across a grid of locations around your address. It reveals whether your visibility is strong everywhere or only close to your physical location.

## Common local SEO mistakes and how to avoid them

### Keyword stuffing in the business name

Adding keywords to your Google Business Profile name violates Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended. Use your exact trading name.

### Using a virtual office or PO box as your address

Google verifies business locations and has specific guidelines about what constitutes an eligible address. Businesses that use virtual offices or mail-forwarding addresses risk suspension. If you work from home, you can hide your address in your profile and show only a service area instead.

### Ignoring negative reviews

A negative review without a response sends a terrible signal to prospective customers. Always respond, calmly and professionally.

### Duplicate listings

If your business has been listed by multiple people, or if you have moved location and the old listing persists, you may have duplicate profiles. Duplicate listings split your review authority and confuse Google. Find and merge or remove duplicates through your Google Business Profile dashboard.

### Identical location pages

If you serve five cities and create five pages with identical text except for the city name, Google may treat them as duplicate content. Each location page needs genuinely unique, locally relevant content.

### Not tracking results

Local SEO without tracking is guesswork. Set up your baseline metrics at the start and review them monthly to see what is working and what needs attention.

## How long does local SEO take to show results?

Local SEO moves faster than national SEO. Google Business Profile improvements can produce measurable results — more calls, more profile views — within four to eight weeks in many markets. Competitive local markets take longer, but three to six months of consistent effort typically produces a clear improvement in map pack visibility and enquiries.

The key is consistency. Local SEO is not a one-time task. Google rewards businesses that actively manage their profiles, regularly collect fresh reviews, publish new photos and maintain accurate information.

## Frequently asked questions about local SEO

### Do I need a website to rank in local search?

No, not always. A Google Business Profile alone can rank in the map pack without a website. But a website significantly strengthens your local SEO by providing pages for Google to index, space for LocalBusiness schema, and location pages that target specific search terms.

### Can I do local SEO myself?

Yes. The fundamentals — completing your GBP, collecting reviews, keeping NAP consistent, adding photos — are all things a business owner can do. The more advanced work — creating location pages, building local links, tracking rankings with geo-grid tools — benefits from more expertise and dedicated time.

### What is the difference between local SEO and regular SEO?

Local SEO focuses specifically on searches with geographic intent and heavily involves your Google Business Profile, reviews and local citations. Regular (national or international) SEO focuses on broad keyword rankings through website content and links. Most local businesses need both.

### How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number, but in most local markets, having more than 50 recent reviews with a rating above 4.5 puts you in a strong competitive position. In lower-competition markets, fewer reviews may be sufficient. The key word is *recent* — a burst of reviews from two years ago counts for less than steady, ongoing review collection.
