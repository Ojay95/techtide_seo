## What is on page SEO?

On page SEO is the practice of optimising individual web pages so they rank higher in search results and convert more visitors into customers. Unlike technical SEO, which focuses on how the site works, and off page SEO, which involves building authority through links, on page SEO focuses on what is on the page itself: the words, structure, headings, images and calls to action.

A well optimised page does two things simultaneously:

1. **Signals clearly to Google** what the page is about, who it is for, and why it is the best answer to a specific search query
2. **Convinces the human visitor** to take action, call, enquire, buy or read more

On page SEO is where content strategy and conversion optimisation overlap. This checklist covers every element you should review and optimise on each important page of your website.

## Before you start: understand search intent

The most important step in on page SEO happens before you write a single word. You need to understand **search intent**, the underlying goal of someone making a search, because Google's primary job is matching content to intent, not just to keywords.

### The four types of search intent

| Intent type | What the searcher wants | Example query |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | To learn something | "what is local SEO" |
| Navigational | To find a specific site or page | "TechTide SEO contact" |
| Commercial | To research before buying | "best SEO agency in Lagos" |
| Transactional | To take an action now | "hire SEO consultant Nigeria" |

**Why this matters:** A service page will not rank for an informational query where all top results are guides and articles. A blog post will not rank for a transactional query where the top results are product or service pages. The format and content of your page must match the intent of the search.

### How to determine intent

Search your target keyword in Google. Examine the top five results:
- What type of content is ranking (articles, service pages, product pages, videos)?
- How long and detailed is the content?
- What questions do the top results answer?
- What does the searcher need to know to feel satisfied?

Your page needs to match and outperform the current top results on these dimensions.

## Part 1: Keyword research and targeting

### ✅ You have identified one primary keyword for this page

Every page should have a single, clear primary keyword, the main search term you want the page to rank for. Trying to rank for multiple competing keywords on one page fragments your focus and confuses Google.

**How to choose your primary keyword:**
- It should accurately describe what the page is about
- It should have search volume (people actually search for it)
- It should match the intent your page fulfils
- It should be realistic for your site's current authority to rank for

### ✅ You have identified supporting keywords and related terms

Beyond the primary keyword, every page should naturally use related terms, synonyms and questions that people asking about this topic also search for. These are sometimes called LSI (Latent Semantic Indexing) keywords, though a more accurate description is simply "topically related terms."

**How to find them:**
- Look at the "People also ask" box in Google for your primary keyword
- Look at the "Related searches" at the bottom of Google's results
- Use free tools like [Google Keyword Planner](https://ads.google.com/home/tools/keyword-planner/) or [Ubersuggest](https://neilpatel.com/ubersuggest/)
- Read the top ranking pages and note which related terms they use

### ✅ This page does not duplicate another page's keyword target

**Keyword cannibalisation** occurs when two or more pages on the same site target the same or very similar search terms. This causes the pages to compete with each other, which typically results in both ranking lower than one consolidated page would.

**How to check:** Search Google for `site:yourdomain.com "your keyword"` to see how many of your own pages appear for that search. If multiple pages appear, consolidate them or clearly differentiate their topics.

## Part 2: Title tag

The title tag is the text that appears in the browser tab, in search results as the clickable headline, and when the page is shared on social media. It is one of the most important on page SEO signals.

### ✅ The primary keyword appears near the start of the title

Google weights keywords that appear earlier in the title tag more heavily. Where natural, put your primary keyword within the first three to four words.

**Example:** "Local SEO Services in Lagos | TechTide SEO" is stronger than "TechTide SEO | Local SEO Services in Lagos."

### ✅ The title is 50 to 60 characters (including spaces)

Google typically displays titles up to around 60 characters. Titles longer than this are truncated in search results with an ellipsis, which can cut off important words. Titles shorter than 50 characters are under optimised.

**How to check:** Use an [SERP snippet preview tool](https://www.seoptimer.com/serp-snippet-optimizer) to see how your title and description appear in search results.

### ✅ The title is unique across the entire site

Every page should have a unique title tag. Duplicate title tags confuse Google about which page to rank for a given search and reduce the diversity of your keyword targeting across the site.

### ✅ The title is written to earn clicks, not just to rank

The title tag does not only influence rankings, it also determines whether a user clicks your result. A good title is:
- Specific: tells the user exactly what they will find
- Relevant to their search intent
- Compelling: includes a clear benefit or promise
- Honest: accurately represents the page content

## Part 3: Meta description

The meta description is the short summary that appears below the title tag in search results. Google does not use it as a direct ranking factor, but it significantly affects click through rate, which influences how much traffic your ranking generates.

### ✅ The meta description is 140 to 160 characters

Longer descriptions are truncated. Shorter descriptions leave opportunity on the table. Aim for 140 to 160 characters that clearly summarise the page's value.

### ✅ The description includes a clear benefit and call to action

Do not simply repeat the title. Instead, expand on it with the key benefit the visitor will get and, where appropriate, a call to action.

**Weak:** "This page explains local SEO and how it works."
**Strong:** "Learn exactly how local SEO works, the six steps to winning the map pack, and what to fix first on your Google Business Profile."

### ✅ The primary keyword appears naturally in the description

Google bolds keywords in the meta description that match the user's search query. A description containing the keyword increases visual relevance and can improve click through rate.

### ✅ Each page has a unique meta description

Like title tags, duplicate meta descriptions across multiple pages are a missed opportunity for differentiation. Write a unique description for each important page.

## Part 4: Headings (H1, H2, H3)

Heading tags are HTML elements that organise your content hierarchically. They tell both readers and search engines what the page is about and how information is structured.

### ✅ There is exactly one H1 on the page

The H1 is the primary heading of the page. There should be one and only one per page. It should clearly state the page's topic, typically using the primary keyword or a close variation.

**Common mistake:** Multiple H1s, often caused by a CMS adding an H1 from the page title while the content area also has its own H1. Check your page source for `<h1>` tags.

### ✅ The H1 matches the searcher's intent

The H1 does not need to be identical to the title tag, but it should serve the same purpose: immediately communicating to the visitor that they are in the right place. The H1 is often the first text a visitor reads on the page.

### ✅ H2 headings organise the main sections

H2 headings divide your content into major sections. Use them to cover the main sub topics that someone searching your keyword would expect the page to address. H2 headings are also often where you include related keywords and questions.

**Good practice:** Take the questions from Google's "People also ask" section and turn them into H2 headings within your content.

### ✅ H3 headings provide further subdivision where needed

Use H3 headings to break down long H2 sections into sub sections. Do not use heading tags purely for styling, use them to organise information logically.

## Part 5: URL structure

### ✅ The URL is short, readable and descriptive

A good URL communicates the topic of the page at a glance. Compare:
- `/services/local-seo/`, clear, descriptive, memorable
- `/services/?id=42&ref=nav`, confusing, not descriptive

### ✅ The primary keyword is included in the URL

Include your primary keyword in the URL slug. For a page targeting "local SEO services Lagos," a URL like `/services/local-seo-lagos/` is appropriate.

### ✅ The URL is lowercase with hyphens between words

Uppercase letters in URLs can cause case sensitivity issues on some servers. Use underscores (\_) only where necessary, hyphens are treated as word separators by Google; underscores are not.

### ✅ The URL is stable and permanent

Changing a URL after it has been indexed creates a broken page. If you must change a URL, implement a 301 redirect from the old URL to the new one. Avoid changing URLs without redirection.

## Part 6: Content quality and depth

This is where on page SEO merges with content strategy. A page that ticks all the technical boxes but contains thin, generic content will not rank consistently for competitive terms.

### ✅ The content fully answers the search query

Review the top ranking pages for your target keyword. Ask: what does a searcher need to know to feel completely satisfied by this page? Your content should cover that ground completely, not padded with filler, but genuinely comprehensive.

### ✅ The content demonstrates real experience

Google's E E A T guidelines place significant emphasis on **Experience**, does the content show that the author has first hand experience with the topic? For a page about plumbing services, photos of real work, explanations of your process and honest discussion of common problems all demonstrate experience that AI generated or generic content cannot fake.

### ✅ The content is accurate and up to date

Outdated information damages your credibility with users and signals to Google that the page has not been maintained. For any page covering facts, statistics or processes that change, schedule regular updates and update the publication date when meaningful changes are made.

### ✅ The primary keyword and related terms appear naturally throughout

Use your primary keyword and related terms where they naturally fit within the content, in the opening paragraph, in headings and throughout the body. Do not force repetitions; write for clarity first and let keyword use emerge from covering the topic well.

**Avoid keyword stuffing:** repeating a keyword unnaturally throughout the page is a Google ranking signal for low quality content and can actively harm your ranking.

### ✅ The content covers questions from "People also ask"

The People Also Ask (PAA) box in Google shows questions that users with the same search commonly ask. Answering these questions within your content signals that you have addressed the full scope of the topic, which can improve ranking and can earn your content a "featured snippet" placement.

### ✅ The page length is appropriate to the topic

Page length should match what the topic requires, not a target word count. A service page might be 600 to 1,000 words. A comprehensive guide might be 3,000 to 5,000 words. The question to ask is: what length is needed to genuinely answer this search better than the current top results?

## Part 7: Images and media

### ✅ Every image has a descriptive alt text

Alt text is the written description of an image, used by screen readers for visually impaired users and by Google to understand what the image shows. Write concise, accurate alt text that describes the image in context.

**Weak:** `alt="image1"`
**Strong:** `alt="Completed bathroom renovation project in Lagos, Nigeria"`

### ✅ Image file names are descriptive

The file name of an image is a minor signal to Google. Use descriptive names with hyphens rather than default camera names.

**Instead of:** `IMG_4923.jpg`
**Use:** `local-seo-services-lagos.jpg`

### ✅ Images are optimised for page speed

Oversized images are one of the most common causes of slow LCP scores. Compress images before uploading, serve them in WebP format, and add explicit width and height attributes to prevent layout shifts.

## Part 8: Internal links

Internal links connect your pages and serve two purposes: they help users navigate to related information, and they distribute "link equity", the ranking authority passed through links, around your site.

### ✅ The page links to relevant related content

Every page should include natural links to related guides, service pages or blog posts. This keeps visitors on your site longer, signals to Google the relationship between pages and distributes ranking authority.

### ✅ The page is linked to from other relevant pages

If nothing on your site links to a page, Google may deprioritise it as unimportant. After publishing a new page, identify two to three existing pages that are topically related and add a link to the new page from each.

### ✅ Link anchor text is descriptive

The clickable text of a link (the anchor text) is a signal to Google about the topic of the linked page. Use descriptive, relevant anchor text rather than generic phrases.

**Weak:** "Click here to learn more."
**Strong:** "Read our complete guide to local SEO in Nigeria."

### ✅ No orphan pages on the site

An orphan page has no internal links pointing to it from anywhere else on the site. Google can only discover it through the sitemap, and it receives no internal link authority. Run a monthly crawl with Screaming Frog to identify orphan pages.

## Part 9: Conversion optimisation

A page that ranks but does not convert is only half valuable. On page SEO includes making sure the page is set up to turn visitors into enquiries, leads or sales.

### ✅ The main call to action is visible above the fold

Visitors should be able to see your primary call to action without scrolling. Whether that is a phone number, an enquiry button or a booking form, it should be immediately visible when the page loads.

### ✅ Phone numbers are clickable on mobile

Mobile users should be able to call you with one tap. Use `<a href="tel:+234...">` for phone numbers. Test this on a real mobile device.

### ✅ Forms ask only for essential information

Every additional field in a form reduces completion rates. Ask only for what you actually need to follow up. Name, email and a brief message is sufficient for most initial enquiry forms.

### ✅ Trust signals appear near the call to action

Trust signals, reviews, testimonials, credentials, certifications, years in business, should appear near your conversion point. Buyers hesitate at the moment of commitment. A nearby five star review or "Google Verified Business" badge reduces that hesitation.

### ✅ The next step is unambiguous

A visitor arriving on your page should be able to answer the question "what do I do next?" without thinking. One clear primary call to action is more effective than multiple competing options.

## Part 10: Final checks

### ✅ The page loads quickly on a mobile connection

Test the page with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and check the mobile score. Aim for a score of 80 or higher, and ensure your LCP and CLS metrics are within the "Good" range.

### ✅ The title, description and H1 are all unique across the site

Run a crawl with Screaming Frog and review the duplicate meta titles and duplicate meta descriptions reports. Every important page should have unique, page specific tags.

### ✅ The page is indexed in Google

Confirm the page appears in Google's index. Search `site:yourdomain.com/page-url` in Google. If the page does not appear, check Search Console's URL Inspection tool to understand why.

### ✅ You have noted the starting position for your target keyword

Before you can measure improvement, you need a starting point. In Search Console under Search results, filter by your target keyword and note the average position and clicks. Set a reminder to review this in 30 days.

## The on page SEO review process

For any new or existing page, work through this order:

1. **Define intent**, what search does this page answer, and what does the searcher want?
2. **Research keywords**, primary keyword plus related terms and questions
3. **Check competition**, what do the top ranking pages look like?
4. **Optimise the title tag**, keyword near the start, 50 to 60 characters, compelling
5. **Write the meta description**, 140 to 160 characters with a benefit and CTA
6. **Set the URL**, short, clean, keyword included
7. **Write the H1**, matches intent, uses primary keyword
8. **Structure content with H2/H3s**, covers the topic fully, answers related questions
9. **Optimise images**, alt text, file names, compression
10. **Add internal links**, to and from related pages
11. **Optimise conversion elements**, CTA visibility, phone links, trust signals
12. **Final checks**, speed, uniqueness, indexing, baseline tracking

## Frequently asked questions about on page SEO

### How many times should I use my keyword on a page?

There is no magic number. Use the keyword where it naturally fits, in the title, H1, opening paragraph and a few more times throughout the content. If you are writing genuinely about the topic, natural keyword usage will follow. Focus on covering the subject well rather than hitting a keyword density target.

### Does my meta description affect my ranking?

Not directly. Google does not use the meta description as a ranking factor. However, a compelling description improves click through rate, which brings more traffic from the same ranking, and there is evidence that sustained high click through rates positively influence rankings over time.

### Should every page be optimised for SEO?

Every public facing page that you want people to find through search should be optimised. Supporting pages like privacy policies, terms of use and internal admin pages do not need keyword optimisation, but they should have descriptive title tags and should not accidentally be set to noindex if they serve a purpose.

### How often should I review and update on page SEO?

Key pages should be reviewed every six months. Competitive rankings can change, search intent can evolve, and outdated information can hurt your rankings over time. The most valuable pages on your site deserve regular attention.
