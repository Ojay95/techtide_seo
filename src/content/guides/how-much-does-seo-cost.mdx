## The honest answer

SEO prices vary widely, because the work depends on your market, your competition and your starting point. A single-location business in a quiet market needs far less than a multi-location company in a crowded one. Instead of searching for a magic number, learn what drives the price and what a fair quote should include.

## What drives the price

- **Competition.** Harder searches need more content, more links and more time.
- **Size of the site.** More pages mean more to fix and maintain.
- **Number of locations.** Each location needs its own profile, pages and reviews.
- **Starting point.** A site with technical problems needs fixing before it can grow.
- **Scope.** A plan that covers content and link building costs more than one limited to fixes and reporting.

## Common pricing models

| Model | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly retainer | A fixed monthly fee for an agreed scope | Ongoing growth |
| One-off project | A fixed price for an audit or a set of fixes | A clear, limited need |
| Hourly consulting | Paid by the hour for advice or training | In-house teams |

Monthly retainers are the most common for ongoing SEO, because results build over months, not days.

## What a fair quote includes

1. A clear scope: what you receive each month.
2. An audit or review of your current position.
3. Named deliverables, such as pages, posts and links.
4. Regular reporting on leads and calls, not only rankings.
5. Ownership: your content, accounts and data stay yours.

## Red flags

- **Guaranteed rankings.** Nobody controls Google.
- **Secret methods.** Good agencies explain what they do and why.
- **Very cheap bulk packages.** These often rely on low-quality links that can harm your site.
- **No reporting.** If you can't see the work, you can't judge it.

## How to compare quotes

Ask each agency what is included, how they report, how long the minimum commitment is and what happens if you leave. Compare scope, not only price.
