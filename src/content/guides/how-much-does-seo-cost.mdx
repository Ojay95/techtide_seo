## The honest answer to "how much does SEO cost?"

SEO pricing ranges from $300 to $30,000+ per month depending on the business, the market and the scope of work. That range is not a cop out, it reflects real differences in what each situation requires.

A single location restaurant in a smaller city needs far less than a multi location ecommerce brand competing nationally against established players. What you should pay is what your specific situation demands, not an industry average.

This guide breaks down exactly what drives the cost, which pricing models exist, what a fair quote actually includes, the red flags that signal a bad agency, and how to compare options confidently.

---

## What factors drive SEO pricing?

Understanding what goes into the price helps you evaluate any quote you receive.

### 1. Competition

This is the single biggest driver. In a competitive market, law firms, insurance, finance, real estate, thousands of businesses are investing in SEO. Winning or holding a top three position requires more content, more links, more technical precision and more time than in a less contested space. The more you need to outrank well funded competitors, the more the work costs.

### 2. Size and complexity of your website

A five page service site needs far less maintenance than a 500 page site with an ecommerce catalogue, a blog and multiple location pages. Crawl audits, internal link management, page optimisations and technical fixes scale with the size of the site.

### 3. Number of locations

Each physical location needs its own Google Business Profile, its own location page on your website, its own set of local citations and reviews. If you have five locations, you have five times the local SEO workload.

### 4. Your starting point

A site that is already technically sound, with a good link profile and decent content, costs less to grow than one starting from scratch, or one that has been penalised by a previous agency's bad practices. Fixing damage takes time before progress can begin.

### 5. Scope of service

A plan that covers only technical fixes is cheaper than one that includes content creation, link building, local SEO and paid search coordination. You get what you scope for.

### 6. Level of access and support

Some clients want a monthly report and low involvement. Others want weekly calls, real time dashboards and a dedicated strategist. Premium access costs more.

## Common SEO pricing models

There are three main ways agencies structure their fees. Each suits different situations.

### Monthly retainer

A fixed monthly fee for an agreed scope of ongoing work. This is the most common model for growing businesses, because SEO is not a one time project, it is ongoing. Content needs to be created, links earned, technical issues monitored, and results reported and acted on every month.

**Typical range:** $500, $10,000+ per month depending on scope and competition.

**Best for:** businesses that want sustained, compounding growth.

### One off project

A fixed fee for a defined piece of work, most commonly an SEO audit. An audit gives you a clear diagnosis of what is holding your site back and a prioritised plan to fix it, without committing to an ongoing relationship.

**Typical range:** $500, $5,000 depending on site size.

**Best for:** businesses that want to understand their position before committing, or those with in house teams that can execute the plan.

### Hourly consulting

You pay by the hour for strategic advice, training or review. This suits in house marketing teams who can implement independently but need external expertise for direction, audits or specific technical problems.

**Typical range:** $75, $300 per hour.

**Best for:** businesses with an in house team that needs guidance, not execution.

## What does a fair monthly retainer include?

Many businesses sign SEO agreements and are never quite sure what they are actually paying for. A fair, transparent retainer should include at minimum:

| What you should receive | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| A clear scope of work | You know exactly what deliverables to expect each month |
| An initial audit or review | You understand your starting position and priorities |
| Named deliverables | Specific pages optimised, content pieces produced, links targeted |
| Reporting on leads, not just rankings | Rankings alone don't pay bills, conversions do |
| Access to your own accounts | Your Google Search Console, Analytics and Business Profile are yours |
| A clear exit policy | Your content, data and accounts remain yours if you leave |

If an agency cannot clearly answer "what specifically will you do each month?", that is a problem.

## How does SEO cost compare to other marketing channels?

It is worth placing SEO investment in context against the alternatives.

| Channel | Cost structure | Visibility |
|---|---|---|
| Google Ads (PPC) | Pay per click, stops when budget ends | Immediate but temporary |
| Social media advertising | Pay per impression/click, ongoing | Immediate but interruptible |
| SEO | Monthly investment, compounds over time | Slow to build, but lasting |
| Referrals / word of mouth | No direct cost, unpredictable | Unscalable |

The key difference with SEO is that the value you build does not disappear when you stop paying. A page that ranks today can bring enquiries for years. That compounding nature means the return on investment typically improves over time as the cost stays fixed while traffic grows.

## Red flags that signal a bad agency or deal

### "Guaranteed page one rankings"

Nobody can guarantee a Google ranking. Search engines make that decision. Any agency making this promise is either lying or using risky black hat tactics that can get your site penalised.

### Very cheap bulk packages

SEO services sold at $50, $150 per month as packages typically rely on automated link building, low quality content farms or other tactics that violate Google's guidelines. These can cause lasting damage to your site's reputation.

### No transparency about methods

A good agency explains exactly what they are doing and why. If an agency is vague about their methods, it is usually because the methods would not survive scrutiny.

### No ownership of your data

Your Google Search Console property, your Analytics account, your Business Profile and any content produced for you should be yours. Agencies that keep control of your accounts to make you dependent on them are not operating in your interest.

### No reporting or vague reporting

If you cannot see what the work produced, pages published, links earned, rankings moved, calls generated, you have no way to evaluate whether the investment is working.

### Locking you in without results

Some contracts lock businesses in for 12 months with no performance expectations. A confident, ethical agency will agree a clear scope of work and be willing to discuss what happens if the results disappoint.

## Questions to ask before hiring an SEO agency

Before signing anything, ask every agency you are considering these questions:

1. **What specifically will you do each month?** Ask for a written scope.
2. **How do you measure success?** Rankings are a proxy; leads and revenue are the goal.
3. **Who will work on my account?** Junior freelancers or experienced strategists?
4. **Can I see examples of work you've done for similar businesses?**
5. **What happens if I leave? Do I keep my content and accounts?**
6. **What is the minimum commitment, and what notice is required?**
7. **How will you report, and how often?**
8. **Have you ever had a client receive a Google penalty? How did you handle it?**

## Is cheap SEO ever worth it?

Occasionally. A small local business with low competition might get good results from a lightweight, lower cost engagement if the agency is honest about scope. The danger is not the price, it is whether the work being done is genuine and sustainable.

The cost of recovering from a penalty caused by bad SEO, lost rankings, lost traffic, lost enquiries, and the time and money to fix it, far exceeds the cost of having done good SEO from the start.

When evaluating a quote, the question is not "is this cheap?" but "does this scope justify the price, and does this agency do work I can be proud of?"

## How to compare SEO quotes

When you have multiple quotes, do not compare on price alone. Compare on these dimensions:

- **Scope:** what deliverables are named?
- **Reporting:** what metrics will you see, and how often?
- **Communication:** who is your point of contact and how often do you speak?
- **Track record:** do they have relevant experience?
- **Terms:** what is the minimum commitment and exit policy?
- **Ownership:** who owns the content and data at the end?

A more expensive agency that delivers clear work, transparent reporting and genuine results is a better investment than a cheaper one that does invisible work with no accountability.

## Frequently asked questions about SEO pricing

### Is SEO worth it for a small business?

Yes, especially for local businesses. A small business with a well optimised Google Business Profile, good reviews and a handful of well targeted pages can compete effectively for local searches even against larger competitors. The return on a modest monthly investment can be significant when even one or two new clients per month more than covers the cost.

### How do I know if I'm overpaying?

Review what you receive each month against the deliverables in your contract. If the reporting is vague, the content is thin and nothing seems to be improving after six months, you may be overpaying. A third party audit can give you an independent view.

### Can I do SEO myself to save money?

Yes, especially for the basics. Setting up Search Console, fixing obvious technical errors, writing good page titles and completing your Google Business Profile are all doable without an agency. The limitation is time and expertise, SEO done well is a specialist, full time discipline.

### What is the ROI of SEO?

This varies enormously by business. A business where a single new client is worth $10,000 can justify a $2,000/month SEO investment if it brings in even one new client every two months. Track calls, enquiries and revenue attributable to organic search to calculate your return.
