## What is SEO?

SEO, or search engine optimisation, is the practice of making your website more visible in organic (unpaid) search engine results so that the right people find you at exactly the moment they need what you offer. It involves a combination of technical improvements, well-crafted content, and earning trust from other authoritative websites.

The goal of SEO is not traffic for its own sake. The goal is qualified traffic — people who are actively searching for your product, service or expertise — and then turning that traffic into calls, leads and revenue.

If you have ever typed a question into Google and clicked a result, you have experienced SEO from the user's side. On the business side, SEO is the disciplined, ongoing work that earns your page one of those clicks instead of your competitor's page.

## How do search engines work?

To understand SEO, you need to understand what search engines actually do. Google — which handles [over 90% of global searches](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share) — performs three core jobs before it shows you a result.

### 1. Crawling

Google sends out automated programs called crawlers or spiders. These programs follow links across the internet to discover new and updated pages. If your page has no links pointing to it from anywhere else, crawlers may never find it.

### 2. Indexing

When a crawler finds a page, Google analyses it and stores a representation of it in a massive database called the index. Google evaluates the page's topic, quality, language, and whether it offers genuine value to a reader.

If a page is blocked by a `robots.txt` rule, has a `noindex` tag, or is very thin or duplicate, Google may choose not to index it. Pages that are not indexed cannot rank.

### 3. Ranking

When a user types a search query, Google scans its index and selects the pages it believes best answer that query. It then orders them — that ordering is the ranking. Over 200 signals influence ranking, but the core question is always: which page best satisfies this search?

Understanding this three-step process tells you where to focus your SEO energy: make sure Google can find and index your pages, and then make sure those pages are the best answer for the searches that matter to your business.

## Why does SEO matter for your business?

### It captures demand that already exists

People search before they buy. Research by [BrightEdge](https://videos.brightedge.com/research-report/BrightEdge_ChannelReport2019_FINAL.pdf) consistently shows that organic search drives more than half of all website traffic across industries. These are people actively looking for what you offer — the intent is already there.

### It compounds over time

Unlike paid advertising, where visibility stops the moment you stop paying, a well-ranked page keeps delivering visitors month after month. The content you publish today can still bring enquiries two years from now.

### It builds trust and authority

Users trust organic results more than ads. Appearing on the first page of Google signals credibility, especially when your listing appears alongside positive reviews, informative content and a professional website.

### It is measurable

With tools like [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) and Google Analytics, you can see exactly which searches bring visitors, which pages convert them, and what your return on investment is.

## What are the five parts of SEO?

SEO becomes manageable once you understand that it divides into five connected disciplines. Each supports the others, which is why the strongest SEO strategies address all five together.

### 1. Technical SEO

Technical SEO ensures that search engines can find, crawl and understand your website. Without a solid technical foundation, even great content can fail to rank.

Key areas include:

- **Site speed** and Core Web Vitals (how fast your pages load and respond)
- **Mobile friendliness** (Google uses the mobile version of your site for indexing)
- **Crawlability** (no important pages blocked by robots.txt or missing from your sitemap)
- **Indexation** (confirming your key pages are actually in Google's index)
- **HTTPS security** (a basic trust signal)
- **Structured data** (schema markup that helps Google understand your content type)
- **URL structure** (clean, descriptive, logical URLs)

Learn more about [technical SEO and how we fix it](/services/technical-seo/).

### 2. On-page SEO

On-page SEO shapes each page so it clearly matches what your target user is searching for and guides them towards an action.

Key areas include:

- Writing descriptive, keyword-rich title tags that earn clicks
- Crafting clear H1 and H2 headings that organise information logically
- Using the target keyword and related terms naturally throughout the page
- Optimising meta descriptions to improve click-through rates
- Adding descriptive image alt text
- Creating clear, compelling calls to action

Learn more about [on-page SEO and content optimisation](/services/on-page-seo/).

### 3. Local SEO

If your business serves customers in a specific location — a city, region or country — local SEO ensures you appear when those customers search nearby.

The most valuable local ranking position is the **map pack**: the three businesses that appear on a map at the top of local search results. Winning a place there brings direct calls, direction requests and visits.

Key areas include:

- Setting up and fully optimising your Google Business Profile
- Collecting and responding to customer reviews
- Maintaining consistent Name, Address and Phone (NAP) across directories
- Building local links and citations
- Creating location-specific service pages

Learn more about [local SEO for businesses in any market](/services/local-seo/).

### 4. Content SEO

Content SEO means creating genuinely useful pages, guides and blog posts that answer the questions your potential customers are already asking. It builds your authority in a topic area over time, and each well-ranked piece of content can bring in enquiries for years.

Key areas include:

- Keyword research to find what your customers actually search
- Creating a content calendar that groups related topics
- Writing in-depth, accurate, experience-led content
- Updating older content to keep it current and competitive
- Using internal links to connect pieces of content and guide visitors

Learn more about [our content SEO service](/services/content-seo/).

### 5. Authority (Link Building)

Links from other reputable websites to yours are one of Google's strongest ranking signals. A link from a trusted, relevant site tells Google that your page is credible and worth recommending.

Key areas include:

- Earning links through high-quality content others want to cite
- Building relationships with local organisations, suppliers and press
- Digital PR: producing original research or data that earns media coverage
- Outreach to relevant websites with a genuine reason to link to you

## What is keyword research?

Keyword research is the process of finding the specific words and phrases people type into search engines when looking for your product or service. It is the foundation of almost every other SEO activity, because it tells you what to optimise for, what content to create and how to prioritise your effort.

### Types of keywords to consider

| Type | Example | User intent |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | "what is SEO" | Learning, research |
| Commercial | "best SEO agency in Lagos" | Comparing options |
| Transactional | "hire SEO consultant" | Ready to act |
| Local | "SEO company near me" | Location-based need |
| Long-tail | "how long does SEO take for a new website" | Specific, lower competition |

The most valuable keywords for your business combine moderate search volume with clear commercial or transactional intent — people who are ready to buy or enquire, not just browse.

Free tools like [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) show you what searches you already appear for. [Google Keyword Planner](https://ads.google.com/home/tools/keyword-planner/) gives you volume estimates. Paid tools like Ahrefs, Semrush or Ubersuggest give you competitive data.

## How does Google decide what ranks?

Google's ranking algorithm evaluates hundreds of factors, but the most important ones are well documented:

### Relevance

Does the page clearly match what the user searched for? Google reads the content, headings, title, and the context of links to understand what a page is about.

### Quality and experience

Google's [Search Quality Rater Guidelines](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf) describe E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness. Pages that demonstrate real first-hand experience, written by identifiable experts, from credible sites, tend to rank above thin, anonymous content.

### Links

The number and quality of links from other reputable sites pointing to your page remains one of the strongest ranking signals.

### User experience signals

Google measures how people interact with search results. If users click your result and immediately click back, that signals dissatisfaction. Fast-loading, easy-to-use pages that genuinely help people perform better.

### Freshness

For some queries, especially news or time-sensitive topics, recently updated content ranks higher. For evergreen questions, freshness matters less than depth and authority.

## What is the difference between organic and paid search?

**Organic results** are the listings that appear because Google considers them the best match for a search. They are earned through SEO and do not cost money per click. They compound over time.

**Paid results** (often called PPC or Pay-Per-Click) are advertisements. A business bids on keywords and pays each time someone clicks. They appear at the top and bottom of results pages, often labelled "Sponsored." They stop the moment the budget runs out.

Both have a role. Paid search delivers immediate, controllable visibility. SEO delivers lasting, compounding visibility that costs nothing per click once earned. For most businesses, the best long-term strategy is to use paid search for short-term needs while investing in SEO for long-term growth.

## What is black-hat SEO and why does it matter?

Black-hat SEO refers to techniques that try to manipulate search rankings in ways that violate Google's guidelines. Examples include buying backlinks, hiding keyword-stuffed text on pages (keyword stuffing), creating doorway pages and other deceptive practices.

These tactics can produce short-term gains, but Google actively detects and penalises them. A manual or algorithmic penalty can cause your rankings to collapse entirely, sometimes taking months or years to recover from — if at all.

Ethical, sustainable SEO — sometimes called white-hat SEO — follows Google's guidelines, focuses on genuine quality and earns trust over time. It is slower but durable.

## How long does SEO take to work?

There is no single honest answer, but these are realistic patterns:

| Timeline | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–4 | Audit, baseline measurement, first technical fixes. Little visible change in rankings. |
| Months 2–3 | Fixed pages re-indexed. Early movement on local and low-competition searches. |
| Months 4–6 | Content and links build up. Steadier growth in visibility, clicks and enquiries. |
| Months 6–12 | Compounding results as pages age and authority grows. Progress on harder searches. |

Local SEO results — especially Google Business Profile improvements — can show within weeks. Competitive national searches often take six months or more.

## What should you measure?

Rankings are not enough. Track the metrics that connect to your business outcomes:

- **Organic clicks and impressions** — from Google Search Console
- **Goal completions** — calls, form submissions, bookings, purchases
- **Organic revenue or enquiry value** — the actual business impact
- **Keyword rankings** — for your target searches, tracked over time
- **Backlink growth** — quantity and quality of links pointing to your site
- **Core Web Vitals** — speed and user experience scores from Search Console

## Common SEO mistakes to avoid

### 1. Ignoring technical foundations

Content is wasted if Google cannot properly crawl and index your site. Fix the technical base first.

### 2. Targeting the wrong keywords

High-volume keywords are not always the right keywords. Align your targets with what your buyers actually search and what you can realistically compete for.

### 3. Thin or duplicate content

Pages with very little unique, useful content rarely rank. Every page should earn its place by answering a specific question better than the alternatives.

### 4. Ignoring mobile users

Google indexes the mobile version of your site. A poor mobile experience hurts both rankings and conversions.

### 5. Expecting instant results

SEO is an investment. Businesses that stick with it consistently for six to twelve months see the compounding returns. Those who stop after two months rarely do.

### 6. Buying links

Purchased links violate Google's guidelines and can trigger penalties that are costly to recover from. Earn links through genuine quality and outreach.

## Where should you start with SEO?

If you are new to SEO and want to prioritise, work through these five steps:

1. **Check that Google can find you.** Open [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console), set up your property and review the Pages report to see what is indexed.

2. **Fix obvious technical problems.** Check your site speed with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/), make sure you are using HTTPS, and confirm your site works well on mobile.

3. **Optimise your most important pages.** For each key page, ensure it has one clear topic, a unique title tag with the target keyword, and a clear call to action.

4. **Set up or improve your Google Business Profile.** If you serve local customers, a complete, verified profile is the fastest way to gain local visibility.

## Frequently asked questions about SEO

### Does SEO still work in 2026?

Yes. Organic search remains one of the highest-intent channels in digital marketing. Google's algorithms have become more sophisticated, which means that genuine quality, experience and usefulness matter more than ever — but the fundamental value of ranking for the searches your customers make has not changed.

### What is the difference between SEO and SEM?

SEM (Search Engine Marketing) is the umbrella term that covers both SEO (organic) and paid search advertising (PPC). SEO earns traffic; paid search buys it.

### Can I do SEO myself?

Yes, especially for a small local business. The technical and on-page fundamentals are learnable. The challenge is time: SEO done well requires consistent, ongoing effort. Many businesses start by learning the basics and then partner with an agency to scale.

### What is local SEO?

Local SEO is the practice of optimising your online presence specifically for location-based searches — "near me" queries or searches that include a city or region. It centres on your Google Business Profile, reviews and local citations. Read our [complete local SEO guide](/guides/local-seo-guide/).

### How does Google AI affect SEO?

Google's AI-powered features — including AI Overviews and Gemini integration — change how results look, but they still pull from indexed pages. The fundamentals of SEO remain: be the best, most trustworthy answer to a query. Read our blog post on [how to rank in AI search](/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-search-geo-aeo/) for specific tactics.
