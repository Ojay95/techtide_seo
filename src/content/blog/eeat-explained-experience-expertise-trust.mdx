If you read much about SEO, you'll keep running into four letters: E-E-A-T. It stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness. People talk about it as if it's a secret switch you can flip to rank higher. It isn't, and that misunderstanding sends many businesses in the wrong direction.

The truth is simpler and more useful. E-E-A-T describes what makes a source worth believing. When you're choosing a plumber, a doctor or an SEO agency, you look for the same things Google's systems are trying to recognise: has this person done this before, do they know their subject, do others respect them and can I trust what they say?

This guide explains what E-E-A-T is and isn't, why it matters and how to show it on your own website. You'll get a practical checklist of 12 things you can do, with examples. There's no jargon and no tricks.

## What E-E-A-T stands for

- **Experience:** first-hand, real-world involvement. A review from someone who used the product. A guide written by someone who has done the job.
- **Expertise:** knowledge and skill in the subject. Qualifications help, but practical skill counts too.
- **Authoritativeness:** being recognised as a go-to source by others. Mentions, links, citations and reputation.
- **Trustworthiness:** being honest, accurate, transparent and safe. This is the most important of the four.

Google's own descriptions say trust is the centre, and the other three support it. A site can be experienced and knowledgeable, but if it hides who it is or gets facts wrong, people won't trust it.

## What E-E-A-T is not

A few myths are worth clearing up.

**It's not a single ranking factor.** Google has said E-E-A-T isn't a direct ranking factor or a score you can see. It comes from the guidelines given to human quality raters, who help Google test how well its search systems work. Google's ranking systems look at many signals that line up with these ideas.

**It's not a checkbox.** You can't add a plugin and become "E-E-A-T compliant".

**It's not only for medical or financial sites.** It matters most for topics that could affect people's health, money or safety, which Google calls "Your Money or Your Life" topics. But every site benefits from being clear and trustworthy.

**It's not about big brands.** Small businesses with real experience can show E-E-A-T just as well, often better, because they can show real work.

Google's guidance on [creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) uses the questions "Who, How and Why" to explain the same idea: who created the content, how it was made and why it exists. And you can read more about how Google thinks about quality on its [How Search works](https://www.google.com/search/howsearchworks/) site.

## Why it matters for your business

Even if E-E-A-T were never mentioned, the ideas still matter, for three reasons.

1. **Customers decide on trust.** Visitors judge you in seconds. Clear information and real proof turn them into enquiries.
2. **Search and AI tools favour reliable sources.** AI answers rely on pages that are clear and consistent. See [how to rank in AI search](/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-search-geo-aeo/).
3. **It protects you from low-quality content.** Pages written without real knowledge are the easiest to replace.

## 12 practical ways to show E-E-A-T

### 1. Show who wrote it

Add an author name, short bio and a photo or avatar to articles. Say why this person is qualified to write about the topic. A line like "Adaeze has helped Nigerian businesses with local SEO for eight years" is more convincing than "Written by Admin".

### 2. Use real, specific experience

Share what you actually did. Real examples, photos from your own work, mistakes you've seen and lessons you learned are impossible to copy. "In our experience with hotels in Lagos..." works only if it's true.

### 3. Build a proper About page

Tell people who you are, what you do, how long you've done it and who is on your team. Include your registered business name, address and contact details. Add credentials, certificates and awards if you have them.

![Screenshot: an About page with team members, credentials, reviews and contact details](/images/screenshots/about-page-trust-signals.png)

*Figure 3: A good About page shows people, proof and a way to reach you.*

### 4. Make contact details easy to find

A real address, phone number and email show you're accountable. Hiding behind a contact form alone looks suspicious. A WhatsApp or phone number is especially reassuring for local customers.

### 5. Back up claims with sources

When you state a fact, link to a trustworthy source, such as official documentation, research or a reputable publication. It shows you've done your homework and lets readers check you.

### 6. Show real reviews and testimonials

Genuine reviews are strong proof. Display your Google rating, link to your profile and share testimonials with permission. Never invent or buy them. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

### 7. Show your results

Case studies with real numbers, before-and-after photos and named clients (with permission) turn claims into proof. If you don't have them yet, start with one pilot project.

### 8. Keep content accurate and up to date

Review important pages regularly. Correct anything out of date, and show when the page was last updated. Old prices and outdated advice quickly damage trust. A [content audit](/blog/how-to-do-a-content-audit/) helps you find the pages to fix.

### 9. Be open about how you work

Explain your process, your prices or price ranges, your terms and your policies. Make your privacy policy, terms and refund or guarantee details easy to find. Transparency builds trust.

### 10. Earn mentions from respected sources

Links, citations and mentions from reputable sites show others recognise you. Think local organisations, suppliers, industry bodies, news sites and directories. Our guide to [safe link building](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/) shows how.

### 11. Keep the site secure and professional

Use HTTPS, avoid broken pages, intrusive pop-ups and spelling mistakes, and make the site work well on phones. These small details affect how credible you seem. See our [technical SEO for beginners](/blog/technical-seo-for-beginners/).

### 12. Add structured data that reflects reality

Organization, Person and Article markup can help search engines understand who you are and who wrote what. Only mark up what's visible on the page. Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) explains how.

## Be careful with high-stakes topics

If you write about health, money, law or safety, the bar is higher. Content should be written or reviewed by qualified people, cite reliable sources and avoid guarantees. If you're not qualified, say so clearly, and point readers to proper professionals. Don't pretend.

## What to avoid

- **Fake author bios and credentials.** Inventing an expert is dishonest and risky.
- **Fake reviews and testimonials.** They break platform rules and can destroy trust.
- **Stolen or generic content.** Copying others shows no experience at all.
- **Hiding who you are.** Anonymous sites are harder to trust.
- **Over-claiming.** "The best in Nigeria" without proof sounds empty.
- **Using AI to invent expertise.** AI can help with drafts, but people must supply real experience and check facts. See [how to use AI for SEO without hurting your rankings](/blog/how-to-use-ai-for-seo-content/).

## A quick E-E-A-T checklist

- Does every article show who wrote it and why they're qualified?
- Is there a clear About page with real people and real details?
- Can visitors easily find your address, phone and email?
- Are claims backed by sources, examples or results?
- Are reviews and testimonials genuine and visible?
- Is the content accurate, dated and reviewed?
- Are privacy, terms and pricing information easy to find?
- Do reputable sites mention or link to you?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is E-E-A-T a ranking factor?
Google says it isn't a single, direct ranking factor. It's a concept used by quality raters, and many signals in Google's systems align with it. Treat it as a guide to being trustworthy.

### Do small businesses need E-E-A-T?
Yes, and you can do it well. Real local experience, real photos, reviews and clear contact details are strong signals.

### What does the first E (Experience) add?
It rewards first-hand knowledge. A product review from someone who used it, or a guide from someone who did the job, is more useful than a summary of other pages.

### Can I show E-E-A-T without credentials?
Yes. Experience, results, reviews and transparency count, especially for topics where formal qualifications aren't required. Be honest about what you do and don't know.

### How long does it take to build authority?
It builds over months and years through consistent, accurate work and earned mentions. Start with the basics above and keep going.
