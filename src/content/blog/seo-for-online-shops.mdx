Running an online shop means competing with everyone. Big marketplaces, brand websites, social media sellers and the shop next door that just launched an Instagram page. Customers have endless choice, and most of them start the same way: by typing what they want into a search box.

If your products appear when they search, you get the sale. If they don't, someone else does. And the frustrating part is that many shops lose because of small, fixable things. A product page with the supplier's copied description. A title that says nothing. Photos that take forever to load. No price in the search result.

This guide explains SEO for online shops in plain language. You'll learn how to structure your site, how to build category and product pages that rank and convert, how to add structured data so prices and ratings can show in search, and how to avoid the problems that trip up shops. It works for small stores and large ones, and for shops anywhere, including Nigeria.

## Why shop SEO is different

A shop has some special challenges:

- **Lots of similar pages.** Dozens or thousands of products with similar descriptions.
- **Products change.** Items sell out, prices move, ranges are discontinued.
- **Filters and variations.** Colours, sizes and sort options can create thousands of near-duplicate addresses.
- **Intent is commercial.** Searchers are close to buying, so trust and clarity matter as much as rankings.

Google has a whole section of guidance for this at [ecommerce SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce). It's worth reading once you've covered the basics here.

## Step 1: Research what shoppers search

Product searches come in a few types:

- **Category searches:** "solar inverters", "women's sandals". Broad, high value.
- **Product searches:** "3.5kva solar inverter pure sine wave". Specific, ready to buy.
- **Comparison and advice searches:** "best inverter for a small shop". Early in the journey.
- **Brand and model searches:** people who know exactly what they want.

Use [keyword research](/blog/how-to-do-keyword-research/) to find the words your customers use, including local terms, and to decide which category and product pages deserve the most attention. Pay attention to the questions people ask, such as "does it work with solar panels?" You can answer those on the page.

## Step 2: Build a clear site structure

A simple structure helps shoppers and search engines.

- **Home page** links to your main categories.
- **Category pages** group related products.
- **Subcategories** where needed.
- **Product pages** sit inside categories.

A shopper should reach any product in three clicks or fewer. Use clear, readable addresses, such as `/shop/inverters/3-5kva-solar-inverter/`, and avoid long strings of numbers and symbols. Google's guidance on [designing URL structure for ecommerce sites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites) is helpful.

Add breadcrumbs, so visitors see the path (Home, Solar, Inverters), and so search engines understand your hierarchy.

## Step 3: Make your category pages work hard

Category pages are often your best chance to rank for broad, valuable searches. Many shops treat them as plain lists of products. Do better.

- **A clear title:** "Solar Inverters in Nigeria | Pure Sine Wave and Hybrid | Example Store".
- **A short, helpful introduction** above or below the products. Explain what the category includes and how to choose. Keep it natural and useful, not stuffed with keywords.
- **Good filters** for price, size, brand, power and so on.
- **Clear product cards** with photo, name, price and rating.
- **Links** to buying guides and popular subcategories.

Be careful with filters. They can create countless address variations that waste Google's attention. Keep filtered pages out of search where they add nothing, and use canonical tags to point variations to the main category page. See Google's guide to [consolidating duplicate URLs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls).

## Step 4: Write product pages that win trust

Each product page should answer every question a buyer has, so they don't leave to find the answer elsewhere.

![Screenshot: a product page with title, rating, price, stock status, bullet features, buttons and a specification table](/images/screenshots/product-page-seo-example.png)

*Figure 2: A strong product page shows the key facts clearly and makes buying easy.*

### Title and heading

Use the product name, with the key detail people search for: "3.5 kVA Solar Inverter, Pure Sine Wave | Example Store". Keep the main H1 clear and matching. Our guide to [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-and-meta-descriptions-that-get-clicks/) has templates for product pages.

### A unique description

Don't paste the supplier's description. Hundreds of other shops use it, and Google sees no reason to prefer yours. Write your own: what it is, who it's for, the main benefits, key specifications and how it compares with alternatives. Use plain language and short paragraphs. Our guide to [writing SEO content](/blog/how-to-write-seo-content/) helps.

### Specifications and details

Show size, power, materials, colours, warranty, compatibility and what's in the box. A clear table beats a wall of text.

### Price, stock and delivery

Show the price, whether it's in stock and how long delivery takes, including to major cities. Be clear about shipping costs and returns. Hidden costs cause abandoned baskets.

### Photos

Use several clear photos from different angles, plus zoom. Compress them so the page loads fast, and add descriptive alt text, such as "3.5 kVA pure sine wave solar inverter, front view".

### Reviews

Real customer reviews build trust and fresh content. Invite reviews after delivery, and never invent them. Show the rating and the number of reviews near the title.

### Easy ways to buy or ask

Add a clear "Add to cart" button and, for many Nigerian shoppers, a "Chat on WhatsApp" option for quick questions. Our guide to [local SEO for Nigerian businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-nigerian-businesses/) explains click-to-chat links.

### Related products and links

Link to accessories, alternatives and the parent category. It helps shoppers and spreads links through your site. See [internal linking](/blog/internal-linking-guide/).

## Step 5: Add Product structured data

Structured data tells search engines the details of your product: name, image, description, price, currency, availability and ratings. It can make your listing eligible for rich results, such as stars, price and "in stock" in search.

![Screenshot: a search result with star rating, price and in-stock status next to a plain result without them](/images/screenshots/product-rich-result-example.png)

*Figure 3: A rich result shows price, rating and availability before anyone clicks.*

Rules to follow:

- Use Google's guidance on [product structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product).
- Mark up only what's visible on the page.
- Keep price and availability accurate. Many platforms update them automatically.
- Use ratings only from genuine customer reviews.
- Test with the Rich Results Test.

Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) explains how to add and check it. Rich results aren't guaranteed, because Google decides what to show.

## Step 6: Consider free product listings

Google lets merchants share product data so their items can appear in free listings on shopping surfaces. You'll need to set up an account and a product feed, and follow the rules. Google's [Merchant Center help](https://support.google.com/merchants) explains the requirements. It's worth exploring once your product pages are accurate and your feed is clean.

## Step 7: Handle out-of-stock and discontinued products

Products come and go, and handling them well protects your SEO.

- **Temporarily out of stock:** keep the page, say when it will return and offer notifications or alternatives.
- **Permanently discontinued, with a close replacement:** redirect to the replacement with a 301 redirect.
- **Discontinued with no replacement:** redirect to the category page, or let the page return "not found" if it has no links or traffic.
- **Never let hundreds of dead pages build up**, since they frustrate customers and waste crawling.

## Step 8: Fix duplicate content from variations

Colours, sizes and tracking parameters can create many addresses for one product. Use these tools:

- **One page per product** where variations are choices on the same page, if you can.
- **Canonical tags** pointing variations to the main version.
- **Parameter control** so filters and tracking don't create indexable duplicates.

## Step 9: Make it fast and mobile-friendly

Most shoppers browse on phones. Slow, heavy product pages lose sales as well as rankings.

- Compress and resize images, and lazy-load those below the fold.
- Remove unneeded apps and scripts.
- Keep the checkout simple.
- Test everything on a real phone.

See [mobile SEO](/blog/mobile-seo-guide/) and [Core Web Vitals explained](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/).

## Step 10: Build trust signals

Online buyers worry about scams, delivery and returns. Show:

- a clear About page, with address and contact details,
- delivery and returns policies in plain language,
- secure payment options,
- real reviews and ratings,
- social proof such as photos from customers.

These are the same trust signals we describe in our guide to [E-E-A-T](/blog/eeat-explained-experience-expertise-trust/).

## Step 11: Create content that helps people buy

Buying guides, comparisons and how-to articles bring in shoppers early in their search, and link to your products.

- "How to choose an inverter for a small shop"
- "Inverter vs generator: which is cheaper to run?"
- "How to size a solar system"

Link from these guides to the relevant category and product pages. Our [content audit guide](/blog/how-to-do-a-content-audit/) shows how to keep this content healthy.

## Step 12: If you have a physical shop

Claim and complete your Google Business Profile, with photos, hours and products. Local shoppers often search "near me", and reviews help. See [Google Business Profile mistakes](/blog/google-business-profile-mistakes/) to avoid common errors.

## A 90-day plan for a shop

**Days 1 to 30:** Fix speed and mobile. Write unique titles and descriptions for your top 20 products and main categories. Add breadcrumbs. Set up tracking.

**Days 31 to 60:** Add product structured data. Improve category pages with helpful intros. Start collecting reviews. Fix duplicate and out-of-stock pages.

**Days 61 to 90:** Publish two buying guides. Build links from suppliers, partners and local sites. Review your data and improve pages with many impressions but few clicks.

## Mistakes online shops make

- **Copying supplier descriptions.**
- **Thin category pages with only a grid of products.**
- **Heavy, uncompressed photos.**
- **Hiding price, delivery or returns.**
- **Letting filters create endless duplicate pages.**
- **Deleting product pages without redirects.**
- **Faking reviews or ratings.**
- **Ignoring mobile checkout problems.**
- **Not tracking sales from search.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a blog for my online shop?
Not at first. Start with strong category and product pages. Buying guides can bring more traffic later.

### How many words should a product description have?
Enough to answer buyers' questions, usually 150 to 400 words for important products. Quality matters more than length.

### Should I use manufacturer descriptions?
Rewrite them in your own words, and add what customers really want to know.

### Do reviews help SEO?
They add fresh, useful content, build trust and can enable star ratings in search. Only use genuine reviews.

### What platform is best for SEO?
Most major platforms can do good SEO. What matters most is control over titles, addresses, structured data, speed and redirects.
