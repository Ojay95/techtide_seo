Picture a customer in Lekki who needs a generator repaired today. She doesn't ask a friend or scroll Instagram. She picks up her phone, searches "generator repair near me" and looks at the top three results on the map. She taps one, checks the photos and reviews, and sends a WhatsApp message. Whoever she picks gets the job.

That's local SEO. It's the work of making sure your business is one of those top three. For Nigerian businesses, it's often the cheapest, fastest way to win new customers, because people searching on a map are already ready to buy.

This guide shows how to do it properly in a Nigerian context: Google Maps, tricky addresses, reviews, WhatsApp and area pages. You don't need a big budget, but you do need to be consistent.

## What local SEO is, in plain words

Local SEO means helping your business show up when people search for something near them or in a named place. It covers:

- **Google Maps and the "map pack":** the three businesses Google shows with a map for local searches.
- **Your Google Business Profile:** your free listing, with your details, photos and reviews.
- **Your website:** pages about your services and the areas you serve.
- **Reviews and mentions:** what customers and other sites say about you.

Google says it ranks local results using three ideas: relevance, distance and prominence. Its guide on [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) explains them. You can't change how close you are to a searcher, but you can improve how relevant and well known you appear.

## Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

This is the highest-value step. If you do nothing else in this guide, do this one.

1. Search for your business on Google Maps. Claim the listing if it exists, or create one at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com).
2. Complete Google's verification. It may involve a postcard, phone, video or other method depending on your situation.
3. Choose the **primary category** carefully. It's one of the most important signals. Pick the most specific option, such as "Generator repair service" rather than just "Service".
4. Add secondary categories for other services you offer.
5. Add your phone number, website, opening hours and a clear description.
6. List each service, with a short description.
7. Upload real photos of your premises, team and work.

Our [Google Business Profile guide](/guides/google-business-profile-optimization/) walks through every field, and Google's [Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business) covers the rules.

## Step 2: Handle your address properly

Addresses can be a real headache in Nigerian cities, where streets may be hard to find and landmarks matter more than house numbers. Here's how to deal with it.

**Be consistent.** Use the same address format everywhere: your profile, your website and your directory listings. Don't write "Opp. First Bank" in one place and a full street address in another.

**Add a landmark to your description and website.** Something like "near the Lekki Phase 1 gate, opposite the pharmacy" helps customers on the day. Put it in your profile description, on your contact page and in your website footer.

**Use a Plus Code if you need one.** Google's [Plus Codes](https://maps.google.com/pluscodes/) are short codes that point to an exact spot on the map, even where street addresses are unclear. They're helpful for customers who navigate by phone.

**Place your map pin accurately.** Zoom in and make sure the pin sits on your actual entrance, not nearby.

**If you visit customers instead of receiving them,** set your profile up as a service-area business and hide the street address. Google only lets you show an address if customers can meet you there in person. Never use a virtual office or a friend's address.

## Step 3: Build pages that match how people search

People search with area names: "wedding planner in Ikeja", "laptop repair in Wuse 2", "dentist in Port Harcourt". If your website never mentions those places, you won't appear for them.

**One page per main service.** Instead of one long "Services" page, create a page for each thing you do. Include what it involves, a price range, photos and how to contact you.

**Add area pages where it makes sense.** If you serve several areas, create a page for each, such as "Generator repair in Ikeja" and "Generator repair in Surulere". Each should have unique, useful content, such as local examples, photos, reviews and directions. Don't copy one page and swap the area name. Google sees that as thin content.

**Show your prices.** Even a range like "Generator servicing from ₦25,000 to ₦60,000, depending on size" builds trust and answers a question most people have. (Use your real numbers.)

**Make it fast on mobile.** Most visitors arrive on a phone, often on mobile data. Compress your images and test your pages with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). See [Core Web Vitals explained](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/).

## Step 4: Make WhatsApp your best contact channel

For many Nigerian customers, WhatsApp is the easiest way to ask a question. If your website makes people hunt for a phone number or fill in a long form, you'll lose them.

- Add a **click-to-chat button** on every page, especially on mobile. WhatsApp supports links in the form `https://wa.me/234XXXXXXXXXX`, using your number in international format without the plus or leading zero.
- Add a **pre-filled message**, such as "Hi, I'd like a quote for generator repair", so people don't stare at a blank chat.
- Use the free [WhatsApp Business app](https://business.whatsapp.com/) to set up a profile, catalogue, quick replies and away messages.
- **Reply quickly.** Local customers often message several businesses at once. The first helpful reply often wins.

## Step 5: Collect reviews the right way

Reviews are social proof at the exact moment a customer decides. They also influence your ranking, because Google lists positive, recent reviews among the factors for local results.

1. Open your profile and copy your direct review link.
2. After every job, send it on WhatsApp with a short message: "Thank you for choosing us. If you were happy, a quick Google review helps others find us: [link]".
3. Reply to every review, kindly and briefly. Thank positive reviewers and answer negative ones calmly, offering to resolve the problem.
4. Never buy reviews, offer rewards for them or pick who you ask. Google's rules forbid it.

## Step 6: Keep your details identical everywhere

Search engines compare your business name, address and phone number across the web. When they match, trust grows. When they don't, you create doubt.

- Use one format for your name, address and phone number, with the +234 country code.
- Claim your listings on the main directories and the ones relevant to your industry, and remove duplicates.
- Update everything when you move, change number or rebrand.

## Step 7: Earn local mentions and links

Links and mentions from local sources show you're a real, established business. Think about:

- suppliers and partners who can list you,
- chambers of commerce, trade associations and business groups,
- local event sponsorships,
- community and school partnerships,
- local news or blog features.

Quality beats quantity. A handful of relevant, local links is worth more than hundreds of random ones. See [how to get backlinks the safe way](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/).

## Step 8: Post and update your profile regularly

An active profile signals a business that's open and engaged.

- Add new photos every week or two.
- Post offers, news and events.
- Keep opening hours up to date, including holidays.
- Add new services as you start offering them.
- Answer questions customers ask on your profile.

## Common mistakes Nigerian businesses make

- **No Google Business Profile at all.** You're invisible on the map.
- **Keyword stuffing the business name.** Adding "Best Cheap Plumber Lagos" to your name can get your profile suspended.
- **Using a fake or shared address.** It breaks Google's rules and risks removal.
- **Ignoring reviews.** No replies, or no reviews, looks inactive.
- **A slow, heavy website.** Customers leave before it loads.
- **Hiding the phone and WhatsApp contact.** Make it effortless.
- **Duplicate listings.** Old and new profiles split your reviews and confuse customers.
- **One page for every area and service.** Search engines can't match it to specific searches.

## A 30-day local SEO plan

**Week 1:** Claim and verify your Google Business Profile. Choose the right categories. Add your services, hours and photos.

**Week 2:** Fix your website's mobile speed. Add a WhatsApp button and your phone number to every page. Create a page for each main service.

**Week 3:** Start asking every customer for a review. Reply to any existing reviews. Search for and fix duplicate or wrong listings.

**Week 4:** Create one or two area pages with useful local content. Ask a supplier or local group to link to you. Record your starting numbers: calls, WhatsApp messages and profile views.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does local SEO take to work?
Profile improvements can show in a few weeks. Competing for the top three in a busy area, such as central Lagos, usually takes a few months of steady work.

### Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?
A website gives you more control, more search visibility and a place to build trust. A profile alone works, but you'll do better with both.

### Can I rank in several cities?
You can serve several areas, but Google ranks you mainly where your business is located. For other cities, create strong area pages and, if you have real branches, separate profiles for each.

### Should I use a Nigerian phone number or WhatsApp number?
Use the number customers can actually reach you on, in international format, and keep it identical everywhere. A landline-style number can add trust if you answer it.
