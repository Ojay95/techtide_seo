Hiring an SEO agency can feel like a leap of faith. You're handing over part of your marketing to people who speak in jargon, promise big results and ask you to trust the process. Some agencies are excellent. Others sell hope, send confusing reports and disappear when results don't arrive.

The difference usually shows up before you sign anything. If you know what to ask and what to watch for, you can tell the good from the bad in a few conversations.

This guide gives you 12 practical questions, the red flags that should make you walk away and a simple way to compare agencies side by side. You don't need to know anything technical. You just need to be a careful buyer.

## First, do you need an agency at all?

Not everyone does. If you have a small site, a few hours a week and the patience to learn, you can do a lot yourself. Google's own advice on [whether you need an SEO expert](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-you-need-seo) is a balanced read, and it's honest that good SEOs can help but you can also do a lot on your own.

An agency makes sense when:

- you don't have the time to stay consistent,
- your market is competitive,
- your website has technical problems you can't fix,
- you want a clear plan, regular reporting and someone accountable.

If that sounds like you, keep reading.

## Before you talk to anyone: know your goal

Agencies are easier to judge when you're clear about what you want. Write down:

1. **What counts as success.** More phone calls? Online bookings? Leads from a particular city or country?
2. **What a customer is worth.** Even a rough number helps you judge a price.
3. **Your budget range.** Be honest. A good agency will tell you what's realistic.
4. **Your timeline.** SEO takes months, not days. Read [how long SEO takes](/guides/how-long-does-seo-take/) so your expectations are realistic.

With that in hand, you can have a useful conversation instead of a sales pitch.

## The 12 questions to ask

### 1. What exactly will you do in the first three months?

You want specifics: an audit, technical fixes, keyword research, new or improved pages, a review plan, link outreach. A strong answer reads like a plan with milestones. A weak one sounds like "we'll optimise your site".

### 2. Who will actually work on my account?

Big agencies sometimes sell you with senior people and hand the work to juniors. Ask who your day-to-day contact is, how experienced they are and how many clients they handle at once.

### 3. How will you report, and how often?

Ask to see a sample report. It should focus on outcomes such as calls, leads and bookings, not only rankings and traffic. It should explain what happened and what comes next in plain language. Our [how we work page](/how-we-work/) shows the kind of monthly reporting to expect.

### 4. What results are realistic, and when?

Beware of anyone who names an exact ranking or date. A good answer includes ranges, depends on your market and acknowledges uncertainty. Local improvements can show in weeks, and competitive searches often take months.

### 5. Can I see examples and speak to clients?

Ask for case studies with real numbers and a couple of references. Newer agencies may have fewer, and that's fine if they're honest about it. They can show their own website's results, or a pilot project with a clear before and after.

### 6. How do you build links?

This question reveals a lot. Good answers mention outreach, digital PR, partnerships, useful content and local mentions. Alarming answers include buying links, "private networks" or promises of hundreds of links a month. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) treat link schemes as a violation, and penalties can take months to fix.

### 7. Who owns the accounts, content and data?

You should own your website, analytics, Search Console, Google Business Profile and any content created for you. Never let an agency set these up under its own login only. If you leave, everything should leave with you.

### 8. How do you use AI?

It's a fair question now. A good agency uses AI to speed up research and drafts but keeps experts in charge of strategy, editing and fact-checking. Ask how they make sure content is accurate and original. Our guide on [using AI for SEO without hurting your rankings](/blog/how-to-use-ai-for-seo-content/) explains what good practice looks like.

### 9. What do you need from me?

SEO works best as a partnership. Expect requests for access, product information, photos, approvals and sometimes quotes or case studies. If an agency says it needs nothing from you, be careful. It may be planning generic work.

### 10. What does the contract say?

Check the minimum term, notice period, what's included, what costs extra and what happens to your work if you leave. A three-month minimum is common, because results build slowly. Very long lock-ins with heavy penalties are a warning sign.

### 11. How do you price your work, and why?

Whether it's a monthly retainer, a project fee or hourly, you should understand what you're paying for. Compare scope, not only the number. Our guide on [how much SEO costs](/guides/how-much-does-seo-cost/) explains typical models.

### 12. What happens if it isn't working?

A confident agency has a clear answer: they review the data, adjust the plan and discuss it openly. A poor one blames Google, your website or "the algorithm" without evidence.

## Red flags that should stop you

- **Guaranteed rankings or "page one in 30 days".** Nobody controls Google. Honest agencies promise work and reporting, not positions.
- **Secret methods.** Good SEO isn't a secret. If they won't explain what they're doing, you can't judge it.
- **Cheap bulk packages.** Offers like "1,000 backlinks for a small monthly fee" usually mean low-quality links that can harm your site.
- **High-pressure sales.** "Sign today or the price goes up" is a sales tactic, not a service.
- **Reports full of jargon and no business results.** If you can't tell whether it's working, neither can they.
- **Taking over your accounts.** You should always have full access.
- **No questions about your business.** A good agency wants to understand your customers before proposing anything.

## Green flags that are worth a lot

- They start with an audit and show you what they found.
- They talk about your goals and customers first, tools second.
- They explain trade-offs and admit what they don't know.
- They give you access to everything, including dashboards.
- Their own site is clear, fast and well written, and it ranks for something.
- They're happy to start small, with a pilot or a defined first project.

## A simple scorecard

When you've spoken to two or three agencies, score each from 1 to 5 on these points:

| Area | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Clarity of plan | Specific months-one-to-three actions |
| Transparency | Open about methods, reports and costs |
| Relevant experience | Similar businesses or markets |
| Communication | Quick, clear and honest answers |
| Reporting | Focus on leads and sales, not vanity metrics |
| Fit and trust | Do you feel comfortable working with them? |

The highest total isn't automatic. Trust your judgement about the person too.

## Choosing an agency in Nigeria or abroad

If you're based in Nigeria, you may be deciding between a local agency and one overseas. Think about:

- **Time zones and communication.** Shared working hours make calls and approvals easier.
- **Market knowledge.** Local agencies often understand how Nigerians search, including mobile-first habits, WhatsApp and area-based searches. Our guide to [SEO in Nigeria](/blog/seo-in-nigeria-practical-guide/) explains what makes the market different.
- **Payment.** Check the currency, payment methods and any fees.
- **Your customers.** If you want customers abroad, look for experience with international pages and markets.

Neither is automatically better. Ask the same 12 questions of both.

## Start small if you can

If you're unsure, begin with a paid audit or a three-month trial. You'll see how the agency communicates, how clear their findings are and how well they deliver, before you commit for a year. It's the safest way to learn whether you're a good fit.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I expect to pay?
It depends on your market and scope. Plans from reputable agencies vary widely, so compare what's included, not just the price. Our plans start from $500 per month.

### Is a cheap agency always bad?
Not always, but be extra careful about what's included and how links are built. Very low prices often hide poor-quality work.

### How long should I commit for?
Three to six months is common, because results build gradually. Avoid long contracts with heavy penalties unless you're very confident in the agency.

### Can I switch agencies later?
Yes, if you own your accounts and content. That's why question 7 matters so much.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?
A good freelancer can be excellent for smaller projects. An agency offers more skills under one roof and continuity if someone's away. Judge each by the same questions.
