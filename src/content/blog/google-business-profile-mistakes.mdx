Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees. Before your website, before your Instagram, before anything you've written. It shows your name, your photos, your rating, whether you're open and a button to call. If the details are wrong or missing, you lose the customer in about three seconds.

The frustrating part is that most profile problems are small and easy to fix. A wrong category. A phone number from three years ago. A name with a few extra words added to "help with ranking". None of them take long to correct, but each one can quietly cost you calls every week.

This guide walks through the 12 mistakes we see most often, explains why each one hurts and shows how to fix it. There's also a section on what to do if your profile gets suspended, and a quick audit checklist you can run in 15 minutes.

## Why your profile matters so much

For local searches such as "generator repair near me" or "dentist in Wuse", Google shows a map with a few businesses at the top. These come from Business Profiles. Google says it ranks them using relevance, distance and prominence, and you can read how in its guide to [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091).

You can't change how far you are from the searcher. But you can improve relevance and prominence, and that starts with a complete, accurate, honest profile. Our [complete local SEO guide](/guides/local-seo-guide/) covers the wider picture.

## Mistake 1: Adding keywords to your business name

**The problem:** Changing "Ade & Sons Generator Repairs" to "Ade & Sons Best Cheap Generator Repair Lagos" might seem clever. It isn't. Google's guidelines say your name should reflect your real-world business name, the one on your signage, website and invoices.

**Why it hurts:** Keyword stuffing can get your profile edited, suspended or removed. It also looks spammy to customers.

**The fix:** Use your real, everyday business name. Put your services in the categories, services list and description instead.

## Mistake 2: Choosing the wrong primary category

**The problem:** A vague category like "Service" or "Company", or one that doesn't match what you mostly do.

**Why it hurts:** The primary category is one of the strongest signals Google uses to decide which searches you're relevant for.

**The fix:** Pick the most specific category that describes your main service, then add secondary categories for other things you offer. Look at what the top-ranking competitors use for ideas.

## Mistake 3: Using a fake or shared address

**The problem:** Listing a virtual office, a PO box, a friend's house or a co-working desk you don't staff.

**Why it hurts:** Google requires an address where you can meet customers during stated hours, or a service-area setup if you travel to them. Fake addresses are a common cause of suspension. Google explains the rules in its [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

**The fix:** If customers visit you, use your real address and add a landmark in the description. If you go to customers, set up a service-area business and hide the street address.

## Mistake 4: Leaving your profile unverified

**The problem:** An unverified profile can't use many features, and it may not appear in results.

**The fix:** Complete verification, whether by video, phone, email or another method Google offers. Keep an eye on your notifications if Google asks for more evidence.

## Mistake 5: Having duplicate listings

**The problem:** Two or more profiles for the same business, often because of a move, a rebrand or a former manager creating a second one.

**Why it hurts:** Reviews and signals are split. Customers may call an old number or visit an old address.

**The fix:** Search for your business name, old names and old addresses on Maps. Claim any duplicates you own, then merge or remove them using Google's tools. Mark old locations as closed only if they really are.

## Mistake 6: Inconsistent name, address and phone number

**The problem:** One version of your details on Google, another on Facebook, a third in a directory.

**Why it hurts:** Search engines compare these details. Contradictions create doubt.

**The fix:** Write down one official version of your name, address, phone number and website, then update everywhere to match. Our guide to [local citations](/blog/local-citations-explained/) shows how.

## Mistake 7: Wrong or missing opening hours

**The problem:** Hours that haven't been updated, no holiday hours or no special hours during events and festive periods.

**Why it hurts:** A customer who arrives at a closed door rarely returns, and may leave a bad review.

**The fix:** Check your hours today. Add special hours for public holidays and any change in schedule. If you answer calls after hours, say so in your description.

## Mistake 8: Not listing services, products and attributes

**The problem:** An empty services section, or services written only in the description.

**Why it hurts:** Services and attributes help Google match you to specific searches, and help customers see exactly what you offer.

**The fix:** Add every service with a short description, and prices or ranges where it makes sense. Fill in attributes such as "wheelchair accessible", "online appointments" or "women-owned" only if they're true.

## Mistake 9: Weak photos, or none at all

**The problem:** No photos, one blurry logo, or stock images that aren't your business.

**Why it hurts:** Photos build trust. Customers want to see your premises, team and work.

**The fix:** Upload clear, real photos: the outside (so people can recognise it), the inside, your team and examples of your work. Add new ones regularly. Make sure they're well lit and show real detail.

## Mistake 10: Ignoring reviews

**The problem:** Few reviews, no replies, or an angry reply to a bad one.

**Why it hurts:** Reviews influence both ranking and decisions. A profile with no replies looks abandoned.

**The fix:** Ask every happy customer with your direct link, reply to every review within a couple of days and handle criticism calmly. Never buy reviews, offer rewards for them or ask only happy customers. Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) has templates.

## Mistake 11: Never posting or updating

**The problem:** A profile that looks frozen in time.

**The fix:** Add a short update, offer or event every week or two, with a clear photo. Keep adding new services and photos. It signals an active business.

## Mistake 12: Wrong website link, no tracking and call-tracking number confusion

**The problem:** A website link that goes to the home page instead of the best page, an outdated address, or a tracking phone number used as the main number.

**The fix:** Link to the page that best serves most customers. Add a simple tracking parameter, so you can see visits from your profile in analytics. If you use call tracking, keep your real number as the primary one, and use the tracking number only as an additional number.

## Bonus: Not looking at your insights

Your profile shows how many people viewed it, called, asked for directions and clicked your website. If you never look, you can't see what's working.

## What to do if your profile is suspended

A suspension is stressful, but it's usually fixable.

1. **Don't create a new profile.** That can make things worse.
2. **Find the reason.** Google usually tells you what was wrong, such as a guideline violation or missing information.
3. **Fix the problem.** Correct the name, address, category or whatever was flagged.
4. **Submit a reinstatement request** through the Business Profile Help tools, with clear evidence such as photos of your premises and signage, a business licence and utility bills if asked.
5. **Be patient.** Reviews can take days. Avoid repeated submissions.

Google's [Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business) explains the process and what evidence is accepted.

## A 15-minute profile audit

Open your profile and check:

- Is the name exactly your real business name?
- Is the primary category specific, with relevant secondary ones?
- Are the address, pin and service area accurate?
- Are the phone number, website and hours correct, including holidays?
- Are services, attributes and description complete?
- Are there at least 10 recent, real photos?
- Have you replied to every review?
- Have you posted or updated something in the last month?
- Is there only one listing for your business?
- Have you looked at your performance numbers this month?

Fix whatever is missing, and note the date. Repeat monthly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why isn't my business showing on Google Maps?
Common reasons are an unverified profile, a wrong or vague category, a suspended listing or a pin placed in the wrong spot. Check each one.

### Can I rank without a website?
Yes, to a degree. But a website with good service pages gives you more chances to be found and more trust. Both together work best.

### How long do profile changes take to appear?
Some changes show within minutes, others within days. Edits to sensitive details, such as name or address, may be reviewed first.

### Should I hide my address?
Only if you serve customers at their location and don't have a place for them to visit. If customers come to you, show the address.

### Can competitors report my profile?
Anyone can suggest edits or report problems. That's another reason to keep everything accurate and follow the guidelines.
