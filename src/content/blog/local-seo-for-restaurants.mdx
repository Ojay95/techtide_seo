It's a Friday evening. A group of friends in Ikeja can't decide where to eat. Someone takes out a phone and types "good jollof near me". The screen fills with three restaurants on a map, each with a photo, a star rating and an "Open now" label. Thirty seconds later, the group has chosen one, and the other two restaurants never knew they were in the running.

That's how most restaurant decisions are made now. People don't flick through a printed directory or wait for a recommendation. They search, glance at the top few results, check the photos and reviews, and go. If your restaurant isn't in those top few, or it is but the photos are dark, the hours are wrong and the menu is a blurry picture, you lose the table.

The encouraging part is that restaurant SEO is more practical than technical. Most of it is about showing people what they're about to eat, proving that other people liked it and making it easy to book or order. Done well, it brings in customers who are already hungry and nearby, which is the best kind.

This guide is a complete plan for restaurants, cafés, bars, lounges and bakeries. It's written for owners and managers, in plain language, with steps you can follow this week. There's a 60-day plan at the end.

## How people search for restaurants

Restaurant searches are different from most businesses, because they're driven by mood, time and place. Here are the main types:

| What they type | What they want | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| "restaurants near me" | Anything good, close and open now | A complete, well-rated Google profile |
| "jollof rice in Ikeja" | A specific dish in a specific area | Menu text and photos that mention the dish and area |
| "brunch spot Lekki" | A particular kind of meal | Clear categories, photos and a menu page |
| "restaurant for birthday dinner" | A place for an occasion | An events or private dining page |
| "[your restaurant name]" | To check hours, menu or book | A correct profile and an easy website |
| "open now" or "open late" | A place that's actually open | Accurate hours, including holidays |
| "restaurants with parking" | A practical detail | Attributes and a clear page |

Most of these searches happen on a phone, often on mobile data, often in the last hour before eating. So everything has to be quick, clear and correct on a small screen.

Google ranks local results using relevance, distance and prominence, which it explains in its guide to [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). You can't change how far away a customer is, but you can improve how relevant and well known you look. That starts with your Google profile.

## Step 1: Build a complete Google Business Profile

For a restaurant, your Google Business Profile is the most important page on the internet. It's what people see first. Claim it, verify it and complete every section.

![Screenshot: a restaurant profile with photos, rating, attributes, opening hours, action buttons, popular dishes and popular times](/images/screenshots/restaurant-google-profile-example.png)

*Figure 2: A complete restaurant profile answers the main questions before anyone visits your website.*

### Choose the right categories

Your **primary category** tells Google what you mainly are. Be as specific as you can: "Nigerian restaurant" is better than "Restaurant". Then add **secondary categories** for what else you do, such as "Bar", "Takeaway", "Caterer" or "Event venue". Look at what popular competitors use.

### Fill in the attributes

Attributes describe your service and setting. Tick the ones that are true: dine-in, takeaway, delivery, outdoor seating, live music, accepts reservations, wheelchair accessible, parking, Wi-Fi, good for groups, family-friendly. They help Google match you to specific searches and help people decide.

### Get your name right

Use your real business name, the one on your signage. Don't stuff keywords into it, such as "Mama Titi Best Jollof Rice Lagos". That can get your profile suspended. Our guide on [Business Profile mistakes](/blog/google-business-profile-mistakes/) explains.

### Keep hours exactly right

Nothing frustrates a hungry customer like arriving at a closed door. So:

- Set your **regular hours**, including late-night hours for bars and lounges.
- Add **special hours** for public holidays and events: Christmas, New Year's Eve, Eid, Easter, Valentine's Day and any private bookings.
- If your **kitchen closes earlier** than the bar, say so in your description.
- Update hours **on the day** if something changes.

### Add your menu, prices and dishes

Where Google offers menu and dish features in your country, use them. If it doesn't, add a link to your online menu. Make sure the menu on your profile, your website and any delivery apps tell the same story, with the same dish names and similar prices.

### Add booking and ordering links

Link to your reservation page and your online ordering page if you have them. Where Google offers reservation and order buttons through partners, connect them. For many Nigerian restaurants, a **WhatsApp ordering link** is the easiest option. Add it to your website and mention it in your description.

### Write a description that sells the experience

You have 750 characters. Use them to say what you serve, what makes you different, where you are and how to order. Write it like a person: "Smoky party jollof, ofada rice and grilled fish in the heart of Ikeja. Dine in, take away or order on WhatsApp. Free parking."

### Use posts and updates

Share new dishes, offers, events and holiday menus. A short post with a good photo every week keeps the profile active.

### A note for delivery-only and cloud kitchens

Rules for delivery-only kitchens differ from regular restaurants, since customers can't visit. Read Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) before you create a profile, and don't use a fake address.

## Step 2: Photos that make people hungry

Photos are the single biggest influence on whether someone chooses you. Dark, blurry or generic photos cost you customers. Plan a simple shot list and refresh it every few months.

**Food (the most important):**
- Your top ten dishes, photographed in natural light, from above and at an angle.
- Close-ups that show texture and steam.
- Full tables, to show portion sizes and sharing platters.

**Place:**
- The exterior, including the sign and entrance, so people recognise it.
- The dining room at different times of day.
- The bar, outdoor area, private dining rooms and any view.

**People:**
- The chef and team at work.
- Happy guests, with permission.

**Menu:**
- A clear photo of the printed menu, as a backup.

Use real photos of your own food, never stock images. Add new ones every few weeks, and tell customers they can upload their own. Respond to and thank people who do.

## Step 3: Make your website work for hungry people

Your website doesn't have to be fancy. It has to be fast, clear and easy on a phone. Our guide to [mobile SEO](/blog/mobile-seo-guide/) explains what that means.

### Put your menu on the page as real text

This is the most common restaurant website mistake. Many restaurants upload a PDF or a photo of the menu. It looks fine to a person with a big screen, but:

- search engines can't read it well, so your dishes don't help you rank,
- it loads slowly on mobile data,
- it's hard to read on a small screen,
- it's often out of date.

![Diagram: a menu written as page text compared with a menu uploaded as a PDF or photo only](/images/diagrams/restaurant-menu-text-vs-pdf.svg)

*Figure 3: Put your menu on the page as text. Keep a PDF only as an extra.*

Instead, create a **menu page with real text**: dish names, short descriptions and prices, grouped by section. Add a link to order or book.

![Screenshot: a restaurant menu page with dishes, descriptions, prices, WhatsApp order links, address and a booking button](/images/screenshots/restaurant-menu-page-seo-example.png)

*Figure 4: A menu page written as text, with prices, an order link and a booking button.*

Write the descriptions the way people search: "Party jollof rice with chicken", "Ofada rice and ayamase", "Egusi soup with pounded yam". These words match real searches.

### Pages every restaurant should have

1. **Home page:** who you are, your cuisine, your area, photos and a clear way to book or order.
2. **Menu page:** as above, with a separate section for drinks and any special menus.
3. **Contact and location page:** address, map, landmarks, parking, phone and WhatsApp, and hours. See our guide to [local SEO for Nigerian businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-nigerian-businesses/) for tips on addresses and landmarks.
4. **Book a table or reservations page.**
5. **Events, private dining and catering page:** many people search for birthday venues, wedding receptions and corporate lunches. Give them details, capacity, packages and photos.
6. **About page:** your story, your chef, where ingredients come from. People like to know who feeds them.
7. **Gallery:** your best photos, compressed so they load quickly.
8. **FAQ:** parking, dress code, halal options, kids' menu, accessibility and so on.

### Write unique titles and descriptions

Each page needs its own title and description. For example: "Menu | Mama Titi Kitchen, Ikeja" and "Nigerian dishes, soups and grills with prices. Order on WhatsApp or book a table in Ikeja." Our guide to [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-and-meta-descriptions-that-get-clicks/) has templates.

### Make it fast

Restaurant sites are full of big photos. Compress and resize them, use modern formats and avoid autoplay videos. Test on a real phone with weak data. See [Core Web Vitals explained](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/).

## Step 4: Add restaurant structured data

Structured data helps search engines understand your restaurant. Schema.org has a **Restaurant** type with fields for cuisine served, price range, opening hours, menu link, reservations and address. Add it to your home page and contact page, and make sure it matches what's visible.

Google's [local business structured data guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) explains the fields, and our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) shows how to add and test it. Only mark up information that's true and visible on the page.

## Step 5: Reviews are your most powerful tool

Diners read reviews more than almost any other customers. Both the number and the freshness matter. A restaurant with 300 recent reviews looks safe. A restaurant with 12 old ones looks risky.

### How to get more reviews

1. **Make it easy.** Put a QR code linking to your review page on the bill folder, the table or the receipt.
2. **Ask at the right moment.** When a guest compliments the food, or when you send the receipt or follow-up message.
3. **Train the team.** Waiters can say, "If you enjoyed it, we'd love a quick Google review."
4. **Follow up on WhatsApp** for orders and reservations, with a direct link.
5. **Never buy reviews,** offer discounts only for positive ones or ask only happy guests. It breaks the rules and can get reviews removed.

Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) has message templates you can copy.

### How to reply

- **Reply to every review,** within a day or two.
- **Thank people by name,** and mention something specific they said.
- **Stay calm with complaints.** Apologise for what went wrong, say what you'll do and offer to continue offline.
- **Never argue,** and never reveal private details.
- **Let natural keywords appear.** A reply such as "Thank you for choosing our jollof and suya platter" helps people and search engines, as long as it sounds human.

### Learn from them

Read your reviews every week. They tell you which dishes people love, where service is slow and what to fix. Fix the repeated complaints. That's the quickest way to improve both your food and your rating.

## Step 6: Handle delivery apps and direct orders

Delivery apps bring customers, but they take a commission and own the relationship. Many restaurants use them for reach, then try to build direct orders.

- **Keep menus and prices consistent** across the apps, your website and your Google profile.
- **List your restaurant under one consistent name** on every platform.
- **Promote direct ordering,** such as WhatsApp, a website order form or a phone line, with a small benefit: a free drink, a quicker pickup or a loyalty reward.
- **Put your own order link** on your Google profile and website.
- **Include a leaflet or card in each delivery** that tells customers how to order direct next time.
- **Watch for duplicate or outdated listings** on apps and directories. If your old address or number is still online, customers get lost. Our guide to [changing your business details](/blog/how-to-change-business-details-without-losing-rankings/) shows how to find and fix them.

## Step 7: Create local content that brings people in

You don't need a big blog. A few well-chosen pages can bring in people who haven't picked a place yet.

**Guides that match real searches:**
- "Where to eat in Ikeja" or "best places for brunch in Lekki", with an honest mix of places, including yours.
- "How to plan a birthday dinner for 20."
- "Our menu for Christmas and New Year."
- "Private dining and event hosting at [your restaurant]."

**Seasonal pages and posts:**
- December and festive bookings,
- Valentine's Day,
- Easter and Eid menus,
- Ramadan and iftar offers, if relevant,
- graduations, anniversaries and corporate end-of-year lunches.

Publish them a few weeks before demand rises, and keep them up to date. Our guide to [writing SEO content](/blog/how-to-write-seo-content/) shows how to write pages people want to read.

## Step 8: Earn local mentions and links

Mentions from other sites show you're a real, respected business.

- **Food bloggers and local influencers:** invite them to taste the menu, and let them be honest.
- **Event planners, wedding vendors and venues:** exchange links and recommendations.
- **Hotels and offices nearby:** offer a concierge or corporate partnership.
- **Local news, lifestyle and "best of" lists:** pitch new menus, events or a story about your chef.
- **Suppliers and farms:** ask them to feature you as a customer.
- **Community events and charities:** sponsor or cook for local causes.

Quality and relevance beat quantity. Our guide to [safe link building](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/) explains how.

## Step 9: Measure what fills tables

A full dining room is the result you care about, but you can measure the steps that lead to it.

## Different kinds of restaurants, different priorities

**Casual and quick-service restaurants:** focus on location, hours, photos, delivery and reviews. Volume matters.

**Fine dining:** focus on photography, the story, reservations and press mentions. A few excellent reviews and features matter more than volume.

**Bars and lounges:** keep late-night hours perfect, and show atmosphere, events and security information. Be careful with accuracy about opening times.

**Cafés and bakeries:** early-morning searches, "coffee near me", and pre-orders for cakes and events. Photos and a simple order flow help.

**Caterers and event venues:** your website matters more, with packages, sample menus, galleries and enquiry forms.

**Hotel restaurants:** use your own page and profile, and link them with the hotel. See our guide to [SEO for hotels](/blog/seo-for-hotels/).

**Restaurants with several branches:** each needs its own profile, page and review routine. See [local SEO for businesses with several branches](/blog/local-seo-for-multiple-locations/).

## Common restaurant SEO mistakes

- **A menu that's only a PDF or a photo.**
- **Wrong or outdated opening hours,** especially on holidays.
- **Few photos, or dark and blurry ones.**
- **No replies to reviews.**
- **Different menus and prices** on the profile, website and delivery apps.
- **Keywords stuffed into the business name.**
- **Hiding the phone number or WhatsApp.**
- **No page for events or private dining.**
- **Duplicate listings** from a move or rebrand.
- **A slow website with huge images.**
- **Not tracking** bookings and orders from search.

## A 60-day restaurant plan

**Days 1 to 7: foundation**
- Claim and verify your Google profile, and check the name, categories and hours.
- Upload at least 20 fresh photos.
- Add your WhatsApp ordering link and booking link.
- Make sure there's only one listing for your restaurant.

**Days 8 to 21: website**
- Build a real-text menu page, with a clear order and booking button.
- Write unique titles and descriptions for each page.
- Add a contact page with landmarks, parking and a map.
- Compress images and test on a phone.

**Days 22 to 40: reviews and trust**
- Add review QR codes at tables and on receipts.
- Train the team to ask.
- Reply to every existing review.
- Add Restaurant structured data.

**Days 41 to 60: grow**
- Publish an events and private dining page.
- Publish one local guide or a seasonal menu page.
- Invite two local food bloggers or partners.
- Review your numbers, and decide what to improve next.

Then repeat monthly. Keep photos, hours and the menu fresh, and keep asking for reviews.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can my restaurant appear in the top three on Google Maps?
Complete and verify your profile, choose precise categories, keep your details accurate, add lots of real photos and collect fresh reviews steadily. Distance still matters, so no one can guarantee a position.

### Do I need a website if I have a Google profile?
You can start without one, but a simple website with a real-text menu, bookings and your story builds trust and helps you rank for more searches.

### Should I put my menu on Google as a PDF?
Use a real-text menu page as your main menu, and link to it. A PDF can be an extra, but it shouldn't be the only version.

### How many reviews does a restaurant need?
There's no fixed number. Aim to match or beat the best nearby competitors, and keep a steady flow of new reviews.

### Should I use delivery apps?
They can bring reach and orders. Use them alongside your own direct ordering, keep the details consistent and encourage repeat customers to order direct.

### How long does restaurant SEO take?
Profile improvements can show in a few weeks. Competing in busy areas, such as central Lagos, usually takes a few months of steady work.

### Can I do all this myself?
Most of it, yes. A specialist can help with the website, structured data, content and ongoing reporting.
