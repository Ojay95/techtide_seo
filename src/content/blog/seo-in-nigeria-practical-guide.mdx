If you run a business in Nigeria, you probably already get customers through WhatsApp, Instagram and word of mouth. That works, until it doesn't. A competitor opens down the road, a platform changes its rules, or your best referrer moves away. Then you realise how much of your business depends on places you don't control.

Search is different. When someone types "wedding planner in Lekki" or "best laptop repair in Abuja" into Google, they aren't browsing. They're ready to choose. If your business shows up with the right information, you get the call. If it doesn't, someone else does.

This guide explains SEO (search engine optimisation) in plain language for Nigerian businesses. You'll learn how customers really search, what matters most, a simple 90-day plan and the mistakes that waste money. You don't need to be technical to follow it.

## What SEO is, in one minute

SEO is the work of helping search engines understand your business and show it to people who are looking for what you sell. It has a few parts:

- Your **website** has to load fast and make sense on a phone.
- Your **pages** have to answer what people actually search.
- Your **Google Business Profile** has to be complete, so you appear on Google Maps.
- Other trusted sites have to **mention you**, through reviews, directories and links.

If you want the full picture, our guide to [what SEO is and how it works](/guides/what-is-seo/) covers the basics. Here, we'll focus on what's different about doing it in Nigeria.

## How people search in Nigeria

Every country searches a little differently. These patterns show up again and again for Nigerian businesses, and they shape what you should do.

**Phones come first.** Most people reach your website on a mobile phone, often on a data plan where every megabyte costs money. A heavy page that takes ages to load loses the visitor before they read a word.

**"Near me" and area names are everywhere.** People search with places in their query: Ikeja, Lekki, Wuse, Port Harcourt, Ibadan. They also search by landmark. If your pages never mention the areas you serve, you're invisible for those searches.

**Price questions come early.** "How much does it cost" is one of the most common things people type. Pages that give an honest range, even a rough one, tend to win trust over pages that say "contact us for a quote".

**People like to chat.** Many customers would rather send a WhatsApp message than fill in a form. If your contact options are slow or hidden, you lose enquiries you've already earned.

**Trust matters a lot.** Customers want proof that you're real: a clear address, phone numbers that answer, real reviews and photos of actual work.

## The five things that matter most

You could spend years on SEO. Here are the five areas that move the needle first.

### 1. A fast website that works on a phone

Open your site on a mid-range phone with a normal data connection. Is it quick? Can you read the text without zooming? Can you tap the buttons? If not, fix that before anything else.

The usual culprits are huge images, too many plugins and cheap hosting. Compress every image, remove tools you don't use and test your main pages with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). Our guide to [Core Web Vitals in plain English](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/) explains what the scores mean.

### 2. A complete Google Business Profile

For any business with a location, this is the highest-value free step. Your profile decides whether you appear on Google Maps and in the "map pack", the three businesses shown at the top for local searches.

Fill in everything: the right categories, services, opening hours, photos and a clear description. Address formats can be tricky in Nigeria, so help customers find you by describing the location with a nearby landmark in your description or on your website. Google's own [Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business) explains each field.

### 3. One page per service and area

Many websites cram everything onto one page: "We do plumbing, electrical, painting and tiling in Lagos." That tells Google very little.

Create a separate page for each main service, and where it makes sense, for each area you serve. A page called "Generator repair in Ikeja" can rank for that exact search. A page called "Our services" almost never will. Write something useful on each one: what you do, how it works, a price range, photos and a clear way to contact you.

### 4. Reviews and trust signals

Reviews are social proof at the exact moment someone is deciding. Ask every happy customer for a Google review, and make it easy by sending the direct link by WhatsApp after the job. Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) has ready-to-use messages.

Add other trust signals too: your real address, a working phone number, your registration details if you have them, and photos of your team and work.

### 5. Easy contact, including WhatsApp

Put your phone number and a WhatsApp button where people can see them on every page, especially on mobile. WhatsApp offers a free [Business app](https://business.whatsapp.com/) with a catalogue, quick replies and a business profile. Then track the clicks, so you know how many enquiries your website really brings. A click-to-chat button is one of the simplest ways to turn visits into conversations.

## Local, national or international?

Be clear about who you want as customers, because it changes the plan.

- **Local:** You serve one city or a few areas. Focus on your Google profile, area pages and reviews.
- **National:** You sell or deliver across Nigeria. You'll need stronger content, and probably more links from respected websites.
- **International:** You want customers abroad, for example travellers, the diaspora or overseas buyers. You'll need pages written for them, clear prices and payment options, and a site that loads quickly from other countries.

Many Nigerian businesses are a mix. Start with your strongest, closest market, win it, then expand.

## A simple 90-day plan

You don't need to do everything at once. Here's an order that works.

**Days 1 to 30: build the foundation**
1. Set up [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) so you can see how Google views your site.
2. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile.
3. Fix speed and mobile problems on your home page and main service pages.
4. Make sure each main service has its own page.
5. Add your phone number and WhatsApp link to every page.

**Days 31 to 60: build trust and reach**
1. Start asking every customer for a Google review.
2. Make your business name, address and phone number identical everywhere. Our guide to [local citations](/blog/local-citations-explained/) shows how.
3. Publish one helpful article or page a month that answers a question customers ask you.

**Days 61 to 90: grow and measure**
1. Ask suppliers, partners and local organisations to link to you. See [how to get backlinks safely](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/).
2. Look in Search Console for searches where you appear on page two or three, and improve those pages.
3. Compare your calls, enquiries and reviews with where you started.

## Mistakes that waste money

**Relying only on social media.** It's useful, but you rent that audience. A website and a Google profile are assets you own.

**Buying cheap "SEO packages".** Offers like "1,000 backlinks for a small fee" usually mean spammy links that can get your site penalised. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) explain what to avoid.

**Using one page for everything.** Google can't match a vague page to a specific search.

**Ignoring the phone.** If your site is awkward on mobile, you're losing the majority of visitors.

**Duplicate or outdated listings.** Old phone numbers and addresses confuse customers and search engines.

**Not tracking anything.** If you can't see calls, WhatsApp clicks and enquiries, you can't tell what's working.

**Expecting overnight results.** Local improvements can show in weeks. Competitive searches take months. Our guide on [how long SEO takes](/guides/how-long-does-seo-take/) gives realistic timelines.

## Privacy and trust online

If you collect customer details through forms or mailing lists, you have responsibilities under Nigerian data protection law. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission publishes guidance at [ndpc.gov.ng](https://ndpc.gov.ng/). At a minimum, have a clear privacy policy, only collect what you need and keep it safe. It's also a trust signal, because customers notice businesses that handle their information carefully.

## Should you do it yourself or hire help?

You can handle a lot of this yourself if you have a few hours a week: the Google profile, reviews, basic page fixes and a monthly article. Bring in help when:

- you don't have the time to stay consistent,
- your market is competitive,
- your website has technical problems you can't fix,
- you want a clear plan and honest monthly reporting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SEO worth it for a small business in Nigeria?
Usually, yes, especially if your customers search before buying. The free first steps, such as your Google profile, reviews and a clear page for each service, cost only time.

### How long does it take to see results?
Local improvements, like a better Google profile, can show within a few weeks. Competitive searches often take four to six months or longer.

### Do I need a website if I have Instagram and WhatsApp?
A website gives you something you own and a place Google can show. Social media and WhatsApp work well alongside it, but they don't replace it.

### Can I rank on Google without spending on ads?
Yes. SEO is how you earn visibility without paying per click. Ads can work alongside it. See [SEO vs PPC](/blog/seo-vs-ppc-which-is-right-for-your-business/) to decide which fits.

### Should I use Nigerian directories?
Yes, if they're reputable and relevant to your business, and make sure your details match everywhere. Quality beats quantity.
