Imagine a dental clinic that started in Ikeja. It was busy, trusted and easy to find on Google Maps. Over six years it opened branches in Lekki, Victoria Island, Abuja and Port Harcourt. Every opening was a celebration. Every opening was also, quietly, an SEO problem.

The Lekki branch had no Google profile for three months. Abuja shared the Ikeja phone number. The website had one "Contact us" page with five addresses on it. A manager in Port Harcourt created a second profile because he couldn't find the first. And when someone in Lekki typed "dentist near me", the clinic that had spent years building its reputation was nowhere to be seen.

This is what happens to most businesses when they grow. What worked for one location doesn't simply multiply. Each branch needs its own presence, its own proof and its own care, and all of it has to stay consistent under one brand.

This guide shows you how to run local SEO for a business with several branches. It's long, because the topic is. But every section is a step you can act on, and there's a 90-day rollout plan at the end. It works for clinics, restaurants, salons, schools, shops, banks, logistics companies and any business with more than one place customers can visit.

## What makes multi-location SEO different

With one branch, local SEO is about one profile, one page and one set of reviews. With several, three things change.

**1. Every branch competes in its own market.** Someone searching in Lekki will see Lekki results. Someone in Abuja will see Abuja results. Your Ikeja branch's reputation doesn't carry across the city, let alone to another state. Google ranks local results using relevance, distance and prominence, as it explains in its guide to [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), and distance alone means each branch has to earn its position locally.

**2. Consistency gets harder.** More branches means more phone numbers, opening hours, managers, listings and chances for details to drift apart.

**3. Duplicated content becomes a temptation.** It's tempting to copy one page eight times and change the city name. Search engines and customers both notice.

The answer isn't complicated, but it needs a system. The structure below has two layers: a strong brand website, and a complete, separate presence for each branch.

## Step 1: Decide which locations deserve their own profile

Before you create anything, get this right, because mistakes here can lead to suspended profiles.

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say that a location can have its own profile if it's a real place where customers can visit or be served, staffed during the hours you list. In plain terms:

- **A staffed branch with a street address that customers visit** gets its own profile.
- **A service-area business** that travels to customers (for example, a plumber covering several areas) can have a profile for its real base and list the areas it serves. It shouldn't invent branches in every area.
- **A virtual office, a PO box, a shared desk nobody sits at, or a friend's address** doesn't qualify.
- **A storage unit or warehouse with no customer access** usually doesn't qualify either.

Write this decision down for each location. It protects you from the temptation, especially common in Nigeria where address rules can be loose, to create profiles for places you don't really operate.

## Step 2: Set up one Google Business Profile per branch

This is the foundation. Each branch should have its own profile, verified and complete.

### Use one clear naming rule

Your profile name should be your real-world business name, the one on your signage, website and invoices. Decide the rule once and apply it to every branch. For example:

- If the signs say "Brightway Dental Clinic", every profile is "Brightway Dental Clinic".
- If each branch's signage includes the area, such as "Brightway Dental Clinic, Lekki", then that's the name, and it must match the sign.

Never add keywords or locations just to rank. Google can edit or suspend profiles that do. Our guide to [Business Profile mistakes](/blog/google-business-profile-mistakes/) explains why.

### Give every branch its own details

- **Its own address and map pin**, placed on the real entrance.
- **Its own local phone number**, ideally one that rings at that branch. Shared call centres can work, but branch numbers build local trust and make tracking possible.
- **Its own opening hours**, including holidays.
- **Its own website link**, pointing to that branch's page on your site (see Step 3), not just the home page.
- **Its own photos**: the exterior, entrance, inside, team and examples of work for that branch.
- **Its own services and description.** Don't paste the same text everywhere. Mention the neighbourhood, landmarks and anything particular to that branch.

Choose the same **primary category** across branches that do the same work, and add secondary categories where a branch offers something extra. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/guides/google-business-profile-optimization/) covers every field.

### Manage them in one place

Google lets you manage several locations from one account, and you can organise them into groups, such as "Lagos" and "Other cities". If you manage ten or more locations, Google also offers bulk tools for uploading and verifying them. The details change from time to time, so check [Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business) for the current options.

### Control who owns what

This one decision prevents years of pain.

- **Make the business, not an individual, the owner.** Create the profiles under a company Google account, not a manager's personal one.
- **Add branch managers as managers**, not owners, with their own logins.
- **Keep a list of who has access**, and remove people when they leave.
- **Never share one password.**

When a manager leaves and takes the only login with them, recovering a profile can take weeks.

## Step 3: Build a strong page for every branch

Each profile should link to a page on your website made for that branch. This is the page Google and customers will use to confirm you're real and relevant in that place.

![Screenshot: a branch page for Lekki Phase 1 with address, phone, hours, services, directions and a booking button](/images/screenshots/branch-location-page-example.png)

*Figure 3: A branch page that answers what a local customer needs: where, when, what and how to contact this branch.*

### What every branch page should include

1. **A clear title and heading:** "Dental Clinic in Lekki Phase 1 | Brightway Dental".
2. **The full address,** matching the Google profile exactly, plus directions and landmarks, such as "opposite the filling station, five minutes from the Phase 1 gate".
3. **The branch phone number** and a click-to-call link, plus a WhatsApp link if you use it.
4. **Opening hours** specific to that branch.
5. **Services offered at that branch.** Not every branch offers everything. Be honest.
6. **Photos of that branch,** its entrance and its team.
7. **Names of local staff,** where appropriate. People trust faces.
8. **Reviews from customers of that branch.**
9. **A map embed** or a link to directions.
10. **Parking, accessibility and transport information.**
11. **A booking or enquiry button** that goes to that branch.
12. **Links to nearby areas you serve,** and to your other branches.

Our guide to [local SEO for Nigerian businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-nigerian-businesses/) explains how to handle addresses, landmarks and WhatsApp in more detail.

### Avoid doorway pages

A doorway page is a thin page made to catch searches for a place, with almost the same content as others. Google's spam policies treat this as a problem. If your Lekki page is the Ikeja page with "Lekki" typed in, you're in risky territory.

![Diagram: a strong branch page compared with a copy-paste page](/images/diagrams/branch-pages-strong-vs-copy-paste.svg)

*Figure 4: Make every branch page genuinely different, because the branch genuinely is.*

If a branch doesn't have enough unique things to say yet, add them. Photos, staff names, reviews, directions and local FAQs all count. If you can't, consider whether it needs a page of its own.

### Write the local details only your branch knows

Ask each branch manager to answer five questions:

1. How do customers find you? Which road, landmark or turning?
2. What do customers from this area ask most often?
3. Which services are most popular here?
4. Who are the team members customers know by name?
5. What's one thing about this branch that makes it different?

Their answers are your unique content. It's the part no competitor can copy.

## Step 4: Create a locations hub

Add a main "Locations" page that lists every branch with its area, address, phone number and a link to its page. This is your store locator.

- Use **plain, crawlable links** to each branch page, not a map that only works with JavaScript. If search engines can't follow the links, they won't find the pages.
- Group branches by city or region if you have many.
- Link back to the hub from every branch page, and add breadcrumbs: Home > Locations > Lekki Phase 1.
- Include the hub in your main menu or footer.

Our guide to [internal linking](/blog/internal-linking-guide/) explains how these links spread authority and help discovery.

## Step 5: Add area pages only where they help

If a branch serves several neighbourhoods, a short, useful page for each can help. For example, "Dentist in Ikate" and "Dentist in Oniru" might both link to the Lekki branch.

Each area page needs real content: how long it takes to reach the branch from there, local transport, local customer examples and which services people in that area ask for. If you can't write something honest and different, skip the page. A handful of good pages beats fifty thin ones.

## Step 6: Keep names, addresses and phone numbers identical

Search engines compare your details across the web. When your branch information matches everywhere, trust grows. When it doesn't, you create doubt.

For every branch, create a master record:

- the exact business name,
- the exact address, with a fixed format and landmark,
- the phone number in international format (+234...),
- the website link for that branch,
- the opening hours,
- a short and long description,
- a branch code, such as IKJ-01, so people can refer to it.

Use this one record for every listing: Google, Bing Places, [Apple Business Connect](https://businessconnect.apple.com), Facebook, industry directories and chambers of commerce. Our guide to [local citations](/blog/local-citations-explained/) shows how to find and fix them.

Whenever something changes at a branch, such as a new number or address, update the master record first, then everywhere else. We cover this in detail in our guide to [changing your address, phone number or business name](/blog/how-to-change-business-details-without-losing-rankings/).

## Step 7: Collect reviews branch by branch

Reviews are the biggest local ranking and conversion factor you control. In a multi-branch business, they need to be earned and managed at branch level.

1. **Give each branch its own review link,** and put it on cards, receipts, QR codes and follow-up messages.
2. **Ask at the right moment.** After a completed visit or service, by WhatsApp or SMS, with a short, friendly message.
3. **Make it part of each branch's routine.** Set a simple target, such as five new reviews per week.
4. **Reply to every review,** within two days. Decide who does it. Branch managers know the story; head office ensures the tone is right.
5. **Handle problems calmly,** offering to resolve them offline.
6. **Never buy reviews, offer rewards for them or ask only happy customers.** It breaks the rules and risks removal.
7. **Show each branch's reviews on its own page.**

Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) includes message templates you can give to every branch.

A branch with 12 reviews next to a competitor with 200 loses calls, however good the work is. Track review counts like a sales target.

## Step 8: Add structured data for every branch

Structured data helps search engines understand each location. Add **LocalBusiness** markup (or a more specific type, such as Dentist or Restaurant) to every branch page, with:

- the name,
- the address and map coordinates,
- the phone number,
- opening hours,
- the branch URL,
- a link to the parent organisation.

Google's [local business structured data guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) explains the fields. Only mark up what's visible on the page. Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) shows how to add it and test it.

## Step 9: Earn local links and mentions for each branch

A branch page that nobody links to is harder to rank. Aim for a few relevant, local mentions per branch:

- a sponsorship of a local event or school team,
- a partnership with a nearby business or estate,
- a local business group or chamber listing,
- a feature in a local news or lifestyle blog,
- a mention from a supplier or partner.

Keep it honest and local. Our guide to [safe link building](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/) explains how.

## Step 10: Measure every branch separately

If you only look at the whole business, you'll never see which branches are strong and which need help. Track each branch on its own.

![Screenshot: a spreadsheet comparing branches by profile views, calls, directions, WhatsApp clicks, new reviews and rating](/images/screenshots/branch-performance-sheet.png)

*Figure 5: A simple branch comparison shows where to focus next.*

Each month, record for every branch:

- profile views, calls, direction requests and website clicks from the Google profile,
- WhatsApp and phone clicks from the branch page,
- new reviews and average rating,
- visits and enquiries from the branch page in Google Analytics,
- bookings or sales, if you can link them to the branch.

To tell branches apart in your analytics, add a tracking parameter to each profile's website link, such as the branch code. Our guide to [tracking SEO results](/blog/track-seo-results-ga4-search-console/) explains how to set it up, and [SEO metrics that matter](/blog/seo-metrics-that-matter/) helps you decide which numbers to follow.

Then compare. If one branch has a similar market but half the calls, check its profile, photos, reviews, page and phone number. The problem is usually visible.

## Central or local: who does what?

Some work is best done once, centrally. Some needs someone at the branch. Here's a useful split.

| Task | Best done by |
|---|---|
| Brand name, categories, templates, guidelines | Head office |
| Website, structured data, locations hub | Head office |
| Master records, citations, duplicates | Head office |
| Reporting and quarterly audits | Head office |
| Local photos, staff details, landmarks | Branch |
| Review requests at the point of service | Branch |
| Opening hours and holiday updates | Branch, checked by head office |
| Replies to reviews | Branch drafts, head office guides tone |
| Local partnerships and mentions | Branch |

Write the split down, give each branch a one-page checklist and review it quarterly.

## Opening a new branch: a checklist

Opening day shouldn't be the first time Google hears about the branch.

**Before opening:**
- Add the branch to your master record, with a branch code.
- Prepare photos, hours, phone number and a description.
- Build the branch page, but keep it ready rather than live, if the address isn't real yet.

**On opening day:**
- Create and verify the Google profile. Verification can take time, so start early.
- Publish the branch page, and link it from your locations hub.
- Update your listings, Apple and Bing maps and social profiles.
- Announce the opening with a post and a short update.

**First 30 days:**
- Ask early customers for reviews.
- Add photos and a first post.
- Check that the profile, page and listings all match.
- Set up tracking for the branch.

## Closing or moving a branch

Don't delete the profile or page without a plan.

- **If a branch closes permanently,** mark the profile as permanently closed, so customers aren't sent to an empty shop. Redirect the branch page to the nearest branch or the locations hub with a permanent redirect.
- **If a branch moves,** update the existing profile and page rather than creating new ones, so you keep your reviews and history. Our guide to [changing your business details](/blog/how-to-change-business-details-without-losing-rankings/) shows how.
- **If a branch temporarily closes,** use temporary closure settings and update your page.

## Common mistakes with multiple locations

- **One phone number for every branch.** It hides local relevance and makes tracking impossible.
- **Copying the same page for each city.** Thin, duplicated pages are risky.
- **Creating profiles for places you don't operate.** It breaks the rules and can lead to suspensions.
- **Personal logins as owners.** Access is lost when people leave.
- **Duplicate profiles** created by managers who couldn't find the original.
- **Different names in different places,** such as "Brightway Dental", "Brightway Clinic" and "Brightway Dentals".
- **No reviews at some branches,** or no replies.
- **A store locator that search engines can't read.**
- **Ignoring weaker branches** because the main one performs well.
- **Never checking the details,** so hours and numbers go out of date.

## A 90-day rollout plan

Imagine a business with five branches. Here's a realistic plan.

**Days 1 to 15: audit and fix the foundation**
- List every branch, and decide which qualify for profiles.
- Create the master record for each branch.
- Find and merge duplicates. Claim missing profiles.
- Set ownership and access properly.

**Days 16 to 45: profiles and pages**
- Complete every profile: categories, hours, services, photos and local descriptions.
- Build or rewrite each branch page, and the locations hub.
- Add LocalBusiness structured data.
- Give every branch its own phone number and tracking link.

**Days 46 to 75: reviews and listings**
- Launch the review routine at every branch.
- Clean up citations, Apple Maps and Bing Places.
- Reply to all existing reviews.

**Days 76 to 90: measure and improve**
- Build your branch comparison sheet.
- Compare branches and investigate the weakest.
- Add local content, such as area pages and local FAQs.
- Start local link outreach.
- Plan the next quarter.

Then repeat quarterly. Local SEO for several branches is a rhythm, not a one-off project.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every branch need its own Google Business Profile?
Every staffed, customer-facing branch with a real address should have one. Don't create profiles for places that don't qualify.

### Can I use one website for all branches?
Yes, and it's usually best. Use one strong brand site with a separate, unique page for each branch.

### Should each branch have a different phone number?
Ideally yes, a local number that reaches that branch. It builds trust and lets you track calls.

### How many reviews does each branch need?
There's no magic number. Aim to match or beat the leading competitors in that branch's area, and keep a steady flow of new ones.

### What if two branches are very close together?
Make sure each has a clearly different address and offering, and avoid duplicating content. Google may favour one in a given search.

### How long does it take to see results?
Profile fixes can show within weeks. Competing in busy areas, such as central Lagos, usually takes a few months of steady work.

### Can I do this myself?
Yes, for a few branches. With many branches, a system, templates and clear roles matter more than any single tactic. An outside specialist can help build the system.
