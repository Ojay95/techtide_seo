A generator repair business in Ikeja had a good problem. It had outgrown its small workshop on Alausa Street. The new place, a few streets away on Maryland Road, had space for a proper showroom and a parking area. The owner signed the lease, painted the sign and told everyone.

Three weeks after the move, the phone went quiet. Customers who searched for the business on Google Maps were being sent to the old address. Some of them found an empty workshop. A directory still showed a phone number the owner had stopped answering. The new address on the website didn't match the one on Google. The business hadn't lost its reputation. It had simply lost its trail.

This is one of the most avoidable problems in local SEO. Search engines and customers rely on your details being the same everywhere. When you change your address, your phone number or your name, you break that sameness for a while, and how carefully you repair it decides whether you lose a few weeks of calls or several months.

This guide gives you a complete plan. You'll learn what happens when details change, what to do before, on and after the day, how to handle each type of change and how to tell a normal dip from a real problem. It's long because every missed step is a place where customers can get lost.

## Why changing details is risky for SEO

Three things make it tricky.

### 1. Search engines trust consistency

Google and other services compare your name, address and phone number across the web: on your Google profile, your website, directories, social profiles and mentions. When they match, your business looks established and real. When they don't, doubt creeps in. Our guide to [local citations](/blog/local-citations-explained/) explains why this matters.

### 2. A new address changes your distance

Google ranks local results using relevance, distance and prominence, as it explains in its guide to [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). If you move a few streets, the searchers closest to you change. Some of the people who used to find you first will now be closer to a competitor, and some people who were far away will now find you first. Your rankings can shift for a reason that has nothing to do with your SEO. That's normal, and worth knowing before you panic.

### 3. Your reputation lives in a few places

Your reviews, photos and history sit on your Google profile. If you handle the change the right way, they stay. If you create a new profile, you start from zero.

## What kind of change are you making?

Different changes carry different risks.

| Change | Risk level | Main danger |
|---|---|---|
| Phone number | Low to medium | Missed calls and mismatched listings |
| Address (moving) | Medium to high | Customers sent to the wrong place, ranking shifts |
| Business name (rebrand) | High | Lost recognition, mismatched listings, re-verification |
| Website address (new domain) | High | Lost links and traffic. See our [migration checklist](/blog/website-migration-seo-checklist/) |
| Several of the above together | Highest | Everything above, at once |

If you can, separate big changes. For example, complete a move first, let things settle for a month or two, then rebrand. When changes happen together, as they often do, the plan below still works. You just need to be more careful, and prepare more evidence.

## Seven golden rules

1. **Update your existing Google profile. Don't create a new one.** A new profile means no reviews, no history and a duplicate.
2. **Don't mark the profile as permanently closed when you move or rebrand.** That tells customers you've shut.
3. **Change everything to match, as close to the same day as you can.**
4. **Keep the old phone number alive** for a long time.
5. **Make sure the new details are accurate and real.** Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) require a genuine address and a real-world name.
6. **Keep records.** Write down what you changed, where and when.
7. **Monitor for 12 weeks,** and fix what you find.

## Before you change anything: prepare

Preparation is where most of the saving is. Do this one to two weeks before the change.

### 1. Find every place your details live

Your name, address and number are probably in more places than you think.

![Diagram: eight places where business details appear, including the Google profile, website, schema, social media, directories, email, ads and map apps](/images/diagrams/where-your-business-details-live.svg)

*Figure 2: If it shows your name, address or number, it needs to match.*

Make a list. Include:

- your Google Business Profile,
- your website: header, footer, contact page, location pages, About page and map embed,
- structured data (schema) on your website,
- Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X and YouTube,
- Apple Business Connect and Bing Places,
- industry directories, local directories and chambers of commerce,
- Google Ads, social ads and any call or location extensions,
- email signatures, invoices, receipts, quotes and letterheads,
- WhatsApp Business profile,
- your online booking or ordering system,
- printed materials, vehicles, signage and uniforms,
- partner and supplier websites that list you.

### 2. Build a tracker

Put every place in a spreadsheet, with the old details, the new details, the status and the date.

![Screenshot: a tracker listing each place with old details, new details, status and date updated](/images/screenshots/nap-update-tracker-sheet.png)

*Figure 3: A simple tracker makes sure no listing is left behind.*

### 3. Create a master record

Write the final, correct version once, and copy from it everywhere:

- exact business name,
- exact address, including how you write the area and a landmark,
- phone number in international format (+234...),
- WhatsApp link,
- website address,
- opening hours,
- a short description and a longer one.

Small differences count. "Rd" and "Road", "Ikeja" and "Ikeja, Lagos" can create mismatches. Pick one format and stay with it.

### 4. Check that you control your accounts

Make sure you have working logins for your Google profile, website, Facebook and your main listings. You don't want to find out on moving day that the only login belongs to a former employee.

### 5. Prepare the new materials

- New photos: exterior, entrance, interior and team (for a move).
- New signage and printed materials.
- Updated website text and pages, ready to publish.
- A short announcement for customers.
- Redirect rules, if your website address is changing too.

### 6. Pick the date, and tell your team

Decide when each update happens, and who does what. Everyone who answers phones or messages should know the new details and how to handle questions.

## Changing your address (moving)

### Step 1: Update your Google profile on the day you open

Use your existing profile. Go to your profile's **Edit profile** screen and update the address. Google may ask you to verify the new location again, and it may review the change. Be ready with a video or proof of your new premises, such as signage, the entrance and equipment.

![Screenshot: the Business Profile editor showing the address change, the new phone number and the old number kept as additional](/images/screenshots/gbp-edit-business-info.png)

*Figure 4: Edit the existing profile. Change the address, add the new number and keep the old one as additional for a while.*

**Timing matters.** If you update the address too early, customers go to a place you haven't moved to yet. If you update it too late, they go to a place you've left. Aim to update on the day you open at the new place. If there's a gap, use **temporarily closed** during the move, and reopen when you're ready.

**Don't mark the business "permanently closed".** That's for businesses that have really shut. Marking a move that way can remove you from search and confuse customers.

### Step 2: Refresh your photos

Remove or replace photos of the old premises, and add clear new ones. Photos build trust and show customers what to look for when they arrive.

### Step 3: Place the pin precisely

Zoom in and put the pin on your actual entrance. If your area has unclear addresses, add a landmark in the description, such as "opposite the pharmacy, next to the filling station", and consider a [Plus Code](https://maps.google.com/pluscodes/) so customers can navigate to the exact spot.

### Step 4: Say you've moved

Add a short line to your description for a few months: "We moved to Maryland Road in October 2026." Post an update on your profile, with a photo of the new place.

### Step 5: Update your website

- the address in the header, footer, contact page and location pages,
- the embedded map and directions,
- the schema markup, including the address and coordinates (see [schema markup](/blog/schema-markup-guide/)),
- any "Find us" or "About" text.

Publish the website changes on the same day as the profile change.

### Step 6: Update every listing

Work through your tracker: Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Facebook, directories, chambers and partner pages. Prioritise the ones that appear in search and the ones customers use. Directories that you can't edit may need a request to the owner, so start early.

### Step 7: Deal with the old address

- Leave a clear sign at the old premises with the new address and phone number.
- Forward mail, if possible.
- Tell regular customers and suppliers directly.
- If someone else takes the old space, make sure no profile for them is confused with yours.

### Step 8: Expect your ranking to shift

Because distance is part of how Google ranks local results, your position can move after you relocate. A short dip while Google rechecks the new details is common. Keep the details accurate, keep collecting reviews and keep your profile active. Our [Business Profile mistakes](/blog/google-business-profile-mistakes/) guide shows what else can hold a profile back.

## Changing your phone number

A phone number change is simpler, but it's the one that silently costs the most calls, because customers keep ringing the old number.

### Step 1: Keep the old number working

Keep the old number active for at least six to twelve months, and forward calls to the new one, if your provider allows. Customers have it saved, printed on old materials and listed in places you've forgotten.

### Step 2: Update Google

On your profile, set the **new number as primary** and add the **old number as additional**. This helps Google connect the two, and helps customers who search with the old number. Keep your real number as the primary. Don't use a tracking number as the primary number, because it creates mismatches.

### Step 3: Update everywhere

- website header, footer and contact page,
- click-to-call links and WhatsApp links,
- schema markup,
- Facebook, Instagram and other profiles,
- directories and map apps,
- Google Ads call extensions and call assets,
- email signatures, invoices, quotes and receipts,
- vehicles, signs and printed materials.

### Step 4: Check your WhatsApp

If your WhatsApp Business number changes, update the profile and the links on your website. Tell regular customers directly. Remember that a click-to-chat link contains the number, so every link on your site needs updating. Our [local SEO guide for Nigerian businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-nigerian-businesses/) explains how these links work.

### Step 5: Update your recorded messages

If your old number has a voicemail or answering message, update it with the new number, so callers aren't left wondering.

## Changing your business name (rebranding)

This is the riskiest of the three, because your name is the thing people search for, and the thing Google relies on to recognise you.

### Step 1: Make the name real first

Google requires your profile name to reflect your real-world name, the one on your signage, website and invoices. So change those first. Put up the new sign, update your website and prepare documents that show the new name, such as photos of the signage, registration documents or invoices. If you're asked to prove the change, you'll have evidence ready. Check that your legal registration and tax documents also reflect the name you intend to trade under.

### Step 2: Update the profile name

Change the name in your existing profile. Don't add keywords, taglines or locations that aren't part of your real name, which can lead to suspension. The change may be reviewed.

### Step 3: Connect the old and the new

People will keep searching for the old name for months. Help them.

- Mention the old name in your **description** for a while: "Formerly Ade's Generator Services."
- Add a short note on your **About page** and **home page**.
- Use **alternateName** in your structured data so search engines know both names refer to the same business.
- Keep the old name visible in a few places that make sense, such as invoices, for a transition period.

### Step 4: Update every listing and account

Go through your tracker. Directories, social profiles, email addresses, ad accounts and partner pages all need the new name. For social profiles, change the display name, and the handle if you can, and check that you aren't leaving old handles unclaimed.

### Step 5: Update links and mentions

Other websites may link to you using your old name. Contact the most important ones, such as suppliers, partners, directories and press, and ask them to update the name and link text. Our guide to [safe link building](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/) explains outreach.

### Step 6: If your domain changes too

A new name often means a new website address. That's a bigger project, with its own risks to rankings. Use proper 301 redirects from every old page to its new equivalent, and follow Google's guides on [site moves with URL changes](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes). Our [website migration checklist](/blog/website-migration-seo-checklist/) walks through it step by step.

### Step 7: Tell everyone

Announce the change with a post, an email, a message to customers and a note on your website. Explain it simply: new name, same people and same service.

## If you change more than one thing

Sometimes you move, change your number and rebrand all at once. It works, but be extra careful.

- Prepare your evidence in advance: photos of signage, premises, documents.
- Keep all new details perfectly consistent.
- Update Google and your website on the same day.
- Expect re-verification, and plan for a few days when your profile may be limited.
- Watch your numbers more closely, and for longer.

## A timeline you can follow

**Two weeks before**
- Build your list, tracker and master record.
- Confirm access to every account.
- Prepare photos, text, redirects and announcements.
- Brief your team.

**One week before**
- Order signage and printed materials.
- Prepare website changes, but don't publish yet.
- Warn regular customers and partners.

**Change day**
- Update your Google profile first.
- Publish the website changes, including schema.
- Update your main social profiles.
- Post the announcement.
- Start working through the tracker.

**Week one**
- Update remaining directories, map apps and ad accounts.
- Check that Google, Apple and Bing show the new details.
- Reply to customers who contact the old number or address.

**Weeks two to four**
- Chase listings that haven't updated.
- Look for duplicates and fix them.
- Request reviews at the new place.
- Check your numbers against the previous month.

**Months two to three**
- Keep monitoring.
- Reclaim important links.
- Keep the old number forwarding.
- Review the tracker and close it only when everything matches.

## What to expect afterwards

A short dip is normal. Google needs time to re-check, listings take time to update and customers need time to learn the new details.

![Screenshot: a performance chart showing a dip in calls and profile views after a move, followed by recovery](/images/screenshots/local-visibility-before-after-change.png)

*Figure 5: A short dip, then recovery, when details are updated consistently.*

**Normal:**
- a dip in calls or views for a few weeks,
- some customers still using the old number or address for a while,
- small ranking movements, especially after a move.

**A warning sign:**
- a profile that has been suspended or is stuck in review,
- calls still falling after six to eight weeks,
- a new, duplicate profile appearing,
- Google showing the old address or name after you've updated it,
- missing reviews or photos.

## Troubleshooting

**Your profile asks for re-verification.** Follow the steps in the profile. Have clear video or photo evidence of the premises, signage and equipment. Don't submit repeated requests.

**Your profile is suspended.** Don't create a new one. Check what Google flagged, correct it, and submit a reinstatement request with evidence through [Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business).

**The old address still shows on Maps.** Check for a duplicate profile or an old listing in another dataset. Report outdated information, and update the main listings. Apple and Bing may show old data longer, so check [Apple Business Connect](https://businessconnect.apple.com) and [Bing Places](https://www.bingplaces.com) directly.

**A duplicate profile appeared.** Search for the old name and address, and claim or request removal of the duplicate. See our [Business Profile mistakes](/blog/google-business-profile-mistakes/) guide.

**Calls are still falling after two months.** Compare your website, profile and listings line by line against the master record, and check that the old number forwards correctly. Look at your numbers in [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) and Analytics for anything unusual. Our guide to [tracking SEO results](/blog/track-seo-results-ga4-search-console/) explains how.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Creating a new Google profile** instead of editing the existing one.
- **Marking the old profile as permanently closed** when you've only moved or rebranded.
- **Updating Google but not your website** or listings.
- **Using small variations,** such as "Rd" in one place and "Road" in another.
- **Disconnecting the old phone number** too soon.
- **Putting a tracking number as the primary number.**
- **Adding keywords to the new name.**
- **Forgetting schema, ads, email signatures and invoices.**
- **Making the change in a busy season** without a plan.
- **Not watching your numbers afterwards.**

## A final checklist

**Before:** every place listed, tracker built, master record written, accounts accessed, materials ready, team briefed.

**On the day:** Google profile updated, website and schema updated, social profiles updated, announcement posted.

**After:** listings updated, old number forwarding, duplicates fixed, reviews requested, numbers monitored for 12 weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will I lose my Google reviews if I move?
Not if you update your existing profile. Reviews stay with the profile. Creating a new one would lose them.

### How long does it take for Google to show the new address?
Often within a few days, though it can take longer if the change is reviewed or needs verification. Other maps and directories can take longer still.

### Can I change my business name on Google whenever I like?
Only to your real-world name. Frequent or keyword-driven changes can lead to review or suspension, so change it when you genuinely rebrand, and with evidence ready.

### Should I keep my old phone number?
Yes, for at least six to twelve months, forwarding to the new one. List it as an additional number on your Google profile.

### Will my rankings drop if I move?
They may move, partly because distance is a ranking factor. Keep your details consistent, earn new reviews and keep your profile active, and most businesses recover within weeks to a few months.

### Do I need to tell Google I've moved in some other way?
Updating your profile is the main step. If you're also moving your website to a new domain, use the Change of address tool in Search Console after your redirects are in place.

### What if I can't update a directory listing?
Contact the directory's support and ask them to update or remove it. Keep a note in your tracker and follow up until it's done.
