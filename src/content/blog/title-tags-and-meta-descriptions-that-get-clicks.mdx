Every time you search on Google, you make a split-second decision. You scan a few results, read the blue headline and the grey text underneath, and click the one that seems most likely to help. That blue headline is the **title tag**. The grey text is usually the **meta description**.

Together they're your shop window in search. Two businesses can rank side by side, and the one with the clearer, more appealing listing gets most of the clicks. The good news is that titles and descriptions are among the easiest things in SEO to improve. You don't need technical skills, just a bit of care and a few formulas.

This guide explains what they are, how long they should be, how to write them, how Google sometimes rewrites them and how to find the pages that need work first. It includes copy-and-paste templates and real before-and-after examples.

## What title tags and meta descriptions are

The **title tag** is a line of code in a page's head that names the page. Google usually shows it as the clickable headline in search results. It also appears in browser tabs and when people share the page.

The **meta description** is a short summary of the page, also in the head. Google may show it under the title, but it often chooses its own snippet from the page text if it thinks that matches the search better.

## Why they matter

**Titles help rankings and clicks.** Google uses the title to understand what a page is about, so a clear title with the main topic in it helps. And because it's the headline people read, it influences whether they click.

**Descriptions help clicks, not rankings.** Google has said meta descriptions aren't a direct ranking factor, but a good one can persuade someone to choose your result. More clicks from the same position means more visitors without any extra ranking work.

**They show your quality.** A sloppy title like "Home" or "Untitled Page" tells a searcher that nobody cared. A clear one signals a real, careful business.

## How long should they be?

Google doesn't set strict limits. It truncates titles and descriptions based on the width they take up on screen, not the number of characters. As a practical guide:

- **Title tag:** aim for about 30 to 60 characters. Over that, the end may be cut off with "...".
- **Meta description:** aim for about 120 to 155 characters. Longer descriptions get trimmed, especially on phones.

Put the most important words first, so that even if the end gets cut, the message survives.

## How Google chooses what to show

You write a title and description, but Google decides what to display. Its documentation on [title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) explains that it may rewrite a title if it's too long, stuffed with keywords, missing, or doesn't match the page well. Its guidance on [snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) says the description often comes from the page content when that fits the search better.

This is not a reason to ignore them. A good title and description are used often, and the clearer and more accurate your page is, the more likely Google is to keep what you wrote. If you see Google rewriting yours, treat it as feedback: the title may be too long, too vague or not matching the page.

## A simple formula for titles

Most good titles follow a pattern:

**Primary keyword + a benefit or qualifier + brand**

Examples:

- "Local SEO Services in Lagos | Get Found on Google Maps | TechTide SEO"
- "How to Get More Google Reviews: Templates and Tips"
- "Generator Repair in Ikeja | Same-Day Service | [Business Name]"

A few rules make them work:

1. **Put the main keyword near the front.** It helps both readers and search engines.
2. **Make it specific.** "SEO Services" is vague. "SEO Services for Hotels in Lagos" says who it's for.
3. **Add a reason to click.** A benefit, a number, a year, a time frame or a clear promise, but only if it's true.
4. **Match the search intent.** If people want a guide, say "Guide". If they want to buy, show that you sell.
5. **Keep your brand at the end.** It adds trust but shouldn't push out the useful words.
6. **Make every title unique.** Duplicate titles across pages confuse search engines and users.

## A simple formula for meta descriptions

Think of a description as a tiny advert for the page. A reliable pattern:

**What the page offers + a key benefit or detail + a call to action**

Examples:

- "Not sure why your website isn't on Google? Learn how indexing works and the exact steps to fix pages that are missing. Step-by-step guide."
- "Plumber in Surulere with same-day call-outs, upfront prices and 4.8-star reviews. Call or WhatsApp us for a quick quote."
- "Compare our three SEO plans, from $500 per month. See what's included and request a free audit today."

Good descriptions:

- use plain, natural language,
- include the main keyword once, since Google bolds matching words,
- mention something specific such as a price, speed, location or proof,
- end with an action such as "Learn how", "Get a quote" or "See the plans",
- are different on every page.

## Before and after: real-style examples

Seeing the difference helps. Here are three common problems and fixes.

**Example 1: A vague home page**

- Before: "Home | ABC Ltd"
- After: "Accounting Services for Small Businesses in Abuja | ABC Ltd"

The fix says what the business does, who it's for and where, in the first words.

**Example 2: A keyword-stuffed service page**

- Before: "SEO, SEO services, best SEO company, cheap SEO, SEO Nigeria, SEO Lagos"
- After: "SEO Services in Nigeria | Clear Plans, Monthly Reports | TechTide SEO"

Stuffing looks spammy and often gets rewritten. Clarity wins.

**Example 3: A blog post with a dull title and no description**

- Before title: "Internal Links"
- After title: "Internal Linking: A Simple Guide to Better Rankings"
- After description: "Learn how internal links help Google find your pages and spread authority, with a simple structure, examples and a 30-minute routine."

## Templates you can copy by page type

Replace the words in brackets with your own details.

**Home page**
- Title: [What you do] for [who] in [place] | [Brand]
- Description: [Brand] helps [who] [achieve result]. [Proof or key service]. [Call to action].

**Service page**
- Title: [Service] in [Place] | [Key benefit] | [Brand]
- Description: [Service] for [audience] in [place]. [One strong detail]. [Call to action].

**Location or area page**
- Title: [Service] in [Area] | Local [Business type] | [Brand]
- Description: Looking for [service] in [area]? [What makes you local or fast]. [How to contact you].

**Blog post or guide**
- Title: [Topic]: [Benefit or angle] (with examples or steps)
- Description: [What the reader will learn]. [What's included]. [Hint of the outcome].

**Product page**
- Title: [Product name] | [Key feature] | [Brand]
- Description: Buy [product] with [feature or benefit]. [Price, delivery or guarantee]. [Call to action].

## How to find the pages to fix first

You don't have to rewrite everything. Start where it matters most.

1. **Use Search Console.** In the Performance report, look at pages with many impressions but few clicks. These are the pages where your listing isn't persuading people. Our [Search Console guide](/blog/how-to-use-google-search-console/) shows how to find them.

2. **Crawl your site.** A tool such as [Screaming Frog](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/) lists every title and description, and flags missing, duplicate, too-long and too-short ones.
3. **Check your most important pages first.** Home, services, location pages and top-performing posts.

## How to change them

Most platforms make this simple.

- **WordPress:** use an SEO plugin to set the title and description for each page or post.
- **Website builders:** look for "SEO settings" or "page settings".
- **Custom sites:** edit the title tag and the description meta tag in the page's head.

After you change a page, you can ask Google to refresh it by using URL inspection in Search Console. It may take days or weeks for the new listing to appear.

## How to test whether it worked

Don't expect overnight changes. Wait two to four weeks, then compare click-through rate for the same page in Search Console, before and after. Look at a similar date range, and be aware that seasons and ranking changes can also move the numbers.

If the click-through rate doesn't improve, try a different angle: a clearer benefit, a number, a question or a more specific promise. Small changes made one at a time are easier to learn from.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing titles or descriptions.** Google writes its own, and they're often worse.
- **The same title everywhere.** Every page needs its own.
- **Keyword stuffing.** It reads badly and is often rewritten.
- **Clickbait that the page doesn't deliver.** It raises clicks briefly, then raises the bounce rate and damages trust.
- **Writing for robots.** Real people read these lines.
- **Putting the brand first.** Useful words should lead.
- **Forgetting mobile.** Long titles get cut on phones, so put the key words up front.
- **Never revisiting them.** Update them when the page changes or when the click-through rate is poor.

## A quick checklist

- Is the main keyword near the start of the title?
- Is the title about 30 to 60 characters and unique?
- Does the description sum up the page and include a reason to click?
- Is the description about 120 to 155 characters and unique?
- Is everything accurate and matched to the page?
- Did you check how it looks in search a few weeks later?

## Frequently asked questions

### Are meta descriptions a ranking factor?
Google has said they aren't a direct ranking factor, but they can influence clicks, which matters for your traffic.

### Why does Google show a different title from the one I wrote?
Google may rewrite titles it considers too long, vague, repetitive or not matching the page. Make yours clear and accurate to keep it.

### Should I include my brand name in every title?
Usually yes, at the end, because it builds recognition. On pages where space is tight, put useful words first and trim the brand.

### Can I use numbers and the year in a title?
Yes, if they're true and useful. "10 steps" or "2026 guide" can raise clicks, but keep them honest and update the year when you refresh the page.

### How many characters can I use?
Titles of about 30 to 60 characters and descriptions of about 120 to 155 characters usually display fully. Google uses pixel width, so it varies.
