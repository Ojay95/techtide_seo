Almost every business that wants more customers from Google faces the same question: should we invest in SEO, pay for ads (PPC), or do both? The honest answer is that it depends on your goals, your timeline, your budget and your market. Both channels can work very well, and both can waste money when used badly.

This guide explains how SEO and PPC work, compares them across the things that matter, shows when each one makes sense and gives you a simple framework for deciding. You will also see why many successful businesses end up using both.

## What is SEO?

SEO, or search engine optimisation, is the work of earning visibility in the regular (organic) search results. It includes fixing technical problems, creating helpful pages, building trust through links and reviews, and keeping your business details consistent online. You don't pay Google for each click. You invest in your website and your reputation instead.

If you are new to the topic, start with our guide on [what SEO is and how it works](/guides/what-is-seo/).

## What is PPC?

PPC stands for pay-per-click. It usually means search advertising, such as Google Ads, where you bid to show an ad when someone searches for certain keywords, and you pay when someone clicks. Ads appear above or alongside the organic results and are labelled as ads. Google explains the basics on its [Google Ads help centre](https://support.google.com/google-ads).

## SEO vs PPC: the main differences

| | SEO | PPC |
|---|---|---|
| How you appear | Earned through relevance and trust | Bought through bids and ad quality |
| Time to first results | Weeks to months | Hours to days |
| Cost model | Ongoing work, content and tools | You pay for each click |
| When you stop | Results tend to last, though they can fade | Traffic stops when the budget stops |
| Control | Search engines decide rankings | You choose keywords, budget, locations and times |
| Best for | Steady, compounding growth | Fast testing, launches and offers |

## Speed: how soon will you see results?

PPC wins on speed. Once your campaign is approved, your ads can appear the same day and bring clicks and enquiries almost immediately. SEO takes longer because pages must be crawled, indexed and trusted. Local improvements can show within weeks, while competitive searches often take four to six months or more. Our guide on [how long SEO takes](/guides/how-long-does-seo-take/) explains realistic timelines.

If you need customers this month, PPC is the faster route. If you are building a business for years, SEO deserves a place in your plan.

## Cost: what will you actually pay?

PPC costs depend on how competitive your keywords are, how good your ads and landing pages are and how many clicks you buy. You control the budget, but each click has a price, and prices tend to be higher in competitive industries. Your cost per customer stays fairly steady as long as you keep paying.

SEO costs are mostly people and time: audits, fixes, content, links and reporting. They don't scale per click, so as your pages gain traffic, the cost per visitor tends to fall. SEO has a cost to start and a longer payback, but a lower cost per customer once it is working. ## Trust and clicks

People know the difference between ads and organic results. Many skip the ads and prefer a result that earned its place, though behaviour varies by industry and search. Ready-to-buy searches often get strong ad performance, while research searches lean towards organic pages and guides. Because buyers move between both, being visible in each place can help.

## When PPC makes sense

- You need leads quickly, for a new service, an event or a seasonal offer.
- You want to test which keywords, messages and prices convert before investing in content.
- Your market is so competitive that organic rankings will take a long time.
- You have a clear value per customer, so you know how much a click is worth.

## When SEO makes sense

- You want steady traffic that doesn't depend on a daily budget.
- Your customers research before buying, so helpful content builds trust.
- You want your cost per customer to fall over time.
- You can wait a few months for results to build.

## Why many businesses use both

PPC and SEO support each other. PPC gives you results while SEO builds. PPC data shows which keywords and messages actually bring customers, which tells you what to write and optimise for. SEO then reduces your reliance on paid clicks over time. Some businesses cut ad spend on terms where they rank well organically, while keeping ads on high-value searches.

## A simple framework to decide

1. **Goals:** do you need results this month, or growth over the next year?
2. **Value:** what is a new customer worth? If a customer is worth far more than the cost of a click, PPC can be profitable early.
3. **Competition:** search your main keywords and look at who appears. If the first page is full of large brands, plan for a longer SEO timeline.
4. **Budget and capacity:** can you fund PPC every month, and produce or buy content for SEO?
5. **Tracking:** can you measure calls, form leads and sales? You need tracking for either channel to be worthwhile.

A hypothetical example: a local dentist wants new patients quickly and plans to stay in business for decades. Running a small PPC campaign for urgent searches such as "emergency dentist", while building local SEO and helpful content for the long term, gives both speed and durability.

## Common mistakes

- Judging SEO after four weeks, or giving up on PPC before testing ad copy and landing pages.
- Sending PPC traffic to a slow or confusing page.
- Measuring clicks and rankings rather than calls, leads and sales.
- Treating them as rivals instead of tools that can work together.
- Buying cheap bulk packages for either channel.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SEO better than PPC?
Neither is better in every case. SEO is usually better for long-term, lower-cost growth, and PPC is usually better for speed and testing.

### Does running ads help my SEO?
Not directly. Ads don't improve organic rankings. They can, however, bring traffic and show which searches convert.

### Can I do SEO and PPC on a small budget?
Yes, but focus on one thing at a time. A small, well-targeted campaign or a few high-impact SEO fixes beat spreading a tiny budget everywhere.

### What if I only have budget for one?
If you need results soon, start with PPC. If you can wait and want lasting growth, start with SEO. Review after three to six months.
