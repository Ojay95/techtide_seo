"How much does SEO cost?" is the first question almost every business owner asks, and the answer most agencies give is "it depends". That's true, and it's also deeply unhelpful. If you're trying to plan a budget, you need more than a shrug.

So let's be straight. SEO prices vary widely, because the work varies widely. A single-location shop in a quiet market needs a very different plan from a multi-branch company in a crowded one. But "it depends" isn't a mystery. It depends on a short list of things you can understand, and once you do, you can read any quote with confidence.

This guide explains how SEO is priced in Nigeria and for Nigerian businesses working with international agencies, what drives the cost, what a fair quote includes, how to compare options and where businesses waste money. We don't invent market averages. Instead, we show you how to judge value.

## First, what you're actually paying for

SEO isn't a product. It's a set of services delivered over time. When you pay for SEO, you're usually paying for some mix of:

- **Auditing and strategy:** finding what's wrong and planning what to do.
- **Technical fixes:** speed, indexing, structure, mobile.
- **On-page work:** titles, headings, page content and internal links.
- **Local SEO:** your Google Business Profile, citations, reviews.
- **Content:** researching, writing and publishing pages and articles.
- **Link building:** earning mentions and links from other sites.
- **Reporting and management:** tracking results and keeping the plan on course.

Our guide to [what SEO is](/guides/what-is-seo/) explains each part. The more of these a plan includes, and the more of each you need, the higher the price.

## The main pricing models

Agencies and freelancers usually charge in one of four ways.

### 1. Monthly retainer

You pay a fixed fee each month for an agreed scope of work. This is the most common model for ongoing SEO, because results build over months. It's predictable and aligns with steady, continuous work.

**Watch for:** a retainer with a vague scope. You should know what you get each month.

### 2. One-off project

A fixed price for a defined job, such as an SEO audit, a technical clean-up or a website migration. Good for a clear, limited need.

**Watch for:** unclear deliverables, and projects that quietly need ongoing work to succeed.

### 3. Hourly or day rate

You pay for time. It suits consulting, training or small fixes.

**Watch for:** open-ended hours with no cap or estimate.

### 4. Performance-based pricing

Payment depends on results, such as rankings or leads. It sounds attractive but is tricky: rankings aren't fully controllable, and "results" can be defined in ways that favour the agency. Be cautious, and read the terms carefully. We don't guarantee rankings, because no honest agency can.

## What drives the price

### Competition

Ranking for a quiet, local search is far easier than for a crowded national one. More competition means more content, more links and more time.

### Size of your website

A 15-page site is cheaper to fix and grow than a 1,500-page shop. More pages mean more to audit, fix and maintain.

### Scope of work

A plan that includes technical fixes, monthly content and link building costs more than one limited to reporting and a few fixes. Compare what's included, not only the number.

### Number of locations

Each location needs its own profile, pages, reviews and citations. Five branches is roughly five times the local work.

### Your starting point

A site with serious technical problems needs repair before it can grow. A healthy site with good content can move faster.

### Who does the work

Experience costs more, and it usually saves time and mistakes. Cheap work from beginners often needs redoing.

## What about naira and dollars?

For Nigerian businesses, currency matters. Many agencies and tools price in US dollars, and the naira cost of a dollar price changes with the exchange rate. When you get a quote, ask:

- **Which currency is it priced in?**
- **Can you pay in naira, and at what rate?** Is the rate fixed when you sign, or does it change monthly?
- **Are there transfer or payment fees?**
- **Are taxes included?**

Put the answers in the agreement, so there are no surprises on the invoice.

## Costs people forget

The agency fee isn't always the full picture. Budget for:

- **Tools:** many are free, such as Google Search Console, but some paid tools cost monthly.
- **Content help:** photos, videos, case studies and product information you'll need to supply.
- **Website changes:** a developer for fixes the agency can't make, or a redesign if the site is too weak.
- **Advertising:** SEO and paid ads are separate budgets. See [SEO vs PPC](/blog/seo-vs-ppc-which-is-right-for-your-business/).
- **Your time:** approvals, information and decisions.

## How to compare quotes properly

When you have two or three quotes, put them side by side. Compare scope, not only the price.

![Screenshot: a spreadsheet comparing three agency quotes by fee, term, deliverables, reporting and account ownership](/images/screenshots/seo-quote-comparison-sheet.png)

Compare these things:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What exactly is included each month? | You can't judge value without scope |
| Is an audit included? | It shows how well they understand your site |
| How are links built? | Low-quality link packages can harm you |
| What does reporting cover? | Leads and calls matter more than rankings alone |
| Who owns the accounts and content? | You should own everything |
| What's the minimum term and notice? | Avoid long lock-ins |
| Who does the work? | Experience affects quality |

## Red flags in pricing

- **Guaranteed rankings or "page one in 30 days".** Nobody controls Google.
- **Very low prices for "full SEO".** Often built on bulk links and automated work.
- **No written scope.** You won't know what you're paying for.
- **Long contracts with heavy penalties.** Be careful, especially with a new agency.
- **Agency-owned accounts.** You could lose your data if you leave.
- **Pressure to sign immediately.**

## Is SEO worth the cost? A simple way to think about it

Don't ask "is it expensive?" Ask "what is a new customer worth to me?"

Here's an **illustrative example**, with made-up numbers. Suppose a service business earns an average profit of ₦50,000 from each new customer, and a monthly SEO plan costs the equivalent of ₦400,000. If SEO brings 12 extra customers a month, that's ₦600,000 in profit. After the fee, you're ahead, and the pages, reviews and links you've built keep working.

Use your own numbers. Work out your average profit per customer, then ask how many extra customers you'd need each month to cover the fee. If that number is realistic for your market, SEO may be a good investment. If it isn't, start smaller.

DIY isn't free. It costs your time, usually several hours a week, plus any tools and learning. If you enjoy it and have the time, it's a good way to start, and our guides will help. If you don't, hiring someone costs money but saves hours. Be honest about what your time is worth.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does SEO cost per month in Nigeria?
It depends on scope, competition and who does the work. Our plans start from $500 per month. Be wary of both extremely low and unexplained high prices, and compare scope and reporting.

### Is cheap SEO bad?
Not always, but very cheap offers often rely on low-quality links and automated work that can harm your site. Check exactly what's included.

### Can I pay once and be done?
Some tasks, such as an audit or a technical clean-up, are one-off. Ongoing results usually need ongoing work, because content ages and competitors keep improving.

### How long before I see results?
Local improvements can show in weeks, and competitive searches take months. See [how long SEO takes](/guides/how-long-does-seo-take/).

### Should I pay in naira or dollars?
Whichever your agreement states, but clarify the exchange rate, fees and timing before you sign, so the real cost is predictable.

### What should I do first?
Set a goal, calculate what a customer is worth and get at least two written quotes with clear scope.
