Think about how you choose a hotel. You search, scroll through a few options, check the photos, glance at the rating and compare prices. At some point you click, and you end up on either the hotel's own website or a booking platform's page.

For the hotel, that click is worth a lot more when it lands on its own site. A direct booking avoids commission, gives you the guest's contact details and lets you tell your own story. The challenge is that booking platforms spend huge budgets to show up first, and most independent hotels can't match that.

The good news is that you don't need to. Hotel SEO is about making your own website and Google presence the easiest, most trustworthy choice for the guests who are already looking for what you offer. This guide covers what to fix, in what order, with a 90-day plan you can follow.

## Why hotel SEO is different

Hotels sit at the crossroads of several kinds of search.

- **Local searches:** "boutique hotel in Ikeja", "hotel near the airport".
- **Travel searches:** "where to stay in Lagos for a weekend".
- **Hotel name searches:** people who already know you and want to book.
- **Experience searches:** "hotel with a pool", "romantic getaway".

Each one needs something slightly different from your site and your Google profile. Hotels are also heavy on photos, prices and availability, which makes speed, mobile design and structured data especially important.

## Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile right

For hotels, the profile is the front door. Google shows it for "hotels near me" and hotel name searches, with photos, rating, location and contact details.

- Choose the **right primary category** (for example "Hotel" or a more specific type such as "Boutique hotel", if offered). Our [profile guide](/guides/google-business-profile-optimization/) explains categories.
- Complete the **hotel attributes**: Wi-Fi, parking, pool, breakfast, airport shuttle, accessibility, pets.
- Add **many real photos**: exterior, lobby, every room type, bathrooms, dining, pool, views.
- Add your **website link** and **booking link**, with tracking so you can see visits.
- Keep your **name, address and phone number** identical everywhere. See [local citations](/blog/local-citations-explained/).
- **Reply to every review.**

Avoid the common mistakes, such as keyword-stuffed names or old numbers. Our guide on [Business Profile mistakes](/blog/google-business-profile-mistakes/) lists them.

## Step 2: Build one strong page per room type

Many hotel sites have one page called "Rooms" with a list. That's easy to build, but it doesn't match the way people search or decide. Give each room type its own page.

Each room page should include:

- **A clear title:** "Deluxe Room in Ikeja | Harbourview Boutique Hotel".
- **Photos** from several angles, compressed so the page loads fast.
- **The price**, or "from" price, and what's included.
- **Key features:** size, bed type, view, amenities, breakfast.
- **Policies:** cancellation, check-in and check-out times.
- **A booking button** that's visible without scrolling, especially on mobile.
- **A short FAQ:** "Is airport pickup available?", "Can I cancel?"
- **Links** to related rooms, offers and your location page.

## Step 2b: Don't forget the other pages

Beyond rooms, a hotel site needs pages that capture different searches:

- **Home page:** who you are, where you are and why guests choose you.
- **Location or area page:** "Hotel near Ikeja City Mall", "hotel close to the airport", with directions and distances.
- **Offers and packages:** weekend deals, long stays, corporate rates.
- **Dining, events and meetings:** weddings, conferences and restaurants bring separate searches.
- **Things to do nearby:** guides to local attractions and areas, written from real knowledge. This brings in travellers who haven't chosen a hotel yet.
- **FAQ and policies.**

Use clear titles and descriptions for each. Our guide to [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-and-meta-descriptions-that-get-clicks/) has templates you can adapt.

## Step 3: Make your price show up on Google

Google shows hotel prices in its travel results, often comparing the hotel's official site with booking platforms. Hotels that supply accurate rates and availability can appear there with a link to their own site.

How you connect depends on your booking engine or channel manager, because many of them have integrations. Google's [Hotel Center help](https://support.google.com/hotelprices) explains how hotels and their technology partners send prices and availability. Ask your booking provider whether it supports this, and check that your official-site price is accurate and competitive.

## Step 4: Speed and mobile booking

Most hotel searches now happen on phones, and a slow, clumsy booking journey loses guests, however good your SEO is.

- Compress photos. Hotel sites are often the heaviest on the web.
- Use modern image formats and lazy loading below the fold.
- Make the booking button large and obvious.
- Test the whole journey on a phone: find a room, choose dates, pay.
- Keep the booking engine fast and on a secure connection.

Our guides on [mobile SEO](/blog/mobile-seo-guide/) and [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/) show how to test and fix this.

## Step 5: Reviews that build trust

Guests read reviews on Google and travel platforms, and a hotel with many recent, positive, well-answered reviews feels safer.

- **Ask every guest**, ideally after checkout, with a direct link by email or message.
- **Reply to all reviews**, thanking happy guests and handling problems calmly and specifically.
- **Fix recurring issues.** Reviews show what to improve.
- **Never buy reviews** or offer incentives that break platform rules.
- **Show highlights** on your website, with permission.

Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) includes ready-made messages.

## Step 6: Add hotel structured data

Structured data helps search engines understand your property. Schema.org offers a [Hotel type](https://schema.org/Hotel) with fields for name, address, star rating, amenities, check-in times, price range and more. Add it to your home page, and use Organization, Breadcrumb and Article markup where it fits. Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) explains how to add and test it.

## Step 7: Write for guests, and for different countries

Hotels often serve guests from several places. A business traveller from Abuja, a family visiting from the UK and a diaspora couple returning for a wedding all search differently.

- **Use clear English** and local terms guests know.
- **Show prices in the currencies guests expect**, or at least explain your rate currency.
- **Explain how to reach you** from the airport, with times and options.
- **Consider language or country versions** if a large share of guests come from elsewhere. Our guide to [international SEO and hreflang](/blog/international-seo-hreflang/) shows how.

## Step 7b: Local links and mentions

Mentions from other trusted sites show you're established. Think of:

- tourism boards and city guides,
- event venues, wedding vendors and corporate partners,
- local news and lifestyle blogs,
- airline, tour and car-hire partners,
- relevant directories and review sites.

Quality beats quantity. See [how to get backlinks safely](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/).

## Step 8: Measure what matters

Track:

- **Direct bookings and booking enquiries** from search,
- **Calls, directions and website clicks** from your Google profile,
- **Clicks and impressions** for room and location pages,
- **Share of bookings by channel**, direct versus platforms,
- **Reviews and rating.**

Our guides on [SEO metrics that matter](/blog/seo-metrics-that-matter/) and [tracking SEO results](/blog/track-seo-results-ga4-search-console/) show how.

## Mistakes hotels make

- **One long "Rooms" page for everything.**
- **Huge, uncompressed photos** that slow the site.
- **Hidden or confusing booking buttons.**
- **Inconsistent prices** between your site and platforms.
- **Old addresses and phone numbers** across listings.
- **Ignoring Google reviews.**
- **No pages about the area** or experience searches.
- **Poor mobile booking.**
- **Copying text from a booking platform** or other hotels.

## A 90-day hotel SEO plan

**Days 1 to 30: foundation.** Complete your Google profile and photos. Fix mobile speed. Make sure every room type has its own page with a visible booking button. Set up Search Console and conversion tracking.

**Days 31 to 60: trust and visibility.** Start collecting reviews and replying to every one. Clean up listings. Add Hotel and Organization structured data. Check that your rates appear correctly in Google's travel results.

**Days 61 to 90: growth.** Publish an area guide and an offers page. Ask partners and local organisations to link to you. Review your numbers and improve the pages that get impressions but few clicks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does hotel SEO take?
Profile and page improvements can show within weeks. Competitive searches, such as "hotels in Lagos", usually take several months of steady work.

### Should I stop using booking platforms?
Not necessarily. They bring guests. The aim is to grow the share of direct bookings, not to disappear from platforms.

### Do I need a blog?
Helpful area guides and travel advice can bring in guests early in their search. Start with strong room and location pages first.

### How do I compete with big booking sites?
You won't outspend them, but you can win on specifics: your name, your location, your rooms, your reviews and your best direct offer.

### Can small hotels do this themselves?
Yes. The Google profile, room pages, reviews and speed fixes are all doable. Bring in help for technical work and ongoing strategy.
