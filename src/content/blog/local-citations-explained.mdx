When a customer searches for a business, Google doesn't rely only on the business's own website. It looks across the web for signs that the business is real, where it is and how it can be contacted. Local citations are some of those signs. Get them right and you reinforce your credibility everywhere. Get them wrong and you create doubt, with different phone numbers, old addresses and duplicate listings spread across the internet.

This guide explains what local citations are, why consistency matters, the types you should know, and a clear, step-by-step process to audit, fix and build them. You will also find a tracking sheet layout, advice for special situations and the mistakes to avoid.

## What is a local citation?

A local citation is any online mention of your business's name, address and phone number, often shortened to NAP. It may include your website, opening hours and categories too. Citations appear in:

- business directories and review sites,
- map and navigation platforms,
- social media profiles,
- industry and trade association websites,
- local chamber of commerce and community listings,
- news articles, blogs and event pages.

A citation does not always include a link. Even an unlinked mention of your name, address and phone helps search engines confirm who you are.

## Why citations matter

Google's own advice on local ranking highlights three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. You can read it in [how to improve your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). Prominence reflects how well known a business is across the web, and citations contribute by showing that your details appear consistently in many trustworthy places.

Citations help you in three practical ways:

1. **Consistency builds trust.** Matching details across sources reduce doubt about who you are.
2. **They help customers.** Correct details in directories and maps mean customers reach you, not a dead phone number.
3. **They create discovery routes.** Many customers find businesses through directories, maps and apps rather than search alone.

Citations are not a magic ranking trick. They support a strong profile, reviews, relevant pages and links, which our [complete local SEO guide](/guides/local-seo-guide/) covers.

## NAP consistency: what "matching" means

Your details should be identical wherever possible.

| Detail | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Use your real trading name, exactly as on your signage and website | Add keywords or slogans |
| Address | Use one format for street, area, city and country | Mix abbreviations or old addresses |
| Phone | Use the same number, in a consistent format with country code for international audiences | Use call-tracking numbers inconsistently |
| Website | Use one preferred address | Use different versions or tracking links |

Small variations such as "Street" versus "St." are usually tolerated, but the closer to identical, the better.

## Types of citations

- **Core platforms:** Google Business Profile, [Apple Business Connect](https://businessconnect.apple.com/), [Bing Places](https://www.bingplaces.com/), Facebook and other major social platforms.
- **General directories:** large business directories that list companies of all kinds.
- **Industry directories:** sites specific to your sector, such as trade associations or professional registers.
- **Local and community listings:** chambers of commerce, business networks, local news sites and event pages.
- **Data aggregators:** companies that supply business data to other platforms. Availability differs by country.

Quality matters more than quantity. A handful of accurate, relevant, well-known listings beats hundreds of low-quality ones.

## Step 1: Decide your official details

Write down the exact name, address, phone number, website, opening hours, categories and a short description you will use everywhere. Match them to your Google Business Profile and website. Keep this in a shared document so everyone uses the same version.

If you serve customers at their location rather than at your premises, follow Google's guidelines for service-area businesses, which allow you to hide the address on your profile. Check the current rules in [Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business).

## Step 2: Audit existing listings

Find where your business already appears.

1. Search for your business name, plus each old name, in quotation marks.
2. Search for old and current phone numbers and addresses.
3. Check the major platforms directly.
4. Write down every listing, with its link and what is wrong.

Pay special attention if you have moved, rebranded or changed phone numbers, because old details often linger.

## Step 3: Fix and merge

- Correct wrong details on each listing, claiming it first where necessary.
- Ask the platform to remove or merge duplicates. Duplicates are especially damaging on the major platforms.
- Update old addresses, closed branches and previous names.
- Don't create new duplicate listings in an attempt to replace old ones.

## Step 4: Build the important listings

Start with the platforms that matter most for your market, then add relevant specialist listings. For each one:

1. Claim or create the listing.
2. Fill in every field, including categories, hours, photos and description.
3. Use your official details exactly.
4. Add a link to your website.
5. Verify the listing if the platform requires it.

If you target several countries, claim the leading directories for each market, such as local business registers and national directories, so that your presence matches where your customers search.

## Step 5: Track everything

Keep a simple spreadsheet so the work stays organised and repeatable.

## Special situations

**Moving or rebranding.** Update your Google Business Profile first, then your website, then every listing on your tracker. Keep the old details in mind when searching for duplicates.

**Multiple locations.** Each location needs its own listings with its own address and phone number. Link each listing to a matching location page on your website.

**Virtual offices and shared spaces.** Don't use an address where you don't operate, because platforms may reject or suspend listings.

**Call tracking.** If you use tracking numbers, make sure the main number appears consistently everywhere and use tracking numbers only where properly configured.

## Common mistakes

- Using different business names or phone numbers in different places.
- Leaving old addresses live after a move.
- Paying for hundreds of low-quality directory submissions.
- Creating duplicate listings.
- Never checking listings after they are created.
- Forgetting which email controls each listing.

## Measuring the impact

Citations are a foundation, so judge them by what they support. Watch your profile actions (calls, directions, website clicks), your visibility in the map results, the accuracy of your details across platforms and the number of wrong contacts or calls from outdated information.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are citations still important?
They remain a useful foundation for consistency and discovery, though they are one part of local SEO alongside your profile, reviews, content and links.

### Do citations need a link to my website?
No, but a link adds value and gives customers a path to your site.

### How many citations do I need?
There is no number. Focus on accurate listings on the main platforms and the directories relevant to your industry and location.

### Should I pay for citation services?
Be careful. Some tools save time with distribution and monitoring, but avoid anything that promises hundreds of listings for a low price.

### What if a listing I can't claim has wrong details?
Use the platform's suggest-an-edit or report feature, or contact support to claim or correct it.
