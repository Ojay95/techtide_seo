Take out your phone and open your own website. Don't look at it like the owner. Look at it like a customer who's standing in a queue, on mobile data, with one thumb free.

Can you read the text without zooming? Can you tap the menu without hitting the wrong thing? Does the page load before you lose patience? Can you find the phone number or the WhatsApp button in a second?

That ten-second test tells you more about your SEO than many reports do, because Google mostly looks at the mobile version of your pages when it decides how to rank them. If your site is awkward on a phone, you have a ranking problem and a customer problem at the same time.

This guide explains mobile SEO in plain language. You'll learn what it means, how to test your site, the fixes that matter most and a simple checklist. You don't need to be technical.

## What mobile SEO means

Mobile SEO is making sure your website works well for people on phones, and for the search engine that visits it as a phone. It isn't a separate kind of SEO. It's the same goal, helping the right people find and use your site, with extra attention to small screens, touch and slower connections.

The key idea is **mobile-first indexing**. Google explains in its [mobile-first indexing guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing) that it primarily uses the mobile version of a page's content to index and rank it. If something is missing or broken on your mobile version, Google may not see it.

## Why it matters, especially in Nigeria

For many Nigerian businesses, the majority of visitors arrive on phones. Some are on older devices. Many are on mobile data, where every megabyte has a price and connections can be patchy. That changes what "good" looks like:

- **Light pages win.** A page that loads quickly on weak data keeps the visitor.
- **Taps beat typing.** A call button or WhatsApp link is easier than filling in a form.
- **Clear text matters.** Cramped layouts and tiny buttons cause mistakes and abandoned visits.

Even for businesses with international customers, a phone is often the first screen they use. See our guide to [SEO in Nigeria](/blog/seo-in-nigeria-practical-guide/) for more on the local context.

## How to test your site on mobile

You don't need special tools to start.

**1. Use a real phone.** Open your home page, a service page and your contact page. Try to do what a customer would do.

**2. Check in your browser.** On a computer, [Chrome DevTools has a device mode](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/device-mode) that shows your site at phone sizes. It's handy for quick checks.

**3. Run PageSpeed Insights.** Enter your page at [pagespeed.web.dev](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and read the mobile results first. Our guide to [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/) explains each score.

**4. Look in Search Console.** The Core Web Vitals report groups your pages by mobile performance and tells you which ones need work.

## The fixes that matter most

### 1. Use a responsive design

A responsive site adjusts its layout to the screen. Most modern themes and builders do this. Check that yours really does, and that nothing is cut off or requires sideways scrolling.

Make sure your pages include the viewport meta tag, which tells the browser how to scale the page. Without it, phones may show a tiny desktop version.

![Screenshot: the same page shown as a desktop layout and as a mobile layout with large tap targets](/images/screenshots/responsive-comparison.png)

*Figure 3: The same page, rearranged for a phone with bigger buttons and a one-tap chat link.*

### 2. Make text readable

Body text should be comfortable to read without zooming. As a rule of thumb, use about 16 pixels for body text, with enough line spacing and strong contrast. Avoid long, dense paragraphs.

### 3. Make buttons and links easy to tap

Fingers are bigger than mouse pointers. Make buttons large, at least 44 to 48 pixels high, and leave space between links so people don't tap the wrong one. Put your main call to action where a thumb can reach it.

### 4. Keep the same content on mobile and desktop

Because Google mainly uses the mobile version, important text, images, headings, structured data and links should be present on mobile too. Don't hide key content behind features that don't work on phones.

### 5. Speed up mobile loading

Slow pages lose visitors fast. The biggest wins are usually:

- **Compress and resize images.** Use modern formats such as WebP.
- **Remove unneeded plugins and scripts.**
- **Turn on caching.**
- **Use good hosting.**
- **Don't load heavy videos or sliders at the top of the page.**

See the speed section of our [technical SEO for beginners](/blog/technical-seo-for-beginners/) for the steps.

### 6. Avoid pop-ups that block the page

Large pop-ups that cover the content are frustrating on a small screen. If you need a banner, such as a cookie notice, keep it small and easy to close.

### 7. Make contact effortless

Add click-to-call and click-to-chat buttons. On mobile, a person who wants to reach you should be able to do it in one tap. See [local SEO for Nigerian businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-nigerian-businesses/) for WhatsApp tips.

### 8. Simplify forms

Typing on a phone is slow. Ask only for what you need, use the right keyboard type for each field (number, email), and allow autofill.

### 9. Check your menus

A menu that works on desktop may be cramped on mobile. Use a clear menu icon, keep the number of items small and make sure every item is easy to tap. Our own site uses a simple menu button for this reason.

### 10. Keep layout stable

Pages that jump around while loading cause mis-taps. Set sizes for images and reserve space for banners so content doesn't shift. That's what the Cumulative Layout Shift score measures.

## Mobile SEO for local businesses

If customers find you through Google Maps, they're nearly always on a phone. Make sure:

- your Google Business Profile has accurate hours, phone number and photos,
- your website's contact page works well on mobile,
- your address has a landmark or directions,
- your phone number is clickable.

Our [Google Business Profile guide](/guides/google-business-profile-optimization/) explains the details.

## Common mobile mistakes

- **Designing only on a desktop and hoping for the best.** Always check on a real phone.
- **Huge images straight from a camera.** Resize and compress.
- **Tiny text and tight links.** Frustrating and easy to get wrong.
- **Hiding content on mobile.** Google may not see it.
- **Forgetting the viewport tag.** The page won't scale correctly.
- **Blocking CSS or scripts.** Google then can't render the page properly.
- **Using pop-ups everywhere.** They drive people away.

## A mobile SEO checklist

- Does the site look right at small sizes, with no sideways scrolling?
- Is body text easy to read without zooming?
- Are buttons and links large and spaced out?
- Does the mobile page contain the same important content as desktop?
- Does the page load quickly on mobile data?
- Are phone and chat links one tap away?
- Do forms work smoothly?
- Is Core Web Vitals in Search Console mostly green on mobile?

## A 30-minute mobile audit you can do today

If you only have half an hour, do this.

1. **Minutes 0 to 5:** open your home page, your top service page and your contact page on a real phone. Note anything awkward.
2. **Minutes 5 to 10:** run each page through PageSpeed Insights and write down the mobile scores.
3. **Minutes 10 to 15:** try to complete your main goal, such as calling, sending a message or booking, using one thumb. Count the taps.
4. **Minutes 15 to 20:** open the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console and note any pages marked poor.
5. **Minutes 20 to 25:** check your Google Business Profile on the Maps app. Is the phone number tappable and the information right?
6. **Minutes 25 to 30:** write the three biggest problems, and fix the quickest one first.

Repeat every month, and after any redesign. Small, regular checks catch problems before customers do.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a separate mobile website?
No. A single responsive site is the usual and best choice. Separate mobile sites are harder to maintain and can cause problems.

### Does Google only index the mobile version?
Google mainly uses the mobile version for indexing and ranking. A desktop-only feature may not count.

### Is AMP required?
No. Accelerated Mobile Pages aren't required for good mobile SEO. A fast, responsive page is enough.

### How can I tell if my site is mobile-friendly?
Test it on a real phone, check PageSpeed Insights and review the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console.

### What is the single most important mobile fix?
Usually speed. A fast, readable page keeps visitors long enough to contact you.
