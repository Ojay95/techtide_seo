Try this experiment. Open ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity and ask: "Which company should I hire for [what you sell] in [your city]?" Then see who gets named.

Maybe you're there. Maybe a competitor is. Maybe the answer is a list of companies you've never heard of, or a made-up one that doesn't exist. Whatever happens, one thing is clear: your customers are asking these questions, and the answers are shaping who gets the call.

The honest truth is that nobody can pay for a mention or guarantee one. But you can do a lot to make it easier for AI tools to find you, understand you and describe you correctly. This guide shows how, step by step, in plain language.

## First, understand where AI answers come from

AI assistants don't know things the way a person does. Their answers come from three places.

1. **What the model learned in training.** This is a huge snapshot of text from the past. It can be out of date, and small or new businesses are often missing from it.
2. **What it finds when it searches the web.** Many assistants can look up pages and sources live, then write an answer with links. Perplexity does this by default, and ChatGPT and Gemini do it when search is switched on or needed.
3. **What the person tells it.** If a user pastes in a list or a website, the assistant uses that.

For most businesses, the second source is where you have the most influence in the short term. If your business is clearly described on pages that search tools can find, and several trusted places say the same things about you, you give the assistant what it needs.

## Step 1: Test what AI says about you today

Before you change anything, find your starting point. Open two or three assistants and ask questions your real customers would ask. Use plain wording, the way people talk.

Here are some prompts to adapt:

- "What is the best [service] company in [city or country]?"
- "Who offers [service] for [type of customer] in [place]?"
- "How much does [service] cost in [place], and who can I ask?"
- "Tell me about [your business name]."
- "Is [your business name] a good company?"
- "Compare [your business name] with [a competitor]."

Ask each one in a fresh chat, so earlier answers don't affect the result. For every answer, write down:

- whether you were mentioned,
- how you were described, and whether it was accurate,
- which competitors appeared,
- which websites were linked or cited.

Put it all in a spreadsheet with the date. That's your baseline.

Don't be discouraged if you're missing. Small and newer businesses usually are. The point is to learn what the tools see and where the gaps are.

## Step 2: Find the sources AI trusts in your category

Now look closely at the sources listed in those answers. They're a map of where to be. You'll often find a mix of:

- review and rating sites,
- business directories,
- "best of" and comparison articles,
- news and magazine pieces,
- forums and question-and-answer sites,
- company websites and Google Business Profiles.

If the same sites keep appearing, those are the ones that matter for your category. For example, an SEO agency might find directories like [Clutch](https://clutch.co), roundup articles and local business lists. A restaurant might find review platforms and food blogs.

Make a short list of the ten sources that appear most. That's your target list.

## Step 3: Get properly listed on those sources

For each site on your list, ask: can I be listed here honestly? Many have a free or paid profile, and some let customers leave reviews. Do it properly:

- **Claim the profile** and fill in every field.
- **Use the same description, name, address and phone number** you use everywhere else.
- **Add photos, services and links** to your website.
- **Invite real customers to leave reviews**, without paying for them or pressuring them.

For roundup articles and lists, you can't just add yourself. You can contact the writer politely, explain what you do and offer something useful, such as a quote, data or a case study. Never offer to pay for a review in a way that isn't disclosed. Honest outreach takes longer, but it lasts.

## Step 4: Make your own website easy to understand

Your website is the one source you control completely, so make it clear.

**Say what you do, plainly.** On your home page and About page, state in one or two sentences who you are, where you're based, what you offer and who it's for. "TechTide SEO is an SEO agency based in Ikeja, Lagos, helping businesses in Nigeria and worldwide get found on Google" is much easier to quote than "We craft digital growth journeys".

**Answer the questions customers ask.** Add pages and sections for pricing, process, timelines and comparisons. Use question-style headings. Put the direct answer first.

**Show your proof.** Case studies, results with real numbers (when you have permission), testimonials and credentials all help.

**Add structured data.** Organization and LocalBusiness markup tell search engines your name, address, contact details and what you do. Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) walks you through it.

**Keep pages crawlable and fast.** If bots can't read your pages, they can't use them. See our guide on [ranking in AI search](/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-search-geo-aeo/) for the technical checks.

## Step 5: Build a presence in Google and Bing

Many AI tools rely on search indexes, so be visible in the main ones.

- Set up [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) and submit your sitemap.
- Complete your [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com).
- Add your site to [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters) and claim your Bing Places listing. You can import your site from Search Console in a few clicks.
- Keep your information identical across all of them.

Our guide on [local citations](/blog/local-citations-explained/) shows how to keep details consistent.

## Step 6: Stay consistent everywhere

AI tools get confused by contradictions. If one site says you're in Lagos, another says Abuja, and a third lists an old phone number, the tool may leave you out or describe you wrongly.

Create one short "fact sheet" with:

- your exact business name,
- a one-sentence description,
- your address, phone number and email,
- your main services,
- your founding year, if you want to share it,
- links to your official profiles.

Use it every time you create or update a listing. Consistency is dull, but it works.

## Step 7: Create things worth mentioning

Assistants love to cite sources that offer something specific. Think about what you can publish that others will reference:

- a price guide for your market,
- a short report from your own customer data,
- a free checklist or template,
- a clear comparison, written fairly,
- a detailed case study.

Make it easy to find, give it its own page, and promote it to people who write about your field. Useful, original resources attract links and mentions over time.

## Step 8: Fix wrong or outdated information

Sometimes AI says something incorrect about your business: the wrong location, an old service, a closed branch. Don't ignore it. Find where the wrong fact might come from, and correct it at the source: your own site, directories, old press articles and profiles. Then update your website to state the correct fact clearly. Check again after a few weeks.

Most assistants also have a feedback option for answers. It's worth using, though changes through feedback alone are slow and unreliable.

## Step 9: Re-test every month

Repeat your prompt tests monthly with the same questions. Look at the trend: are you mentioned more often? Is the description more accurate? Are new sources appearing? Adjust your list of sources and keep improving the ones that matter.

Keep your expectations realistic. Answers change from day to day and from person to person, and tools update often. One good or bad result tells you very little. Patterns over several months tell you a lot.

## What not to do

- **Don't buy fake reviews or fake mentions.** It breaks platform rules and can damage your reputation.
- **Don't flood forums and social sites with promotion.** Join conversations to help, and be open about who you are.
- **Don't hide text for AI on your pages.** Content aimed at machines and invisible to people can be treated as spam. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) are a good guide to what to avoid.
- **Don't trust anyone promising guaranteed AI rankings.** Nobody controls these answers.

If your business is based in Nigeria or another market where fewer websites and directories exist online, you may find AI answers rely on a small number of sources. That's an opportunity. Being properly listed, clearly described and consistently mentioned can make a real difference. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pay ChatGPT to recommend my business?
No. ChatGPT's answers aren't a paid placement. Any company claiming it can sell you guaranteed mentions in organic AI answers should be treated with caution.

### Why does the AI mention a competitor and not me?
Usually, that competitor is described more clearly or in more places that the tool trusts. Study the sources it cites and compare.

### How often do AI answers change?
Often. The tools, their search sources and your competitors all change. That's why monthly tracking matters more than a single test.

### Does this work for a brand-new business?
It takes longer, because there's little written about you yet. Start by building the basics: a clear website, profiles, reviews and a few quality mentions.

### What if the AI says something false about me?
Find where the wrong information comes from, fix it at the source, state the correct fact clearly on your site and use the assistant's feedback option. Then re-test.
