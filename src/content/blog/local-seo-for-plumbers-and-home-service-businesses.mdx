It's 9 pm. A pipe has burst under a kitchen sink in Maryland, and water is spreading across the floor. The homeowner turns off what she can, grabs her phone and types "plumber near me". She doesn't browse. She doesn't compare five websites. She taps the first business that looks real, has good reviews and shows a phone number, and she calls. If nobody answers, she calls the next one.

That's the world of home service businesses. Plumbers, electricians, AC technicians, cleaners, painters, pest controllers, generator mechanics and movers all share it. Customers search when something is wrong or when a job has to be done soon. They're worried, they're in a hurry and they're deciding on trust, speed and price, often within a few minutes.

This makes local SEO unusually powerful for service businesses. A customer who finds you on Google at the moment of need is already convinced they need someone. They just need to believe it should be you.

This guide shows you how to build that trust and visibility, step by step. It's written for plumbers, and the same plan applies to most home service trades. You'll learn how to set up your Google profile when you travel to customers, how to build pages that win jobs, how to use WhatsApp for quotes, how to collect reviews and how to track which marketing brings in real work. There's a 90-day plan at the end.

## What makes service businesses different

Four things shape how you win customers.

**1. Customers are urgent.** Many searches are emergencies or have a deadline. Speed of response matters as much as ranking.

**2. Trust is everything.** Letting a stranger into your home or compound is a big decision. Customers worry about overcharging, shoddy work and safety.

**3. You go to the customer.** Unlike a shop, you may not have a place people visit. That changes how you set up your Google profile.

**4. Customers search by problem and place.** "Burst pipe Ikeja", "drain unblocking Yaba", "water tank installation Lekki". Your site and profile must match these.

## Step 1: Set up your Google Business Profile correctly

For most plumbers, the Google Business Profile is the main source of calls. Treat it as your shopfront.

### Decide: do customers visit you, or do you go to them?

This decision changes your setup.

- **If customers visit your workshop or showroom,** and it's staffed during the hours you list, you can show that address.
- **If you serve customers at their location,** you're a **service-area business**. You can hide your street address and list the areas you cover.

![Screenshot: the Business Profile editor for a service-area business with the address hidden and several service areas listed](/images/screenshots/service-area-business-profile-setup.png)

*Figure 2: A service-area profile hides the address and lists the areas you really serve.*

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say that your address must be a real place, and that virtual offices, PO boxes and fake locations aren't allowed. Many fake plumber listings exist, and Google removes them. Don't be tempted. A suspended profile loses all your reviews.

### Choose your categories

Your **primary category** should be the most accurate one: "Plumber". Add **secondary categories** for other services you really offer, such as drainage, water heater repair or water tank installation, where Google offers them.

### List the areas you serve

Add the neighbourhoods you genuinely cover, such as Ikeja, Maryland, Ogba and Yaba. Don't list areas you can't reach. Customers who call from there and can't be served leave bad reviews.

### Be honest about hours

If you offer emergency service, say how: "Mon to Sat, 7 am to 8 pm. Emergency line answered 24 hours." Don't claim to be open 24 hours if nobody picks up at night. Failed emergency calls cost you more than they win.

### Add services and prices

List each service with a short description and, where it helps, a price or a starting price. Services such as "Leak repair", "Drain unblocking", "Water tank installation" and "Pump repair" help Google match you to the right searches and help customers choose.

### Write a description that builds trust

Use the 750 characters well. For example: "AdeFlow Plumbing fixes leaks, blocked drains, water tanks and pumps across Ikeja, Maryland, Ogba and Yaba. Licensed technicians, upfront prices and same-day call-outs. Call or send a photo on WhatsApp for a quick quote."

### Photos that prove you're real

Add real photos: your van, your team in uniform, your tools, before-and-after shots of jobs, and your workshop if you have one. Customers want to see who might come to their home. Avoid stock images.

### Verify, and keep the details consistent

Service-area businesses are often asked to verify by video. Prepare to show your vehicle, tools, equipment and any signage or documents. Keep your name, phone number and website the same everywhere. ## Step 2: Make it effortless to call or message you

In an emergency, every extra step loses a customer. Make contacting you the easiest thing on your website and profile.

### Phone first

- Put your **phone number at the top of every page**, as a tap-to-call link.
- Add a **sticky "Call now" button** on mobile that stays on screen as people scroll.
- **Answer fast.** If you can't, call back within minutes. Many customers call two or three plumbers and hire the first one who responds.
- Consider a **missed-call routine**: a short message such as "Sorry we missed you. We'll call back in 10 minutes. You can also send a photo on WhatsApp."

### WhatsApp with photos

Many customers prefer messaging, and a photo tells you more than a long description. Add a click-to-chat link and invite customers to **send a photo of the problem**. Then reply with a price range and a time.

![Screenshot: a WhatsApp conversation where a customer sends a photo of a leaking pipe and the plumber replies with advice, a price range and an arrival time](/images/screenshots/whatsapp-photo-quote-conversation.png)

*Figure 3: A photo, a price range and a time slot turn a message into a booked job.*

A good first reply does four things:

1. **Acknowledges quickly:** "Thanks, we can see the joint is loose."
2. **Gives safe advice:** "Turn off the valve under the sink if you can."
3. **Gives a price range:** "The call-out fee is ₦8,000. This repair is usually ₦15,000 to ₦30,000, and we confirm before we start."
4. **Offers a time:** "We can be there today at 3 pm."

## Step 3: Build a page for every service you sell

Customers search for the problem they have. If your website has one page called "Services", you'll rarely match those searches. Give each service its own page.

![Diagram: a plumber at the centre with separate service pages for leak repair, drain unblocking, water tanks, pumps and boreholes, bathroom fittings and emergency call-outs](/images/diagrams/local-seo-for-plumbers-and-home-service-businesses.svg)

*Figure 4: One page for each service you sell, so each problem has a clear answer.*

Typical service pages for a plumber:

- Leak repair and burst pipes
- Drain unblocking
- Water tank installation and cleaning
- Pump and borehole installation and repair
- Bathroom and kitchen fittings
- Emergency plumbing
- Plumbing for landlords, estates and offices

### What every service page should include

![Screenshot: a plumber's service page with a heading, trust badges, before and after photos, prices, areas served and call and WhatsApp buttons](/images/screenshots/plumber-service-page-example.png)

*Figure 5: A service page that answers the big questions: what, where, how much and how to contact.*

1. **A clear title and heading:** "Leak Repair in Ikeja and Maryland | AdeFlow Plumbing".
2. **A plain explanation:** what you do, how fast you arrive and how you work.
3. **Trust signals:** licence or registration, insurance, guarantee and years of experience, **only if true**.
4. **Honest prices:** a call-out fee and typical ranges, such as "Most leak repairs ₦15,000 to ₦45,000, depending on the problem."
5. **Before-and-after photos** of real jobs.
6. **Areas served,** listed as text.
7. **Call and WhatsApp buttons,** visible without scrolling.
8. **Reviews** from customers who used that service.
9. **A short FAQ:** "How fast can you arrive?", "Do you charge a call-out fee?", "Is there a guarantee?"
10. **Links** to related services and your contact page.

Write unique titles and descriptions for each page. Our guide to [title tags and meta descriptions](/blog/title-tags-and-meta-descriptions-that-get-clicks/) has templates.

### Area pages, done properly

If you serve several areas, a page for each can help, such as "Plumber in Ikeja" and "Plumber in Yaba". But don't copy one page and swap the name. Make each one real:

- examples of jobs you've done there,
- local landmarks and streets,
- how quickly you can arrive,
- reviews from customers in that area,
- local details, such as common problems in that type of building.

If you can't write something honest and different, skip the page. Thin, copied pages are risky and rarely rank.

### Make it fast on mobile

Customers in a hurry use phones, often on weak data. Compress images, keep pages light and test on a real phone. See [mobile SEO](/blog/mobile-seo-guide/).

## Step 4: Build trust before the customer asks

A stranger is about to enter someone's home. Everything on your website and profile should lower their worry.

- **Show real people.** A team page with names, photos and roles.
- **Explain your process:** call, quote, arrive, fix, check, pay.
- **State your prices or ranges,** and say that you confirm the price before starting.
- **Show proof:** licences, insurance and any certifications, if true.
- **Offer a guarantee,** and say what it covers, if you can honour it.
- **Mention identification.** "Our technicians wear uniforms and carry ID."
- **Share real reviews and results.**
- **Be honest about limits.** If a job needs a specialist or a permit, say so.

These are the same trust signals we describe in our guide to [E-E-A-T](/blog/eeat-explained-experience-expertise-trust/). Never claim a licence, award or guarantee you can't prove.

## Step 5: Collect reviews after every job

For service businesses, reviews are the strongest proof you have, because a stranger's experience is exactly what the next customer wants to know.

1. **Ask as the job finishes,** while the customer is happy and the problem is solved.
2. **Send a direct link on WhatsApp:** "Thank you for choosing AdeFlow. If you were happy with the work, a quick Google review helps other people find us: [link]".
3. **Make it a habit.** Set a target, such as one review request per completed job.
4. **Reply to every review,** thanking the customer and mentioning the job type naturally.
5. **Handle complaints calmly.** Apologise, explain and offer to fix it. Future customers read how you respond.
6. **Never buy reviews or write your own.** Google's rules forbid it, and fake reviews are removed.

Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) includes message templates. Reviews that describe the job, such as "fixed a burst pipe the same evening", also help people searching for exactly that.

## Step 6: Publish helpful content that earns trust

You don't need a big blog. A few practical articles can bring in people before they need to call, and show you know your trade.

**Guides that match real searches:**
- "What to do when a pipe bursts" (turn off the water, switch off power if needed, call a plumber)
- "How to find and use your stopcock"
- "Why is my water pressure low?"
- "Blocked drain: what you can try and when to call a plumber"
- "How much does it cost to install a water tank?" with honest ranges and what changes the price

**Pages for property managers and landlords:**
- maintenance contracts,
- emergency response times,
- invoicing and reporting.

**Seasonal pages and posts:**
- preparing drains and gutters before heavy rains,
- cleaning tanks before the rainy season,
- checking pumps and pipes after long power cuts.

Keep it plain, practical and safe. Don't encourage dangerous DIY. Our guide to [writing SEO content](/blog/how-to-write-seo-content/) shows how to write pages people want to read, and our [E-E-A-T guide](/blog/eeat-explained-experience-expertise-trust/) explains how to show real experience.

## Step 7: Keep your listings consistent

Search engines compare your business details across the web. Use the same business name, phone number and website everywhere: your profile, website, Facebook, directories and chambers of commerce.

- Claim your listings on **quality local and trade directories**.
- Be careful with directories that sell you leads at high prices, or that show your number to competitors. Check the terms.
- Report **fake or duplicate listings** that use your name.
- If you change your phone number or name, follow our guide to [changing your business details](/blog/how-to-change-business-details-without-losing-rankings/).

## Step 8: Add structured data

Structured data helps search engines understand your business. Schema.org has a **Plumber** type (a kind of local business), and other types for electricians, HVAC contractors and more. Add fields for the name, phone number, website, opening hours, **areas served** and, if you wish, a price range.

For a service-area business without a public address, include only what's true and visible. Google's [local business structured data guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) explains the fields, and our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) shows how to add it.

## Step 9: Earn local links and referrals

Service businesses often grow through referrals, and online mentions work the same way.

- **Estate and property managers:** offer maintenance support, and ask to be listed as a recommended contractor.
- **Real estate agents and builders:** partner on handovers and repairs.
- **Hardware and plumbing supply shops:** ask to be a recommended installer.
- **Electricians, tilers and other trades:** refer each other.
- **Local business groups and chambers:** join and be listed.
- **Local blogs and news:** offer a seasonal tips article.
- **Community and school events:** sponsor or help.

Quality beats quantity. Our guide to [safe link building](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/) explains how.

## Step 10: Track jobs, not just clicks

You want phone calls and paid jobs. Track where they come from.

![Screenshot: a jobs tracker listing the date, lead source, area, job, quote, result and whether a review was requested](/images/screenshots/jobs-tracker-sheet.png)

*Figure 6: A simple tracker shows which sources bring paid jobs and where you lose quotes.*

For every enquiry, record:

- the date,
- **where it came from:** Google profile, website, WhatsApp, referral, Facebook,
- the area,
- the job,
- the quote,
- whether you **won** it,
- whether you **asked for a review**.

After a month, you'll see which sources bring jobs, which areas are strongest and where quotes are lost. Pair this with the numbers from your Google profile (calls, directions and website clicks) and your website. ## Common mistakes service businesses make

- **A fake or shared address** to look local. It breaks the rules and risks a suspended profile.
- **One "Services" page** instead of a page for each service.
- **No prices at all.** Customers fear overcharging, so ranges build trust.
- **Slow replies.** Customers hire whoever answers first.
- **No photos of real work.**
- **No review requests,** or no replies to reviews.
- **Claiming licences or guarantees** you can't prove.
- **Listing areas you don't serve.**
- **Copy-and-paste area pages.**
- **Different numbers and names** on different platforms.
- **Paying for leads without tracking** which ones turn into jobs.
- **Never checking the numbers.**

## A 90-day plan for a plumber or service business

**Days 1 to 14: foundation**
- Claim and verify your Google profile, set it up as a service-area business and list your areas.
- Add real photos, services and an honest description.
- Put your phone number and WhatsApp link on every page, with a sticky call button on mobile.

**Days 15 to 45: pages and trust**
- Build a page for each main service, with prices, photos, FAQs and trust signals.
- Add a team page and a process explanation.
- Write unique titles and descriptions.
- Add structured data.

**Days 46 to 75: reviews and proof**
- Start asking every customer for a review, with a direct link.
- Reply to all reviews.
- Publish two helpful guides, such as what to do when a pipe bursts.
- Clean up directory listings.

**Days 76 to 90: measure and grow**
- Build your jobs tracker, and review sources and win rates.
- Create one or two honest area pages with real job examples.
- Contact two or three referral partners, such as estate managers or hardware shops.
- Plan the next quarter.

Then repeat monthly. In service businesses, speed, honesty and a steady flow of reviews compound over time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a website if I have a Google profile?
A profile alone can bring calls, but a website with service pages, prices and reviews builds trust and helps you rank for far more searches. Both together work best.

### Can I hide my address on Google?
If you serve customers at their location and don't welcome them at your address, you can set up a service-area business and hide it. If customers do visit you, show your address.

### How many service areas can I list?
Google limits the number and expects you to list only the places you genuinely serve. Check the current limit in the Business Profile Help.

### Should I show prices?
Show a call-out fee and typical ranges, and explain that you confirm the price before starting. It builds trust and filters out people who can't afford the work.

### How do I compete with bigger companies?
Win on speed, honesty, reviews and local knowledge. Be reachable, show real photos and make it easy to get a price.

### Are lead-generation directories worth it?
Sometimes, but check the cost per paid job, not per lead, and whether competitors receive the same leads. Always track your results.

### How long does it take to get results?
Profile improvements can show in a few weeks. Ranking in busy areas, such as central Lagos, usually takes a few months of steady work.
