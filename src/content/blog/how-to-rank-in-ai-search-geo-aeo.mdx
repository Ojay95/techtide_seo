Something changed in the way people look for businesses. Not long ago, you typed a question into Google and clicked one of ten blue links. Now you can ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Claude the same question and get a written answer, often with a short list of recommended companies.

That raises a fair question for anyone who runs a business: how do I get mentioned in those answers? A whole new vocabulary has grown up around it. You'll hear GEO (generative engine optimisation), AEO (answer engine optimisation), "AI visibility" and plenty more.

Here's the calm version. This guide explains what's real, what's hype and what to do about it, in plain language. No tricks, no magic, and no promises that anyone can honestly make.

## What "AI search" actually means

AI search is a catch-all for tools that answer questions in sentences instead of lists of links. There are a few kinds:

- **AI features inside search engines**, such as Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, which write a short summary above or beside the normal results.
- **AI assistants with web search**, like ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude, which look up pages and write an answer with sources.
- **AI assistants answering from memory**, where the model replies from what it learned in training, without checking the web.

The third kind is the hardest to influence quickly. The first two work much more like search, and that's good news, because you already know how to influence search.

## GEO and AEO, without the jargon

**AEO**, answer engine optimisation, means making your content easy for a system to pull a clear answer from. **GEO**, generative engine optimisation, means making it likely that an AI-written answer will mention or cite you.

They overlap heavily, and both overlap with SEO. In practice:

- If your pages are easy to find, crawl and understand, you're doing the technical part.
- If your content answers real questions clearly, you're doing the content part.
- If other trusted places mention you consistently, you're doing the reputation part.

Google says this directly in its guidance on [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features): there are no special requirements for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and the same SEO fundamentals apply. Pages need to be indexed and eligible to appear in search with a snippet. That is the honest starting point.

## How AI tools choose what to mention

Nobody outside these companies knows the full recipe, and it changes. But from how these tools describe themselves and how they behave, a few patterns hold up.

**They often start with a search.** When an assistant has web access, it searches, reads results and writes an answer using the pages it finds. Some use their own index, and some use partners. If you aren't visible in search, you're less likely to be found.

**They break big questions into smaller ones.** Google has described AI Mode as running several related searches at once, which it calls "query fan-out". A question like "best SEO agency for a hotel in Lagos" may turn into searches about hotel SEO, agencies in Lagos, reviews and pricing. Pages that answer those smaller questions have more chances to be picked.

**They favour clear, specific, trustworthy sources.** A page that states a fact plainly, shows who wrote it and backs it up is easier to quote than a vague one.

**They rely on what others say about you.** An AI can only mention your business with confidence if several places agree on what it is. Reviews, directories, news and your own consistent website all help.

## A practical plan, step by step

### Step 1: Make sure AI crawlers and search engines can reach you

Start with the boring but essential step. If your pages are blocked, nothing else matters.

- Check your `robots.txt` file. It shouldn't block the search bots you want. OpenAI explains its crawlers, including one for search, in its [bots documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots), and Perplexity does the same in its [bot guide](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots).
- Make sure your important content is in the page's HTML, not only added later by JavaScript. Many crawlers don't run scripts.
- Keep pages fast and error-free.
- Check your firewall or bot protection isn't blocking good crawlers by mistake.

Whether you allow your content to be used for training AI models is a separate choice. Each company's documentation lists which crawler does what, so you can decide.

### Step 2: Put the answer first

AI tools love content that gets to the point. For each important page, ask: "What question does this answer?" Then answer it in the first two or three sentences, and add detail below.

For example, a page about SEO costs might open with: "SEO plans for small businesses usually start from a few hundred dollars a month. The price depends on your market, competition and goals." Then explain the factors. Readers get what they need quickly, and so do machines.

### Step 3: Use headings that match real questions

Write headings the way people ask. "How much does SEO cost?" works better than "Pricing considerations". Under each, give a short, direct answer first. Lists and tables help when the content really is a list or a comparison.

### Step 4: Add facts, not fluff

Vague pages say "we offer great service". Useful pages say "we respond within two hours", "plans start from $500 per month" and "we serve clients in Nigeria and abroad". Specific, checkable facts are easier to cite. Keep them accurate and up to date, and show the date a page was last updated.

### Step 5: Be clear about who you are

AI tools build an understanding of "entities", which means businesses, people and places. Help them get yours right:

- Write a clear About page: who you are, where you're based, what you do and for whom.
- Use the same business name, address and description everywhere.
- Add structured data to your site. Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-guide/) explains how, and [Schema.org](https://schema.org/) lists the types.
- Link your website to your official profiles, such as your Google Business Profile and LinkedIn page.

### Step 6: Earn mentions on other trusted sites

This is the part many businesses skip, and it's often the most powerful. AI answers frequently draw on review sites, directories, "best of" lists, news articles and forums. If you're nowhere on those, you're easy to overlook.

Find out which sources show up when you ask AI tools about your category (we cover this in our guide to [getting mentioned by ChatGPT and other AI assistants](/blog/how-to-get-mentioned-by-chatgpt-and-ai-assistants/)). Then work on being genuinely present there: collect honest reviews, claim your listings and offer useful comments to journalists and bloggers. Our guide on [safe link building](/blog/how-to-get-backlinks/) shows how.

### Step 7: Publish things worth quoting

Original information gets cited because nobody else has it. That could be a survey of your customers, a price guide, a checklist or a case study with real numbers. If you're the source of a useful fact, people and machines link back to you.

### Step 8: Keep content fresh

Facts go out of date. Review your key pages every few months, correct what changed and update the "last updated" date honestly. Out-of-date prices and old addresses are a quick way to lose trust.

## What about llms.txt?

You may have heard about `llms.txt`, a file that gives AI tools a tidy summary of your site. It's a proposal described at [llmstxt.org](https://llmstxt.org). At the time of writing, major AI companies haven't confirmed that they use it for ranking or citing. Adding one is quick and harmless, but don't expect it to change your results. Spend your time on the steps above first.

## Myths to ignore

**"Stuff your pages with AI keywords."** It doesn't work and makes your content worse.

**"You need a special AI optimisation plugin."** Google says there are no extra requirements for its AI features. Be wary of anyone selling secret formulas.

**"SEO is dead."** The skills still matter, because most AI tools lean on search results and web content. The work has widened, not disappeared.

**"Mass-produce AI pages to cover every topic."** Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) treat large amounts of unhelpful, low-value content as spam, however it's made.

**"Someone can guarantee you a mention."** Nobody controls these answers, and they vary from one person and one day to the next.

## How to check your progress

You can't open a dashboard that says "your AI rank". Instead, test regularly:

1. Write five questions your customers might ask, such as "Which agency offers SEO services in Nigeria?"
2. Ask them in two or three AI tools every month.
3. Record whether you're mentioned, how you're described and which sources are cited.
4. Note which competitors appear, and which sites the tools rely on.

Expect answers to vary. Look for trends over months, not single results. Also keep watching your normal numbers in [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console), because AI features still send traffic through ordinary results.

## A simple one-month checklist

- Check robots.txt and make sure key pages load without JavaScript.
- Rewrite your top five pages so each answers its main question in the first lines.
- Update your About page with clear, factual details.
- Add Organization and relevant schema markup.
- Claim or fix your main profiles and listings.
- Ask three recent customers for honest reviews.
- Run your first set of AI test questions and save the results.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GEO different from SEO?
There's a lot of overlap. SEO is the foundation, and GEO adds attention to clear answers, entity clarity and mentions on other sites.

### Do I need to rank number one on Google to be cited by AI?
No, but being visible in search helps a lot. AI tools often cite pages that rank well, and they sometimes cite pages from deeper in the results too.

### Does schema markup help with AI search?
It helps search engines understand your content, and it's good practice. Google hasn't said it's required for AI features, so treat it as a useful foundation, not a guarantee.

### How long does it take to appear in AI answers?
It depends on your starting point. If your site is new and little is written about you, expect months, not days.

### Should I block AI crawlers?
That's your decision. Blocking search-related crawlers can reduce your chances of being cited. Blocking training crawlers is a different choice. Read each company's documentation before you decide.
